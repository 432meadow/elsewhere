[ listen ]

an endless synthesized field recording of anywhere on earth. one html file, no samples, no server: every voice is made as it plays. spin the globe and tap, and you are standing there, now.

the place

the land under your feet decides who sings. the lower 48 are drawn by hand into fourteen biomes; the rest of the world comes from the real köppen–geiger climate grid, so the deserts, monsoons and boreal belts are where they are. seven realms carry their own voices — the nightingale in europe, gibbons in asia, the screaming piha on the amazon, the kookaburra in australia, the indri on madagascar — and the mountains find theirs above 1400 m. antarctica is its own realm: adélies and emperors at the shore, seals under the ice, the ice itself calving, and inland only the wind and a snow petrel far from the sea.

every site keeps what it has: a stream, a lake, a marsh, a farm, a town near enough for a train. everyone who taps the same place on the same day gets the same weather, the same fog morning, the same afternoon storm. [ share this place ] copies a link that replays it — the place and day, a frozen hour, a world, the walk, the dub — all of it.

winter is a season, not a silence: the ptarmigan's go-back chuckle, redpoll flocks over, snow sliding off a branch, the owls and wolves at their midwinter courting, and the birds that stay singing on through a snowfall that hushes everyone else.

the clock

[ now ] follows the actual solar time at the site. the dawn chorus assembles in its true order, robins first; the crickets chirp at the rate the temperature says they should; the thrushes wait for dusk and the nightjars stop at sunrise. the season comes from the date and flips south of the equator. [ a day in an hour ] runs the sun at twenty-four times, so a whole day passes in one sitting; [ a year in an hour ] runs the seasons at 365 days an hour, the spring arrivals one species at a time, each day's weather planned afresh.

some things are on longer clocks. the tide rises and falls with the moon's hour, twice a day on most coasts and once a day in the gulf of mexico, springs at new and full moon, neaps between; the surf comes up the beach and the waders feed on the ebb. eclipses fall where the tables put them, 2017 to 2035: stand in the path on the day and the birds go quiet, the crickets start, and the light comes back to a false dawn. the periodical cicadas keep the longest clock of all — fifteen broods on cycles of seventeen or thirteen years, each on its own ground: indiana in late may 2038 roars with brood x, and in 2039 the forest is itself again. and the clock runs backward too: a ?day= in an earlier year, and whoever was still singing here then joins the living cast — passenger pigeons over a living ohio forest, wolves in an irish wood — while the sounds not yet invented stay out.

moving

[ here ] asks the phone where you are and stands you there; the answer is never kept or sent. [ elsewhere ] goes somewhere at random. [ ∞ drift ] sets you walking: a slow seeded wander whose bearing meanders and reflects off the coasts, so every ten or twenty minutes you cross into another biome and the whole cast turns over. [ ride the dawn ] stands you where the sun is just rising and carries you west with it, fifteen degrees an hour, so the chorus never ends; over the sea the ride goes on over the water.

otherwise

three other worlds, one at a time. [ moth variation ] turns the page to deep night and gives the world to the moths, heard as through a bat detector: wingbeats close to the ear, a tiger moth's clicks, hawk-moths at the flowers, now and then a bat's feeding buzz and what answers it. [ what can no longer be heard ] goes cold and pale, and only the lost sing, on their old ranges at their old numbers, each named with the year it was last heard; the less is known of a voice, the fainter and farther it sings. hawaiʻi and aotearoa are left unsung on purpose: their lost birds are their own people's to voice. [ under the surface ] puts you below the water wherever there is water: snapping shrimp in warm shallows, the fish choruses at dusk, dolphins, the humpback's song on its winter grounds, orcas where orcas are, seals under the polar ice, water boatmen singing in a lake, the gravel rolling in a stream. [ vlf ] is the earth through a very-low-frequency receiver: every lightning stroke on the planet a crackle, the night's tweeks, whistlers gliding down from the far hemisphere, and at high latitude on a disturbed morning the magnetosphere's own dawn chorus; wherever there are people, the mains hum from the lines. the day's geomagnetic weather is one number, kp, seeded for the whole planet.

the treatment

[ dub ] runs the land through a tape echo: a rhythmic throw gate catches whatever the place is singing into a wobbling, saturated feedback delay, over muted chord stabs and a soft sub pulse rooted in the site's own key. the field recording underneath stays untouched, and a depth fader sets how deep it sits.

listening

density is how many voices; wind and space shape the air; the six faders under voices are stems — birds, insects, frogs, fauna, air & water, and people — on their own levels; the last one is every human trace, the train and the bells and the far jet, and it goes all the way down. on a phone, [ turn your head ] keeps the field fixed to the world: turn, and the thrush stays where it was. [ sleep ] fades the world out over its last five minutes, after thirty, sixty or ninety. [ duet ] turns the microphone on, and a whistle in the room is answered from across the field a beat later; nothing is recorded, nothing leaves the page.

a song is seldom alone: a neighbour of the same kind answers from the other side a beat after the phrase ends. on spring and autumn nights a thin traffic of flight calls passes overhead. stand within reach of a geyser basin and the ground speaks on its real period. the page wears the hour: its two accents follow the site's sun.

the making

every voice is synthesized in the hyperrealism manner — pitch-contour whistles, resonant pulse swarms, filtered noise, distance as lowpass, level and reverb — from research done species by species, with the frequencies and timings kept. the maps are public-domain data: us census outlines, natural earth, the köppen–geiger grid of kottek et al. 2006, nasa's eclipse tables. it is a progressive web app: add it to your home screen and it works entirely offline.

there are also one or two things it will not tell you about.
