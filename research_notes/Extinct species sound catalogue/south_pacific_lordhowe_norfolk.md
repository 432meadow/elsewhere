# Lost-species acoustic dossier: Lord Howe, Norfolk, Christmas Island (IO), Polynesia and Melanesia

**How this was researched.** The WebSearch budget ran out after 4 queries. The rest came from direct fetches: Wikipedia, the Australian Museum, BioOne, the Wilderness Society and artensterben.de. The most useful source was the full OCR text of **A. F. Basset Hull, "The Birds of Lord Howe and Norfolk Islands," Proc. Linn. Soc. NSW 34 (1909, issued 1910), pp. 636–693**, https://archive.org/details/biostor-54912 (text: https://archive.org/download/biostor-54912/biostor-54912_djvu.txt). Hull arrived on Lord Howe on 3 Oct 1907 and worked on Norfolk in Oct 1908–Feb 1909, so his notes describe the austral spring and summer. I also used **Gould's own text for *Nestor productus*** (KU digital library OCR, links below).

**Sources I could not get:**
- NFSA pages return 403 or a Cloudflare challenge, which I did not bypass.
- The environment.gov.au SPRAT profile returns 403.
- BirdLife datazone pages render with JavaScript, so the fields came back empty.
- Hindwood 1940 (Emu) and Hindwood 1938 were not reachable.

**Labels used below.**
- **RECORDED** = an actual sound recording exists.
- **DESCRIBED** = a written verbal description.
- **IMITATION** = an onomatopoeic name.
- **INFERRED** = my own reasoning with no source. Treat these as unsourced.

Coordinates are approximate island or site centroids from general geography. They are not taken from the sources.

## LORD HOWE ISLAND (~31.55°S, 159.08°E)
Black rats came ashore from the SS *Makambo*, grounded June 1918 (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tasman_starling ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lord_Howe_fantail). "Within two years 40% of Lord Howe's endemic bird species were extinct" (Tasman starling article).

### Lord Howe gerygone / warbler — *Gerygone insularis*
- **Local names:** "Rain-bird" and "Pop-goes-the-Weasel" (L.H.I.). These are IMITATION or folk names (Hull 1909).
- **Range and abundance:** "fairly numerous, flying briskly about the tree-tops; … especially active in the pursuit of small insects after a shower of rain" (Hull). Wikipedia: "very common … mostly in canopies of the native forests and secondary regrowths."
- **Nest:** domed, with a side entrance and a projecting hood. Clutch of 3 (Hull; Wikipedia).
- **Last record:** 1928 (Sharland 1929, cited at https://recentlyextinctspecies.com/passeriformes-passerine-birds/gerygone-insularis). The cause was rats.
- **Provenance:** DESCRIBED. Hull 1909 (verbatim): "The sweetly mournful note of this bird somewhat resembles that of *Gerygone albigularis*, in that it is chromatic, but it is more staccato and varied in its tones. By some of the Islanders it is called 'Pop-goes-the-Weasel,' it being considered that its song resembles that ancient air."
- **Synthesis notes:**
  - A descending, chromatic, staccato phrase built on the rhythm of the "Pop Goes the Weasel" tune.
  - More active after rain; canopy height.
  - No frequency data exists.
  - Model (sourced by Hull's own comparison): white-throated gerygone (*G. albigularis*, now *G. olivacea*). Norfolk gerygone (*G. modesta*) as a congener model is my own INFERRED suggestion.
- **Status:** IUCN EX; EPBC Extinct (not verified here).
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lord_Howe_gerygone ; Hull, above.

### Lord Howe thrush / vinous-tinted ouzel — *Turdus (poliocephalus) vinitinctus*
Wikipedia now splits the island-thrush complex into 17 species: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Island_thrush
- **Local names:** "Doctor Bird" and "ouzel."
- **Provenance:** DESCRIBED, and the local name is an IMITATION. Hull 1909: "the 'Doctor Bird' is seen everywhere, scratching amongst the dead leaves … or perched on the low shrubs, emitting its sharp whistling chirp. Its local name is said to be derived from a sharp double knocking sound uttered when the bird is alarmed." A loud noise "will send it scolding away."
- **Abundance and breeding:** "very common and exceedingly tame." It nested low, at palm bases. Clutch of 2; eggs dated 18 Oct 1907 and 18 Nov 1908 (Hull). "Quite common in 1906", declining from 1913, gone by about 1924 (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lord_Howe_thrush).
- **Synthesis notes:**
  - Ground-level leaf-litter scratching.
  - A sharp whistled chirp.
  - An alarm call as a double "knock"; scolding when flushed.
- **Model:** extant island thrushes (*Turdus* spp. of Vanuatu, Fiji and Samoa). INFERRED.

### Lord Howe starling — *Aplonis fusca hulliana*
- **Local names:** "Cidgimarik" (Hull's spelling) and "cudgimeruk" (Wikipedia); also "red-eyes." Hull: "The Lord Howe Island local name is onomatopoetic, based on the birds' usual cry." So the name is an IMITATION: roughly 3 syllables, *cudg-i-ma-rik*.
- **Voice:** DESCRIBED. Hull treated the Lord Howe and Norfolk populations together, so this applies to both taxa: "bold and noisy marauder … loud challenging notes"; the male "would utter a few calls from an adjacent pine-tree … whistling a sharp staccato note at short intervals … uttering satisfied notes between mouthfuls … they scolded for a while."
- **Breeding:** "September to November, and February to March." Nests in hollow dead limbs and tree-fern trunks. Clutch of 4, occasionally 5; eggs 15 Sept 1907 on Lord Howe (Hull).
- **Last record:** 1919 (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tasman_starling).
- **Model:** Rarotonga, Samoan or Polynesian starlings (*Aplonis* spp.). INFERRED.

### Robust white-eye — *Zosterops strenuus*
- **Local names:** "Big Silver-eye" (Hull); "big grinnell" (Wikipedia).
- **Voice:** DESCRIBED. Hull: "found in great numbers … where its powerful song makes music all day long in the palm-glades and on the wooded hillsides."
- **Nest:** loose cups under Kentia palm crowns. Hull found many old nests in Oct 1907.
- **Last record:** extinct by 1923; rats (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robust_white-eye).
- **Synthesis notes:** a diurnal chorus, all day, in palm glades.
- **Model:** the extant Lord Howe silvereye, *Z. tephropleurus* (a sympatric congener). INFERRED.

### Lord Howe fantail — *Rhipidura fuliginosa cervina*
- **Behaviour:** very tame; "will often enter the kitchen and capture flies from the walls." Nesting early to mid Oct 1907, with fledglings on 11 Oct (Hull).
- **Last record:** 1924, "practically wiped out" (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lord_Howe_fantail).
- **Voice:** no description found. Classed INFERRED; model on New Zealand or grey fantail.

### Lord Howe boobook — *Ninox novaeseelandiae albaria*
- **Local name:** "Morepork (L.H.I.)" (Hull). This is IMITATION-grade evidence that its call was a two-note "more-pork."
- **Voice:** DESCRIBED. Hull: "I frequently heard the cry of this bird at night, but did not see it. The cry resembles that of Ninox boobook."
- **Habitat:** "native forests as well as … around the settlements."
- **Last record:** "calls were apparently heard on the island until the 1950s", but it is unclear whether these were endemic birds or introduced ones. Boobooks, barn owls and masked owls were brought in during the 1920s for rat control (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lord_Howe_boobook).
- **Model:** Australian boobook or NZ morepork.

### Lord Howe pigeon — *Columba vitiensis godmanae*
- **Local name:** "white-throated pigeon."
- **Voice:** DESCRIBED only indirectly. Hunters "would break birds' legs to use their cries as lures."
- **Evidence and last record:** known only from paintings (Raper, 1790). Last record 1853; hunting (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lord_Howe_pigeon).
- **Model:** metallic pigeon *C. vitiensis* (extant in Fiji and Samoa). INFERRED.

### Lord Howe parakeet — *Cyanoramphus subflavescens*
- **Abundance:** formerly abundant. Settlers persecuted it for raiding crops. Hull: "Mrs. Nichols informed me that, some years ago, this Parrakeet was very plentiful, but, being destructive in the fruit-gardens, it was gradually exterminated."
- **Last record:** 1869 (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lord_Howe_parakeet).
- **Voice:** no description found (INFERRED). Model: red-crowned parakeet, or Norfolk green parrot *C. cookii*.

### White swamphen / white gallinule — *Porphyrio albus*
- **Voice:** "its nest, eggs and call were never described." Classed INFERRED only.
- **Record:** 1788–90 only; clubbed by sailors (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lord_Howe_swamphen).
- **Model:** pūkeko / Australasian swamphen. INFERRED.

### Lord Howe long-eared bat — *Nyctophilus howensis*
- **Evidence:** a single incomplete skull, Gooseberry Cave, 1972 (G. F. van Tets), from a "modern deposit."
- **Anecdotes:** colonists reported a second, larger bat after Etheridge's 1889 survey. A 1999 search failed.
- **Status:** EPBC presumed extinct (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lord_Howe_long-eared_bat).
- **Voice:** none. Model: *Nyctophilus* echolocation (low-intensity, broadband FM). INFERRED; no source fetched.

**Living soundscape context (Hull 1909):**
- The pied currawong's "musical whistling call rings out with an almost human intonation."
- The emerald dove gives "an oft-repeated and somewhat monotonous 'Coo-coo' … at frequent intervals for an hour or more."

## NORFOLK ISLAND (~29.03°S, 167.95°E; Phillip Island ~29.12°S, 167.95°E)

### Norfolk kākā — *Nestor productus* ("Phillip Island Parrot")
- **Voice:** DESCRIBED, from a captive bird.
  - Gould, *Birds of Australia* vol. 5 (1840–48): "Its voice is a hoarse, quacking, inharmonious noise, sometimes resembling the barking of a dog." (https://digital.lib.ku.edu/islandora/object/ku-gould%3A13874/datastream/OCR/download)
  - Another Gould text: "Its voice was hoarse and inharmonious, frequently resembling the barking of a dog" (https://digital.lib.ku.edu/islandora/object/ku-gould%3A17825/datastream/OCR/download).
  - **Caution:** the widely quoted line "harsh and disagreeable in the extreme" is Yate quoting the **New Zealand kākā**, not *N. productus*. It should not be attributed to the Norfolk bird.
- **Behaviour** (Mrs Anderson via Gould; captive bird in Sydney):
  - Moved "in a succession of leaps."
  - "found among the rocks and upon the loftiest trees of the island."
  - "so tame as to be readily taken alive with a noose."
  - "feeds upon the blossoms of the white-wood tree, or white Hibiscus, sucking the honey."
  - "lays four eggs in the hollow part of a tree."
- **Range:** Gould was told it was confined to Phillip Island. Wikipedia and the Australian Museum give Norfolk, Phillip and Nepean Islands.
- **Last record:** not in Best's 1838/39 diaries. The last captive bird died in London in 1851 (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norfolk_k%C4%81k%C4%81 ; https://australian.museum/learn/animals/birds/norfolk-island-kaka/).
- **Model:** NZ kākā (*Nestor meridionalis*); same genus.

### Norfolk boobook — *Ninox novaeseelandiae undulata*
- **Hull 1909:** "did not see or hear this bird at Norfolk Island, but was informed that it still exists in the gullies on Mount Pitt."
- **Last pure bird:** a single female, named "Miamiti" after a Norfolk matriarch (an honorific name, not a call imitation). She disappeared in 1996.
- **Hybridisation:** in 1987 two NZ morepork males were introduced and one bred with her. By 2018 there were 45–50 hybrids, with no recruitment since 2012. Hybrids "repeat their calls more rapidly than the original birds."
- **Nesting:** tree hollows, clutch of 1–3.
- **Status:** EPBC Endangered (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norfolk_boobook).
- **Recordings:** hybrid calls are presumably recordable today; I found no archive of pre-1987 pure-bird calls.
- **Model:** morepork. Its calls include "more-pork," "a high pitched yelping call, a whistling call similar to kiwi, … 'quork-quork'"; up to 11 call types; nocturnal (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morepork).

### Norfolk starling — *Aplonis fusca fusca*
- **Local name:** "Black Bird (N.I.)" (Hull).
- **Voice:** DESCRIBED. See the Lord Howe starling entry; Hull's banana-raiding scene was observed on Norfolk. Nests in dead tree-fern trunks; eggs 31 Oct 1908 and 20 Feb 1909.
- **Last record:** 1923 (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tasman_starling).

### Norfolk thrush / "Guava Bird" — *Turdus poliocephalus*
- **Local names:** "Guava Bird" and "grey-headed blackbird." The name comes from eating ripe guavas.
- **Breeding:** "two breeding seasons, (1) August to December, (2) March to May" (Metcalfe via Hull). Nests 6–20 ft up, often in wild lemon thickets. Eggs Oct–Dec 1908.
- **Voice:** DESCRIBED only by analogy. Hull says its "habits and general characteristics" closely resemble the Lord Howe thrush.
- **Last record:** confirmed 1975. It also hybridised with blackbirds (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norfolk_Island_thrush).

### White-chested white-eye — *Zosterops albogularis*
I added this species; it was not on your list.
- **Local name:** "Grinnell (N.I.)."
- **Voice:** DESCRIBED. Hull 1909: "It has a loud and not very musical note when in flocks, and a number of the birds arguing together make a noise similar to a mob of quarrelling house-sparrows. Solitary birds, however, occasionally indulge in a long-sustained liquid song, very pleasing to the ear." Eggs 20 Nov 1909.
- **Last record:** official surveys since 1980 found nothing; there are unconfirmed 2005 claims and a nil 2009 survey (Dutson).
- **Range:** about 5 km² of forest around Mt Pitt.
- **Status:** IUCN EX; Australia treats it as extinct (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White-chested_white-eye).

### Norfolk pigeon — *Hemiphaga novaeseelandiae spadicea*
- **Local name:** "wood quest."
- **Record:** Best shot 72 birds in 1838, including 25 on 18 Sept 1838. Last sighting 1901.
- **Relationships:** sister to the NZ kererū by toepad DNA (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norfolk_Island_pigeon).
- **Voice:** none found. Model: kererū (soft "coo", loud wingbeats). INFERRED.

### Norfolk ground dove — *Pampusana norfolkensis*
- **Evidence:** known only from a John Hunter illustration (1788–90); no specimens. Gone within decades of settlement (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norfolk_ground_dove).
- **Voice:** none. INFERRED only.

## CHRISTMAS ISLAND, INDIAN OCEAN (~10.49°S, 105.62°E)

### Christmas Island pipistrelle — *Pipistrellus murrayi*
- **Provenance:** RECORDED. This is the only true recording in this set.
  - The last echolocation call was captured on 26 Aug 2009 by **Dr Rupert Baker (Healesville Sanctuary)**, "through a handheld echolocation detector onto his mobile phone."
  - Baker's quote: "I can still picture the clearing where I saw the pipistrelle, using night vision goggles. It was in a fern-covered opening in the forest." (https://wilderness.org.au/news-events/last-call-of-the-wild)
  - The recording is in NFSA *Sounds of Australia*: "Last call of the Christmas Island Pipistrelle". The NFSA's sound engineers adjusted it to make it audible (https://www.nfsa.gov.au/collection/curated/asset/102431-last-call-christmas-island-pipistrelle, known from the search snippet only because the page is blocked).
  - Lindy Lumsden: not confirmed in any source I could fetch.
- **Decline:** 58% (1994–2002); 90% (1994–2006); 99.4% (Jan 2006–Jan 2009). About 20 individuals in Jan 2009. One bat detected in Aug 2009 that could not be caught (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christmas_Island_pipistrelle ; https://www.artensterben.de/en/christmas-island-pipistrelle/).
- **Size:** 2.6–4.6 g.
- **Roosts:** bark, hollows, dead palm fronds; up to 47 per roost. Maternity roosts under loose bark of *Tristiropsis acutangula*.
- **Echolocation:** it was the island's only echolocating bat. I could not source a call frequency in kHz, so do not invent one.
- **Status:** EPBC CR in 2006. IUCN EX; Wikipedia says 2009, but I recall the formal assessment as 2017, which is unverified.

### Maclear's rat and bulldog rat — *Rattus macleari*, *R. nativitatis*
- **Maclear's rat:** abundant and nocturnal, making "querulous squeaks" (DESCRIBED). Wikipedia says it raided expedition tents; Wikipedia's "Challenger/1886" wording looks doubtful, so verify it (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maclear%27s_rat).
- **Bulldog rat:** burrowed in primary forest on the higher hills (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bulldog_rat).
- **Last records:** both 1903, after die-offs in 1902–03 probably from disease carried by black rats.

### Christmas Island shrew — *Crocidura trichura*
- Last specimens 1985; IUCN EX 2025 (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christmas_Island_shrew).

## POLYNESIA

### Tahiti sandpiper — *Prosobonia leucoptera*; Tahitian name **toromē**
- **Range and habitat:** Tahiti (~17.65°S, 149.43°W); "believed to have occurred near small streams."
- **Records:** Forster illustrated it in Aug 1773 or Apr–May 1774. One specimen survives (Naturalis RMNH.AVES.87556). Extinct before 1819.
- **Moorea sandpiper:** *P. ellisi*, from Moorea, 30 Sept–11 Oct 1777 (Ellis and Webber drawings; Anderson). Jansen, Kamminga & Argeloo 2021 treat it as a junior synonym of *P. leucoptera*.
- **Sources:** https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tahiti_sandpiper ; https://bioone.org/journals/bulletin-of-the-british-ornithologists-club/volume-141/issue-2/bboc.v141i2.2021.a4/Taxonomic-implications-of-the-original-illustrations-of-Prosobonia-from-Tahiti/10.25226/bboc.v141i2.2021.a4.full
- **Voice:** no surviving description found. INFERRED from the Tuamotu sandpiper.

### Kiritimati sandpiper — *Prosobonia cancellata*
- **Evidence:** Ellis illustration and Anderson's description, Cook's third voyage, 24 Dec 1777–2 Jan 1778. Extinct in the first half of the 19th century (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kiritimati_sandpiper).
- **Location:** ~1.87°N, 157.36°W.
- **Voice:** none. INFERRED.

### Tuamotu sandpiper — *Prosobonia parvirostris*; Tuamotuan **kivi-kivi** (acoustic model)
- **Voice:** "a soft, high whistle or piping, transcribed as *meh*."
- **Local name:** "kivi-kivi" is probably an IMITATION.
- **Breeding:** mostly April–June.
- **Population and status:** 700–1,100 mature birds on rat-free atolls; Endangered. It was formerly on Kiritimati too (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tuamotu_sandpiper).
- **Recordings:** extant, so living recordings likely exist, but I did not verify archive IDs.
- **Name correction:** "titi" is not its name; the sources give kivi-kivi.

### Black-fronted (Tahiti) parakeet — *Cyanoramphus zealandicus*; Tahitian **'ā'ā** (Latham 1790) or "aa-maha" (White 1887)
- **Name:** 'ā'ā means "parrot"; plausibly an IMITATION of a harsh call. INFERRED.
- **Records:** Cook specimens from 1769. Last specimen 1844 (des Marolles, MNHN). Kept as pets before cats and rats arrived (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black-fronted_parakeet).
- **Voice:** none. Model: red-crowned parakeet. INFERRED.

### Raiatea parakeet — *Cyanoramphus ulietanus*
- Raiatea (~16.83°S, 151.43°W) forest. Two specimens, 1773 or Nov 1777. Voice unknown (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Society_parakeet).

### Raiatea "starling" / bay thrush — *Turdus ulietensis* (affinity unknown)
- **Voice:** DESCRIBED. Forster noted "it had a soft, fluting voice and lived among the thickets in the valleys."
- **Records:** Forster painting of 1 June 1774. Garrett did not find it in 1850 (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raiatea_starling).

### Tahiti rail — *Hypotaenidia pacifica*; Tahitian **Oomnaa / Eboonàa / Tevea**
- **Voice:** DESCRIBED. Per Bruner, the call was "similar to that of other rails, though it differed in ending in a high-pitched whistle."
- **Habitat:** open areas, marshes, coconut plantations.
- **Records:** Forster plate no. 128. Gone from Tahiti after 1844; possibly survived on Mehetia (~17.88°S, 148.07°W) into the 1930s (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tahiti_rail).
- **Model:** buff-banded rail. INFERRED.

### Tahiti crake (Miller's rail) — *Zapornia/Porzana nigra*
- Forster painting from Cook's second voyage; extinct around 1800. Voice unknown (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miller%27s_rail).

### Moorea reed warbler — *Acrocephalus longirostris* (CR, not extinct)
- Last seen 1987; two unconfirmed 21st-century reports. Lived in 'ohe bamboo thickets (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moorea_reed_warbler).

### Garrett's reed warbler — *A. musae*
- Huahine and Raiatea; extinct, probably in the 19th century (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garrett%27s_reed_warbler).
- Voice unknown for both reed warblers. Model: the extant Tahiti reed warbler. INFERRED.

### Maupiti monarch — *Pomarea pomarea*
- One specimen, Blosseville 1823; extinct (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maupiti_monarch).

### Marquesas monarchs
- **Nuku Hiva monarch** (*P. nukuhivae*): last confirmed 1930s, possible 1975; extinct (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuku_Hiva_monarch).
- **Hiva Oa / Tahuata monarch** (*P. mendozae mendozae*): last sighted 1975 (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marquesan_monarch).
- **Ua Pou monarch** (*P. mira*): last seen 1975, unconfirmed 2010; CR, possibly extinct (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ua_Pou_monarch).
- **Fatu Hiva monarch:** CR, about 17 birds (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fatu_Hiva_monarch). It is the nearest living acoustic model, but I found no voice description.

### Mysterious starling — *Aplonis mavornata* (Mauke, Cook Islands, ~20.16°S, 157.34°W)
- The single specimen was shot by Andrew Bloxam "between 2:30 and 3:30 pm on August 9, 1825." Bloxam already saw rats on the island.
- Extinct by the 1973 visit. Voice unknown (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mysterious_starling).
- Model: Rarotonga starling. INFERRED.

### Samoan moorhen — *Pareudiastes pacificus*; Samoan **puna'e** ("one that jumps up")
- **Habitat:** primary montane forest on Savai'i (~13.63°S, 172.43°W). "Probably nocturnal."
- **Records:** last confirmed 1873. Wikipedia attributes it to the Challenger expedition; treat that attribution as doubtful. Unconfirmed reports in Aug 1984 and 2003.
- **Voice:** DESCRIBED, unconfirmed. The 2003 report described "a deep, gulping call *ooh-ooh-ooh*." CR.
- Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samoan_moorhen

### Manumea / tooth-billed pigeon — *Didunculus strigirostris* (CR; not reliably recorded)
- Samoa's national bird; appears on the 20 tālā note.
- **Population:** 70–380 estimated.
- **Records:** juvenile in 2013; "heard and seen" on Savai'i in 2020. No voice description found (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tooth-billed_pigeon).

### Sheath-tailed bat — *Emballonura semicaudata semicaudata*
- Ranges over Vanuatu, Fiji, Tonga, Samoa and American Samoa. The range map shows extinct areas, but no dates or kHz values (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pacific_sheath-tailed_bat).

### Areas with nothing documented
**Tonga and Pitcairn:** I found no historically documented (post-1500) bird extinctions with acoustic data. Absence not verified.

## MELANESIA

### New Caledonian lorikeet — *Charmosyna diadema*
- **Habitat:** humid montane forest; flies into lowland *Melaleuca* forest; keeps to treetops.
- **Specimens:** two skins from before 1860.
- **Last record:** one shot at Mont Ignambi near Oubatche in 1913 (~20.45°S, 164.58°E, approximate), not preserved.
- **Unconfirmed reports:** 1920s(?); 1953/54; 3 June 1976 west of Mt Panié.
- **Voice:** "unknown but … likely high-pitched screeches" (INFERRED by Wikipedia itself).
- **Status:** CR, possibly extinct (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Caledonian_lorikeet).

### New Caledonian rail — *Gallirallus lafresnayanus*
- 17 specimens, the last in 1890; unconfirmed 1984. Forested river valleys. Flightless, about 45 cm.
- "Call is not known." CR (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Caledonian_rail).
- Possible relatives: weka, Lord Howe woodhen. Weka calls are loud whistles; that detail is INFERRED.

### New Caledonian nightjar — *Eurostopodus exul*
- Known from a single holotype, 1939 (Whitney Expedition), coastal savanna. CR, possibly extinct (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Caledonian_nightjar).

### Makira moorhen / woodhen — *Pareudiastes silvestris*; local name **kia**
- Last confirmed 1953. The 2015–16 surveys failed and concluded it is likely extinct. CR (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Makira_moorhen).

### Choiseul pigeon — *Microgoura meeki*; local **kumku-peka** / **kukuru-ni-lua** ("pigeon-belong-ground")
- **Voice:** DESCRIBED by indigenous informants: a "beautiful rising and falling whistling call given from the roost site every evening." Others described a low "c-r-r-ooo," "cr-ooo," "cr-o-o-o," which are IMITATIONS.
- **Habitat:** lowland forest and coastal swamp without mangroves.
- **Records:** A. S. Meek collected 6 adults and an egg in northern Choiseul, Jan 1904. Last unconfirmed report early 1940s near Sasamungga. Cause: cats and dogs.
- Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Choiseul_pigeon
- This is the richest indigenous acoustic account in Melanesia, with a clear time of day (evening, at the roost).

### Bar-winged rail — *Hypotaenidia poecilopterus*
- Viti Levu; last collected about 1890; unconfirmed 1973. IUCN EX 1994. Voice unknown (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bar-winged_rail).

### Guadalcanal monkey-faced bat — *Pteralopex atrata*
- Extant (EN), last seen Sept 2015. Feared extirpated on New Georgia, known there from a single 1975 skull (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guadalcanal_monkey-faced_bat).

### Levuana moth (Fiji)
- An insect with no sound. Not researched.

## GAPS AND CAUTIONS
