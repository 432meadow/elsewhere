# Extinct and "not heard since" species of Europe, Asia, Africa, the Middle East and the Indian/Atlantic Ocean islands: what is known about their sounds

Scope: extinct, or last reliably recorded, since about 1500. The sources are web research done on 2026-09-30. Provenance tiers are used throughout:
- **RECORDED**: a real audio recording exists.
- **DESCRIBED**: a first-hand written account of sound exists.
- **IMITATION**: a human imitation or onomatopoeic name.
- **INFERRED**: the sound is modelled from anatomy or living relatives.

Research note: the web-search budget for the session ran out partway through. Later species were checked only by fetching Wikipedia or primary pages directly. Several candidates in the brief could not be checked at all; they are listed under Gaps.

## Q1. Which lost species have genuine audio recordings (the RECORDED tier)?

### Takeaway
Only a handful of species in this region have real recordings:
- **Baiji**: captive and wild whistles and clicks, Wuhan Institute of Hydrobiology, 1980s–2002.
- **Slender-billed curlew**: one poor-quality recording (attribution conflicts), plus a digital reconstruction.
- **Northern white rhino**: an 11-call repertoire recorded from the last breeding herd.
- **Jerdon's courser**: a "lost and found" species. First recorded by Simon Wotton, then re-recorded at night in August 2025.
- **Forest owlet**: rediscovered in 1997 and now extant; calls documented.

Everything else in this file is DESCRIBED, IMITATION or INFERRED.

### Cited Findings

**Baiji / Yangtze river dolphin / "Goddess of the Yangtze" (白鱀豚 báijì; 长江女神), *Lipotes vexillifer*. RECORDED.**
- Two main sound types: whistles for communication and clicks for echolocation. Primary refs: Wang, D., Lu, W. & Wang, Z. 1989, "A Preliminary Study of the Acoustic Behavior of the Baiji"; Xiao, Y. & Jing, R. 1989, "Underwater Acoustic Signals of the Baiji", pp. 129–136 in Perrin et al. (eds), *Biology and Conservation of the River Dolphins*, IUCN — [NOAA 5-year review PDF](https://repository.library.noaa.gov/view/noaa/16394/noaa_16394_DS1.pdf)
- Whistles are a narrow-band tone burst with an upswept frequency modulation. Dominant frequency is about 5.8 kHz and a call lasts about 1 s. Clicks are about 60–110 kHz, lower than bottlenose dolphin clicks. (Search-snippet summary of the DOSITS audio gallery; the page itself returned 403) — [DOSITS Baiji](https://dosits.org/galleries/audio-gallery/marine-mammals/toothed-whales/baiji-yangtze-river-dolphin)
- The Chinese Wikipedia gives whistles as stable at about 6 kHz, suited to diffraction in silty water, and clicks at 8–160 kHz. It cites Wang et al. 1999 for whistles — [zh.wikipedia 白鱀豚](https://zh.wikipedia.org/wiki/白鱀豚)
- The English Wikipedia gives peak click frequencies of 70–100 kHz — [Wikipedia: Baiji](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baiji)
- The three click figures (60–110, 70–100 and 8–160 kHz full band) broadly agree once you distinguish peak frequency from bandwidth.
- Akamatsu, Wang, Nakamura & Wang 1998 (*JASA*, doi:10.1121/1.423757) is an echolocation-range study of captive and free-ranging baiji and finless porpoise. It reports click intervals up to 100–200 ms in open water, 4–28 ms in captivity, and echolocation adapted for ranges up to about 140 m. The abstract summary does not make clear which species each figure applies to — [Europe PMC query](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/search?query=(AUTH:%22Akamatsu%20T%22%20OR%20AUTH:%22Wang%20D%22)%20AND%20(baiji%20OR%20Lipotes)&resultType=core&format=json&pageSize=15)
- Qiqi (淇淇), the only long-term captive baiji and the main source for acoustic research:
  - captured 11 January 1980 at Chenglingji near Dongting Lake, about 1 year old;
  - kept at the Institute of Hydrobiology (CAS) in Wuhan;
  - died 14 July 2002.
  — [zh.wikipedia](https://zh.wikipedia.org/wiki/白鱀豚)
- The Turvey et al. 2007 *Biology Letters* supplement includes an audio file labelled "Baiji whistle signal" — [Turvey et al. 2007, PMC2391192](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2391192/)
- Range: about 1,700 km of the middle and lower Yangtze, from Yichang to the mouth at Shanghai, plus Poyang and Dongting lakes — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baiji)
- Abundance:
  - about 6,000 in the 1950s, per Wikipedia — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baiji)
  - about 400 in 1979–81 (Zhou 1982) and about 300 in 1985–86;
  - at least 13 in 1997–99 (Zhang et al. 2003).
  — [NOAA](https://repository.library.noaa.gov/view/noaa/16394/noaa_16394_DS1.pdf)
- Groups were usually 2–4 animals, once 16. Breeding was in the first half of the year, with most births February–April — [NOAA](https://repository.library.noaa.gov/view/noaa/16394/noaa_16394_DS1.pdf)
- Last records:
  - last confirmed stranding 2001 (a pregnant female); last photographed live animal 2002 — [Turvey et al. 2007](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2391192/)
  - English Wikipedia says last sighted August 2004; unconfirmed video from Tongling, Anhui, August 2007 — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baiji)
- The 6 November – 13 December 2006 two-vessel visual and acoustic survey covered 1,669 km from Yichang to Shanghai twice. It found "no baiji sightings or acoustic recordings", and the species was declared functionally extinct — [Turvey et al. 2007](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2391192/)
- Lee et al. 2017 model: 72.45% probability the baiji is extinct — [NOAA](https://repository.library.noaa.gov/view/noaa/16394/noaa_16394_DS1.pdf)
- IUCN status: Critically Endangered (Possibly Extinct) — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baiji)

**Slender-billed curlew, *Numenius tenuirostris*. RECORDED (one poor recording), with a reconstruction.**
- Call: "*cour-lee*", like Eurasian curlew but higher-pitched, more melodic and shorter. Alarm call: a fast "*cu-ee*" — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slender-billed_curlew)
- **Attribution conflict:**
  - Wikipedia and a search summary say the only known recording was made by Claude Chappuis and went to Cornell's Macaulay Library after his death in 2021. In that account, J. P. Gautier and J. P. Richard cleaned the signal and separated it from overlapping Eurasian curlews, and Magnus Robb removed notes he judged to be Eurasian — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slender-billed_curlew)
  - The Sound Approach's review of its *Birds of Morocco* sound guide says the original was made by **Adam Gretton at Merja Zerga in 1990**, of "minimal quality", and is accompanied by "a reconstruction of it digitally produced by Magnus Robb" — [Sound Approach / Ardeola review](https://books.soundapproach.co.uk/?p=11199)
  - Treat these as possibly two different recordings until checked. A xeno-canto entry exists (XC398794) but blocked access — [xeno-canto XC398794](https://xeno-canto.org/398794)
- Breeding: only confirmed in raised bogs north of Omsk, Russia, 1909–1925. Isotopes suggest a main breeding belt in the Kazakh steppe and forest-steppe around 50°N — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slender-billed_curlew)
- Wintering: the Mediterranean and southern Arabia, with claims from Kuwait and Iraq — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slender-billed_curlew)
- Last records:
  - last irrefutable sighting February 1995, Merja Zerga (Moulay Bousselham), Morocco;
  - the Druridge Bay, England, record of May 1998 was removed from the British List in 2013;
  - an unverified Danube Delta report, August 2004.
  — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slender-billed_curlew); [BirdGuides](https://www.birdguides.com/news/slender-billed-curlew-officially-declared-extinct/)
- Declared globally extinct in November 2024 (RSPB/BirdLife/Naturalis/NHM analysis: 99.6% probability extinct, around the 1995 date). IUCN listing updated 10 October 2025 — [Mongabay](https://news.mongabay.com/short-article/2025/10/the-slender-billed-curlew-a-migratory-waterbird-is-officially-extinct-iucn/); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slender-billed_curlew)

**Northern white rhinoceros, *Ceratotherium simum cottoni*. RECORDED; functionally extinct.**
- Policht, Tomášová, Holečková & Frynta 2008, *Bioacoustics* 18(1): 69–96. They recorded 8 individuals in the last breeding herd at Zoo Dvůr Králové (Czech Republic) and found 11 calls in 4 categories: tonal harmonic, puffing, growling and repetitive.
  - Four calls reach infrasound; the "grouch" has the most infrasonic energy. The authors think these are probably not used for long-distance communication.
  - The repetitive "pant" is not known in other rhinos and may improve audibility in open habitat.
  — [Bioacoustics abstract](https://bioacoustics.info/article/vocal-repertoire-northern-white-rhinoceros-ceratotherium-simum-cottoni-recorded-last)
- A 2014 *PLoS ONE* paper, "Contact Calls of the Northern and Southern White Rhinoceros Allow for Individual and Species Identification", exists; the full text was captcha-blocked — [PMC4047034](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4047034/)
- Range: Uganda, South Sudan, CAR and DRC. Garamba NP held the last wild population, gone by 2008. The last male, Sudan (wild-caught in Sudan in 1975), was euthanised 19 March 2018 at Ol Pejeta, Kenya. Two females remain: Najin and Fatu. 38 embryos are stored as of 2025. IUCN: CR (Possibly Extinct in the Wild) — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_white_rhinoceros)

**Jerdon's courser, *Rhinoptilus bitorquatus*. RECORDED; "lost and found" twice.**
- Call: staccato "*twick-too … twick-too*" or "*yak-wak … yak-wak*". Simon Wotton made what was long the only known recording — [Madras Courier](https://madrascourier.com/?p=8517)
- Rediscovered in 1986 in Cuddapah District, Andhra Pradesh, by Bharat Bhushan (BNHS). Last documented 2004 at Sri Lankamalleswara WLS; P. Jeganathan's 2004–08 study found nothing afterwards — [Discover Wildlife](https://www.discoverwildlife.com/animal-facts/birds/jerdons-courser-rediscovered-india)
- **August 2025:** Harish Thangaraj's team (Adesh Shivkar, Ronith Urs, Shashank Dalvi, Pranav) recorded calls at **21:35 on 28 August 2025**. The call was in the last 30 s of a 1 h 55 min recording, and clearer calls were recorded the next night. The site is new, in Andhra Pradesh — [Discover Wildlife](https://www.discoverwildlife.com/animal-facts/birds/jerdons-courser-rediscovered-india)
- Mongabay India dates the birders' recording to 24 August 2025 in the Lankamalla Hills and says conclusive proof is still lacking. Status: Critically Endangered — [Mongabay India](https://india.mongabay.com/2025/09/the-search-for-the-jerdons-courser-needs-to-be-deepened-commentary/)

**Forest owlet, *Athene (Heteroglaux) blewitti*. Lost 1884–1997, now extant (EN).**
- Rediscovered in 1997 by Pamela Rasmussen. The "extinction" had been partly an artefact of Meinertzhagen's fraudulent specimen data.
- Voice:
  - song of "short and mellow" notes;
  - repetitive territorial calls, contact calls and begging calls;
  - courtship peaks January–February, when males respond strongly to playback.
- Diurnal. About 250 mature individuals in central India.
— [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forest_owlet)

### Inferences
- For the artwork, the baiji is the strongest "true voice" candidate.
  - Suggested model: narrow-band upsweeps near 6 kHz, about 1 s long, sparse, from groups of 2–4.
  - Add high-frequency click trains. These are above human hearing, so render them as low-level broadband ticks, or pitch them down and label them as such.
  - Recording provenance: Institute of Hydrobiology, Wuhan (Wang Ding group), especially Qiqi 1980–2002.
- For the slender-billed curlew, a synthesis should be built from Eurasian curlew "cour-lee" parameters: shifted up in pitch, shortened, and made more melodic. Label it INFERRED (not RECORDED), because the one recording is poor and partly reconstructed.
- Jerdon's courser calls at night. Nocturnal placement in the Eastern Ghats scrub-jungle of Andhra Pradesh fits the "secret trace" mode well.
- Approximate coordinates (geographic knowledge, not from the sources):

  | Place | Approx. lat/lon |
  |---|---|
  | Merja Zerga | 34.85 N, 6.28 W |
  | Tongling (Yangtze) | 30.93 N, 117.8 E |
  | Garamba | 4.0 N, 29.3 E |
  | Ol Pejeta | 0.0, 36.9 E |
  | Lankamalla Hills | about 14.6 N, 78.9 E |
  | Omsk bogs (Tara) | about 56.9 N, 74.4 E |

## Q2. Mascarene, Seychelles and Madagascar birds: what did contemporaries hear?

### Takeaway
There are surprisingly vivid first-hand sound descriptions for the Mascarenes:
- the Rodrigues solitaire's wing-rattle, audible at 200 paces, "like thunder in the distance";
- the grey parrots' distress screams, which drew the whole flock;
- the Rodrigues starling's "wonderful warbling";
- the hoopoe starling's "bright and cheerful whistle";
- the Mauritius blue pigeon's "baf baf" and cooing;
- the dodo's "cry like a gosling" (a doubtful account).

Julian Hume's work (Zootaxa 2007, *Extinct Birds* 2012/2017) collects the primary texts.

### Cited Findings

**Dodo, *Raphus cucullatus* (Dutch: *walghvoghel* 1598, "tasteless bird"; *dodaars* 1602, "knot-arse"; *dronte*). DESCRIBED (weak), IMITATION, INFERRED.**
- The only contemporary voice description is François Cauche (1651): "It has a cry like a gosling." Wikipedia calls his account problematic, possibly mixed with a cassowary — [Wikipedia: Dodo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dodo)
- The name "dodo" may be onomatopoeic for a two-note pigeon-like "doo-doo" — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dodo)
- A 2026 brain-endocast study found that the dodo and Rodrigues solitaire "shared better hearing in low frequencies, possibly because their larger size resulted in low-frequency vocalisation" (full citation not retrieved) — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dodo)
- Closest living relative: Nicobar pigeon, "a very vocal species, giving a low-pitched repetitive call" — [Wikipedia: Nicobar pigeon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicobar_pigeon)
- Artist-reconstructed dodo sounds exist: Hindley called his reconstruction "a fantasy" (bass cooing, "screaming", percussive sounds) — [earbirding.com](https://earbirding.com/blog/?p=316)
- Habitat: woods on the drier coasts of south and west Mauritius. Bred around August. Last widely accepted sighting 1662 — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dodo)

**Rodrigues solitaire, *Pezophaps solitaria*. DESCRIBED (wing sound, not voice).**
- Leguat (1708), from his stay in 1691–93: birds "whirl about for twenty or thirty times together on the same side, during the space of four or five minutes. The Motions of their Wings makes then a noise very like that of a Rattle; and one may hear it two hundred Paces off" — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rodrigues_solitaire); [Edward Worth Library](https://birds.edwardworthlibrary.ie/dead-dodos-and-climate-change/le-guat-on-the-solitary-bird-of-rodrigues-island/)
- Tafforet (1726): "a great noise with their wings when angry, and the noise is something like thunder in the distance." Birds had musket-ball-sized carpal knobs used in fights — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rodrigues_solitaire)
- Breeding (Leguat): a single egg on a heap of palm leaves; 7 weeks' incubation shared by both parents; pairs defended about 200 yards; juveniles formed crèches of 30–40. Leguat: "there are abundance of them" — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rodrigues_solitaire)
- Extinct probably 1730s–1760s:
  - Cossigny searched 18 months without success (1755);
  - Pingré saw none in 1761.
  — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rodrigues_solitaire)
- Shares low-frequency hearing with the dodo (2026 endocast study) — [Wikipedia: Dodo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dodo)

**Red rail, *Aphanapteryx bonasia* (Dutch "red hen"; Hoffmann's "toddaerschen"), Mauritius. DESCRIBED (behavioural).**
- Hunted by showing a red cloth; birds would "altogether flie upon it". The cries of a captured bird drew others — [Wikipedia: Red rail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_rail)
- Rare by Leguat's visit in 1693, the last mention. Extinct about 1700 — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_rail)

**Grey parrots: Thirioux's grey parrot *Psittacula bensoni* (Mauritius) and the Réunion grey parrot. DESCRIBED (distress call / flock recruitment).**
- Willem van West-Zanen, Mauritius 1602 (published 1648): "if one is caught and made to cry out, soon hundreds of the birds fly around ones' ears" — [Hume 2007, Zootaxa 1513](https://julianhume.co.uk/wp-content/uploads/2010/07/Hume-Mascarene-Parrots.pdf)
- Steven van der Hagen, 1606–07: "when we had caught a grey parrot, we made it call out, and at once hundreds more came flying around" — [Hume 2007](https://julianhume.co.uk/wp-content/uploads/2010/07/Hume-Mascarene-Parrots.pdf)
- Bontekoe, Réunion 1618: "squeezed it till it screamed, there came all the others from thereabout as if they would free it" — [Hume 2007](https://julianhume.co.uk/wp-content/uploads/2010/07/Hume-Mascarene-Parrots.pdf)
- Dubois 1671–72: Réunion birds spent six months in the lowlands and six in the mountains (altitudinal migration). Feuilley 1705: parrots were fat from June to September — [Hume 2007](https://julianhume.co.uk/wp-content/uploads/2010/07/Hume-Mascarene-Parrots.pdf)
- Last records: Mauritius grey, Cossigny 1764; Réunion grey, Cossigny 1732 ("The woods are full of parrots, either completely grey or completely green") — [Hume 2007](https://julianhume.co.uk/wp-content/uploads/2010/07/Hume-Mascarene-Parrots.pdf)

**Broad-billed parrot, *Lophopsittacus mauritianus* (Dutch "Indian raven/crow"), Mauritius. INFERRED.**
- Called the "Indian crow/raven" from 1598 until the last first-hand account by Hoffmann in 1673–75. Hoffmann described "red crows with recurved beaks and with blue heads, which fly with difficulty" — [Hume 2007](https://julianhume.co.uk/wp-content/uploads/2010/07/Hume-Mascarene-Parrots.pdf)
- Hume suggests the corvid name may reflect "its probable crow-like (raucous) call", or behaviour or colour. Reyer Cornelisz (1602) distinguished "large and small Indian Crows" — [Hume 2007](https://julianhume.co.uk/wp-content/uploads/2010/07/Hume-Mascarene-Parrots.pdf)

**Mascarene parrot, *Mascarinus mascarin*, Réunion. DESCRIBED (doubtful).**
- Hahn (1834): "It is not very lively and its call is harsh." Hume doubts Hahn saw a live bird.
- Last mentioned alive on Réunion in the 1770s; captives lived in Paris in the 1780s (Mauduyt 1784).
— [Hume 2007](https://julianhume.co.uk/wp-content/uploads/2010/07/Hume-Mascarene-Parrots.pdf)

**Rodrigues parakeet (Newton's parakeet), *Psittacula exsul*. DESCRIBED (mimicry).**
- Leguat (1708): "We often delighted our selves in teaching the Parrots to speak, there being vast numbers of them. We carried one to Maurice Isle, which talk'd French and Flemish."
- Common in 1726; rare by 1761; "great flights of … green parrots" in 1843 (Corby).
- Last: a male obtained 14 August 1875; Slater saw one on 30 September 1874. Cyclones followed in 1876.
— [Hume 2007](https://julianhume.co.uk/wp-content/uploads/2010/07/Hume-Mascarene-Parrots.pdf)

**Rodrigues parrot, *Necropsittacus rodericanus*. No voice data.**
- Tafforet (1726) describes birds larger than a pigeon, green, feeding on the southern islets and coming to the mainland to drink. Pingré's 1761 account is the last mention — [Hume 2007](https://julianhume.co.uk/wp-content/uploads/2010/07/Hume-Mascarene-Parrots.pdf)

**Rodrigues starling, *Necropsar rodericanus*. DESCRIBED (song).**
- Tafforet (1725–26): "a wonderful warbling" with "many and altogether different notes".
- Confined to Île Gombrani (islet au Mât), feeding on seabird eggs. Tamed on cooked meat.
- Gone by Pingré's 1761 visit.
— [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rodrigues_starling)

**Hoopoe starling / Réunion starling, *Fregilupus varius* ("huppe"; "calandre"; possibly "tivouch"). DESCRIBED (song).**
- Levaillant (1807): a "bright and cheerful whistle", "clear notes". Large flocks in humid areas and marshes.
- Vinson (1877): flocks in rainforest "flying and leaping from tree to tree".
- Feuilley (1704): tame.
- Seasonal altitudinal movement. Last definite live record in the 1830s–40s; possibly extinct before the 1860s.
— [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hoopoe_starling)

**Mauritius blue pigeon, *Alectroenas nitidissimus* ("pigeon hollandais"). DESCRIBED (captive).**
- A captive bird in the Netherlands around 1790. Arnout Vosmaer noted calls "sounding like 'baf baf', as well as a cooing sound", and a raised, vibrating ruff in display.
- Common around 1732, rare by 1755 (Cossigny). Last specimen 1826 (Savanne); possibly survived to about 1837.
— [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mauritius_blue_pigeon)

**Réunion ibis ("solitaire" of Réunion), *Threskiornis solitarius*. No voice data.**
- Tatton (1625): "white, and in a manner tame". Dubois (1674): the size of a goose, black wing tips.
- Feuilley (1708): "They inhabit the tops of mountains", the last definite account. Extinct about 1710–15.
— [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/R%C3%A9union_ibis)

**Mauritius owl, *Otus sauzieri*. No voice data.**
- Desjardins, September 1837: reports of owls in the Savanne forests. Clark (1859): "no more remaining". Long legs; possibly semi-diurnal.
— [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mauritius_owl)

**Small Mauritian flying fox, *Pteropus subniger* (Mauritius and Réunion). DESCRIBED (roosts), no sound.**
- 1772 account: "They live communally in the large hollows of rotten trees, in numbers sometimes exceeding four hundred". Hunted "throughout all the summer, all the autumn and part of the winter".
- IUCN: extinct between 1864 and 1873.
— [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Small_Mauritian_flying_fox)

**Seychelles parakeet, *Psittacula wardi* ("cateau vert"). DESCRIBED (captive).**
- 1820: "not remarkable for their imitative powers". Marianne North (1883), captive birds on Mahé: they "jabbered in a melancholy way".
- Abundant in 1811, almost exterminated by 1867 (crop persecution). Last shot March 1893 by W. L. Abbott on Mahé. None found in 1906.
— [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seychelles_parakeet)

**Snail-eating (Delalande's) coua, *Coua delalandei*, Madagascar. No voice data.**
- All well-localised specimens come from Nosy Boraha (Île Sainte-Marie). 12 of 14 were collected 1827–1834, many by Bernier. Not reported since 1834 — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Delalande%27s_coua)

**Alaotra grebe, *Tachybaptus rufolavatus*, Madagascar. No voice data.**
- Lake Alaotra. 12 birds in 1982, 2 by 1985; the last birds were possibly little-grebe hybrids.
- Extinction drivers: introduced snakehead fish and nylon gill-nets. Declared extinct in 2010.
- Probable courtship in December and breeding January–March.
— [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alaotra_grebe); [Mongabay 2010](https://news.mongabay.com/2010/05/no-hope-now-remains-for-the-alaotra-grebe/)

### Inferences
- Synthesis cues from the descriptions (not measured values):

  | Species | Cue |
  |---|---|
  | Rodrigues solitaire | A low, rolling mechanical rattle or drum from wing-whirring: 20–30 rotations over 4–5 minutes. Audible at about 200 paces (roughly 150 m); "distant thunder" when angry. A non-vocal percussive layer, not song. |
  | Grey parrots (Mauritius and Réunion) | A single distress scream triggers a mass converging chorus. A good "event" to trigger at historical abundance. Altitudinal seasonality on Réunion: 6 months lowland, 6 months mountain. |
  | Rodrigues starling and hoopoe starling | Varied warbling and clear whistles, modelled on living starlings (Sturnidae). |
  | Mauritius blue pigeon | A barking "baf baf" plus cooing, modelled on the living blue pigeons *Alectroenas madagascariensis*, *A. pulcherrimus* (Seychelles) and *A. sganzini*. |
  | Dodo | A low-frequency, pigeon-like two-note "doo-doo" or gosling-like cry. INFERRED from Nicobar pigeon and crowned pigeons plus the endocast low-frequency hearing evidence. Keep it clearly labelled as reconstruction. |

- Approximate island coordinates (geographic knowledge, not from the sources):

  | Place | Approx. lat/lon |
  |---|---|
  | Mauritius | 20.3 S, 57.6 E |
  | Réunion | 21.1 S, 55.5 E |
  | Rodrigues | 19.7 S, 63.4 E |
  | Île Gombrani | about 19.76 S, 63.43 E |
  | Mahé | 4.7 S, 55.5 E |
  | Nosy Boraha | 16.9 S, 49.9 E |
  | Lake Alaotra | 17.5 S, 48.5 E |

### Gaps
- Not reached in this pass: Mascarene coot, Réunion swamphen ("oiseau bleu"), Réunion kestrel, Rodrigues rail, Rodrigues pigeon, Mauritius night heron, Réunion owl.
- Mauritius kestrel is extant, a conservation success, and was not researched.
- No voice data found for the Réunion ibis, Mauritius owl, the couas or the Alaotra grebe.
- For the Alaotra grebe, the living little grebe's trill is the obvious model, but this is unsourced here.
- Full citation of the 2026 dodo/solitaire endocast paper: not retrieved.
- Hume & Walters *Extinct Birds* (2012/2017) could not be consulted directly.

## Q3. Other Old World and Atlantic-island birds: last records and sounds

### Takeaway
Voices are known or described for:
- pink-headed duck: "wugh-ah" and a wheezy mallard-like whistle;
- Himalayan quail: a shrill whistle when flushed;
- Bonin grosbeak: Kittlitz's "soft, pure and high note";
- Canary Islands oystercatcher: "kvirr"/"kvik" calls, probably inferred from kin.

No voice data were found for the crested shelduck or the Arabian ostrich. The Liverpool pigeon is probably Pacific, so out of scope.

### Cited Findings

**Pink-headed duck, *Rhodonessa caryophyllacea*. DESCRIBED.**
- "A mellow two note call *wugh-ah*"; displaying males gave "a wheezy whistle like a mallard".
- Range: Gangetic plains (stronghold north of the Ganges and west of the Brahmaputra, e.g. Bihar), plus Maharashtra, Nepal, Bangladesh and Myanmar. Habitat: lowland marshes and pools in tall-grass jungle. Non-migratory, singly or in pairs.
- Last wild specimen collected by C. M. Inglis in 1935, Darbhanga district. Captives kept at Clères and Foxwarren. A 2017 Indawgyi (Myanmar) expedition failed to find it.
- IUCN: CR.
— [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pink-headed_duck)
- The last definite wild record is often given as 1949 — [search summary citing Oriental Bird Club and Re:wild](https://www.rewild.org/lost-species/pink-headed-duck)

**Himalayan quail, *Ophrysia superciliosa*. DESCRIBED.**
- Voice: "a shrill whistling note when flushed" (search summary of the Wikipedia article) — [Wikipedia search result](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Himalayan_quail)
- Found only near Mussoorie and Naini Tal (Uttarakhand), on steep grassy slopes at 1,650–2,400 m. Coveys of 6–10, arriving around November. Last verifiable record 1876 near Mussoorie. IUCN: CR — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Himalayan_quail)

**Crested shelduck, *Tadorna cristata* (Korean 원앙사촌, "cousin of the mandarin duck"; Japanese kanmuri-tsukushigamo). No voice data.**
- Last confirmed: one male and two females in May 1964, Rimsky-Korsakov Archipelago near Vladivostok. Disputed North Korea record, March 1971.
- IUCN: CR.
— [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crested_shelduck)

**Canary Islands oystercatcher, *Haematopus meadewaldoi* (*cuervo marino* on Fuerteventura; *grajo de mar* on Lanzarote; *lapero*).**
- Wikipedia gives calls as "repeated *kvirr* or *kvik-kvikkvik*" and an alarm "*peepe-peepe*". The provenance of these is unclear and probably inferred from relatives.
- Last collected 1913 at Jandía, Fuerteventura. Unconfirmed reports from Tenerife in 1968 and possibly 1981. A few hundred pairs at most. Listed Extinct in 1994 (extinction date given as 1950).
— [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canary_Islands_oystercatcher)

**Bonin grosbeak, *Carpodacus ferreorostris* (Chichijima, Ogasawara, Japan). DESCRIBED.**
- Kittlitz (1828): "a soft, pure and high note, sometimes short, sometimes drawn out; sometimes given singly, sometimes in a short series". Singly or in pairs, on or near the ground.
- Extinct soon after 1830.
— [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bonin_grosbeak)

**Bonin thrush, *Zoothera terrestris*. No voice data.**
- Kittlitz (1828) found it common near the landing site on Chichijima. Perry's 1853 expedition did not find it — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bonin_thrush)

**Arabian ostrich, *Struthio camelus syriacus*. No voice data.**
- Two relict populations: southeast Arabia, and the Saudi/Jordan/Iraq/Syria borderlands.
- Records:
  - 1928, east of the Jordan–Iraq border;
  - early 1940s, shot by pipeline workers near Jubail;
  - 1966, a dying bird in Wadi al-Hasa north of Petra.
- Appears in Job 39:13–18 and Lamentations 4:3. Reintroduction since 1994 uses *S. c. camelus*.
— [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabian_ostrich)

**Liverpool pigeon (spotted green pigeon), *Caloenas maculata*.**
- One specimen (World Museum Liverpool, NML-VZ D3538). Origin unknown, possibly Pacific (Tahitian "titi"). Recognised as extinct in 2008. Probably out of scope — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liverpool_pigeon)

### Inferences
- Arabian ostrich: the male's low booming display call in living *S. camelus* is the natural model (INFERRED). It is a strong infrasonic/low-frequency element for Levantine and Arabian steppe at historical abundance. Specific Hz values were not sourced here.
- Crested shelduck: model on common and ruddy shelduck calls (INFERRED).

## Q4. Marine and freshwater mammals: Steller's sea cow, Japanese sea lion, Japanese river otter

### Takeaway
- Steller's sea cow is DESCRIBED as nearly mute: horse-like snorting and sighing at the surface every 4–5 minutes.
- The Japanese sea lion has no documented voice; model it on the California sea lion (INFERRED).
- The Japanese river otter has no voice data.

### Cited Findings

**Steller's sea cow, *Hydrodamalis gigas*. DESCRIBED (Steller, *De Bestiis Marinis*, 1751).**
- "Nearly mute"; heavy breathing, "raspy snorting similar to a horse", and sighs — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steller%27s_sea_cow)
- The head was lifted about every 4–5 minutes to breathe "with a rasping and snorting sound after the manner of horses" — [search summary citing Wikipedia and National Geographic](https://nationalgeographic.com/history/article/stellers-sea-cow-30-foot-hunted-extinction)
- Behaviour: family groups that helped injured members; fed on kelp most of the day; thin in winter.
- About 2,000 animals at discovery in 1741 (Commander Islands). The Pleistocene range covered the North Pacific (Japan to California). Last record 1768; later claims (Attu, 1800s; 1962 near Kamchatka) are unverified.
— [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steller%27s_sea_cow)

**Japanese sea lion, *Zalophus japonicus* (ニホンアシカ nihon ashika; Korean 강치 gangchi). INFERRED.**
- Range: western North Pacific, Sea of Japan and Okhotsk; sandy beaches.
- Abundance: about 3,200 harvested around 1900; 300 by 1915; a few dozen by the 1930s.
- Last confirmed: a juvenile caught in 1974 off Rebun Island, Hokkaido. Unconfirmed sightings in 1983 and 1985. IUCN: Extinct.
- The last colony was on Dokdo/Takeshima (Liancourt Rocks), which is territorially sensitive.
— [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japanese_sea_lion)
- In the 1950s, 50–60 animals were on the Liancourt Rocks — [search summary](https://recentlyextinctspecies.com/128-carnivora/3408-zalophus-japonicus)

**Japanese river otter, *Lutra nippon* (Nihon kawauso).**
- Honshu, Shikoku and Kyushu. Declared extinct by Japan's Ministry of the Environment on 28 August 2012. A 2017 Tsushima otter turned out to be a Eurasian otter.
- Symbol animal of Ehime Prefecture. No voice data.
— [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japanese_river_otter)

### Inferences
- Steller's sea cow: a low, breathy, horse-like snort-exhalation about every 4–5 minutes per animal, in family groups, over surf and kelp in the Commander Islands. Keep it nearly silent otherwise. This is a strong, honest "near-silence" piece. Approximate location: 55.0 N, 166.2 E (geographic knowledge).
- Japanese sea lion: barking rookery choruses as in *Zalophus californianus*. The last known place, Rebun, is at about 45.4 N, 141.0 E; Liancourt Rocks at about 37.24 N, 131.87 E. Treat the Dokdo/Takeshima naming neutrally (e.g. "Liancourt Rocks").

### Gaps
- No primary Steller Latin text or translation was quoted directly. The frequently cited line that sea cows were silent except for sighing when wounded was not confirmed in these fetches.
- No recording or description of Japanese sea lion or Japanese otter voices was found.

## Q5. Terrestrial mammals: big cats, wild cattle and horses, antelopes, wolves, rhinos

### Takeaway
- The quagga is the standout IMITATION case: its Khoikhoi name imitates its "shrill, barking neigh" ("kwa-ha-ha").
- The Japanese wolf has an unverified modern howl claim.
- For most others (aurochs, tarpan, bluebuck, bucardo, tigers, Barbary lion, kouprey, Schomburgk's deer, Syrian wild ass, rhinos) no sound was described. They must be INFERRED from living relatives.

### Cited Findings

**Quagga, *Equus quagga quagga*. IMITATION and DESCRIBED.**
- Its call is "a shrill, barking neigh, of which its name forms a correct imitation" — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quagga)
- The name is from Khoikhoi and onomatopoeic, transcribed "kwa-ha-ha", "kwahaah" or "oug-ga" — [search summary](https://www.newworldencyclopedia.org/entry/Equus_quagga_quagga)
- Karoo and the southern Free State; herds of 30–50. Last wild population gone by the late 1870s. The last captive animal, a female at Artis zoo in Amsterdam, died 12 August 1883.
- A subspecies of the plains zebra. The Quagga Project has run since 1987.
— [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quagga); [SA History](https://www.sahistory.org.za/dated-event/last-quagga-world-dies-zoo-amsterdam-netherlands)

**Aurochs, *Bos primigenius* (Polish *tur*; German *Ur/Aurox*; Latin *urus*). INFERRED.**
- Rut in September; calves in spring; violent fights between bulls.
- Jaktorów Forest herd: about 50 in the mid-16th century, 4 in 1601. The last cow died in 1627 of natural causes.
— [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aurochs); [Cambridge / AGRI history of the aurochs in Poland](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/animal-genetic-resources-resources-genetiques-animales-recursos-geneticos-animales/article/abs/history-of-the-aurochs-bos-taurus-primigenius-in-poland/73E5642DC0324EC98B52B34C57F9AE92)
- A royal inspection of 1630 recorded the 1627 death — [search summary](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/animal-genetic-resources-resources-genetiques-animales-recursos-geneticos-animales/article/abs/history-of-the-aurochs-bos-taurus-primigenius-in-poland/73E5642DC0324EC98B52B34C57F9AE92)

**Tarpan (Eurasian wild horse). INFERRED.**
- Name from Turkic "wild horse". Western Eurasian steppe. Last wild 1879 (southern Russian/Ukrainian steppe); last captive 1909 (one source says 1918).
- Its wild status is debated: a 2021 study found the Kherson specimen was a hybrid. Herds ran from a few to hundreds, often mixed with domestic horses.
— [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tarpan)

**Bluebuck, *Hippotragus leucophaeus* (Afrikaans *blaauwbok*). INFERRED.**
- Southwestern Cape, about 4,300 km². Groups of up to 20.
- Last shot about 1799–1800 (Lichtenstein). Thunberg found it rare in 1774. The first historically recorded extinction of a large African mammal.
— [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bluebuck)

**Pyrenean ibex / bucardo, *Capra pyrenaica pyrenaica* (Basque *bukardo*; Catalan *herc*). INFERRED.**
- The last animal, Celia, was found dead on 6 January 2000 in Ordesa NP (Huesca), killed by a fallen tree. A clone born 30 July 2003 died minutes later.
- Abundant until the 14th century; under 100 by 1900.
— [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pyrenean_ibex)

**Barbary (Atlas) lion, *Panthera leo leo* (North African population). INFERRED.**
- Last recorded shooting in 1942 near Tizi n'Tichka, High Atlas. Relict survival is possible to the early 1960s (Algeria) and mid-1960s (Morocco).
- Moroccan royal lions went to Rabat Zoo, and their descendants are in European zoos. Genetically not distinct (grouped with West/Central African and Asiatic lions).
— [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barbary_lion)
- No description of its roar was found.

**Caspian tiger (Persian *babr* / *babr-e Mazandaran*; Turkic *jolbars*; "Hyrcanian", "Turanian", "Balkhash" tiger). INFERRED.**
- Riverine tugai forest and reedbeds (Amu Darya, Syr Darya, Ili, Chu), from eastern Turkey and the Caucasus through Iran and Central Asia to Xinjiang.
- Last records:
  - Kazakhstan: 1948, near the Ili River;
  - Turkmenistan: 1954, Sumbar valley in the Kopet Dag (some sources give 10 January 1954);
  - Iran: claims in 1973 and footprints in 1974;
  - Turkey: possibly until the early 1990s.
— [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caspian_tiger)

**Javan tiger / Bali tiger. INFERRED.**
- Javan: last tigers sighted in Meru Betiri NP in 1976, where tracks suggested 3–5 animals — [search summary citing Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Javan_tiger)
- Bali: an adult female shot 27 September 1937 was the last confirmed. Unconfirmed reports continued to the early 1970s — [search summary](https://conservewildcats.org/resources/extinct-in-the-wild/bali-tiger/)

**Javan rhino, Vietnamese subspecies *R. s. annamiticus*. INFERRED.**
- The last one was found shot, its horn removed, in May 2010 in Cat Tien NP. Declared extinct in Vietnam in October 2011.
- The species is "much less vocal than the Sumatran", and "very few Javan rhino vocalizations have ever been recorded". The Indian subspecies was extinct by the early 1900s.
— [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Javan_rhinoceros)

**Western black rhino, *Diceros bicornis longipes*. INFERRED.**
- Chad, CAR, north Cameroon and northeast Nigeria. About 10 in 2000, 5 in 2001. Last record 2006 in northern Cameroon; declared extinct by IUCN in 2011 — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_black_rhinoceros)

**Kouprey, *Bos sauveli* (Khmer "forest ox"; Cambodia's national mammal since 2005). INFERRED.**
- Range: eastern Thailand, southern Laos, western Vietnam and northern Cambodia. Last confirmed wild sighting 1969. Never more than about 2,000. IUCN: CR (Possibly Extinct) — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kouprey)

**Schomburgk's deer, *Rucervus schomburgki*. INFERRED.**
- Swampy grass plains of the Chao Phraya valley. Monsoon floods crowded herds onto high ground, where they were hunted.
- Extinct in the wild by 1932. The last captive, a temple animal in Samut Sakhon, was killed in 1938. Antlers were seen in a Laos medicine shop in 1991.
— [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schomburgk%27s_deer)

**Syrian wild ass (hemippe, achdari), *Equus hemionus hemippus*. INFERRED.**
- Large herds reported by 15th–16th-century travellers.
- Last wild animal shot in 1927 at al-Ghams near Azraq, Jordan. The last captive died in 1927 at Schönbrunn, Vienna.
- Appears in Assyrian reliefs at Nineveh and in the Bible (Job, Psalms, Jeremiah).
— [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syrian_wild_ass)

**Japanese wolf / Honshu wolf (ニホンオオカミ Nihon ōkami; 山犬 yamainu; deified as Ōkuchi-no-Makami). INFERRED, plus an unverified modern claim.**
- Honshu, Shikoku and Kyushu. The last specimen was taken on 23 January 1905 at Washikaguchi, Higashiyoshino, Nara.
- Claims include 1910 (Fukui), 1996 (Chichibu, Hiroshi Yagi photos) and 2000.
- Wikipedia mentions a 2018 audio recording of a howl that specialists found "nearly identical to that of an eastern wolf (*C. lycaon*)". This is unverified, and its provenance was not traced.
- Rabies from 1736. About 20 wolf shrines on Honshu, including Mitsumine in Chichibu. Okuri-ōkami ("escort wolf") folklore.
- Hokkaido (Ezo) wolf: last confirmed 1889.
— [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japanese_wolf)
- A requiem ceremony for the last Yoshino wolf is held at Obon — [Down To Earth](https://www.downtoearth.org.in/amp/story/wildlife-biodiversity/shades-of-bahraich-why-the-japanese-revere-their-long-lost-wolves)

**Out of scope (extinct before 1500, or dates unsupported):**
- Atlas bear: the commonly cited 1870 date is disputed; the latest confirmed remains are from the 8th century — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atlas_bear)
- Sardinian pika: extinct in Sardinia after about 810 BC and in Corsica by the 6th century AD. Cetti's 1774 "giant rats" of Tavolara are doubtful — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sardinian_pika)
