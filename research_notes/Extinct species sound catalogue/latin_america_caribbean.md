# Extinct, extinct-in-the-wild and "not heard since" species of Central & South America, the Caribbean, Bermuda and the Galápagos: what is known of their sounds

Scope: losses since ~1500, weighted to acoustic, range and timing detail for synthesis (never sampling) in "elsewhere".

Research method note: Macaulay Library (macaulaylibrary.org), xeno-canto, AmphibiaWeb and PMC all returned bot-challenge / "Access Denied" pages to automated fetches in this session, so recording metadata below comes from search-engine snippets of those pages and from secondary sources. Those items are flagged "(via search snippet)". The web-search budget for the session ran out before mammals, insects, the Sinú parakeet and some Caribbean items could be searched a second time. Those gaps are listed explicitly.

Provenance tiers used throughout:
- RECORDED: a real audio recording of the species is known to exist.
- DESCRIBED: a written description of the voice by an observer.
- IMITATION: a human imitation or onomatopoeic name.
- INFERRED: nothing is known, so the sound must be modelled on relatives or ecology.

## 1. Which species are in scope, and what is their status and last record?

### Takeaway
The region's best-documented recent losses are Brazilian Atlantic Forest and Caatinga birds (Spix's macaw, Alagoas foliage-gleaner, cryptic treehunter, Pernambuco pygmy-owl, Alagoas curassow). Butchart et al. 2018 formalised their status. The second big group is Central American frogs lost to chytrid (golden toad, Rabbs' fringe-limbed treefrog, Atelopus). Behind these are older, voice-less losses: Caribbean macaws, rodents, the monk seal and the warrah. The Spix's macaw's "back in the wild" status (2022–2025) has been thrown into doubt by a 2025 circovirus outbreak.

### Cited Findings

**Butchart et al. 2018 (Biological Conservation) reclassifications relevant here**
- Recommended moving to Extinct: cryptic treehunter (last seen 2007) and Alagoas foliage-gleaner (not seen since 2011). Spix's macaw: Extinct in the Wild. Pernambuco pygmy-owl and glaucous macaw: Critically Endangered (Possibly Extinct). Main drivers: habitat loss from agriculture and logging. — [Mongabay 2018](https://news.mongabay.com/2018/09/8-species-of-birds-have-possibly-gone-extinct-over-past-few-decades/); [NRDC](https://www.nrdc.org/stories/what-eight-latest-bird-extinctions-can-tell-us)

**Scheele et al. 2019 (Science), chytrid panzootic**
- Chytrid drove declines in at least 501 amphibian species, with 90 presumed extinctions. The impact was greatest in large-bodied, range-restricted anurans in wet climates of the Americas and Australia. Atelopus was among the worst-hit Neotropical genera. — [NESP summary](https://www.nespthreatenedspecies.edu.au/publications-and-tools/deadly-frog-fungus-has-wiped-out-90-species-and-threatens-hundreds-more); [FAPESP](https://agencia.fapesp.br/fungus-has-decimated-the-populations-of-501-amphibian-species-worldwide/30127/)
- The species-level list of the 90 was not retrieved (see Gaps).

**Spix's macaw status timeline, 2019–2026**
- IUCN 2019: Extinct in the Wild. As of June 2022, 52 birds had been released in protected areas in Bahia, with more released in December 2022, and chicks hatched in the wild. — [Wikipedia: Spix's macaw](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spix%27s_macaw)
- On 27 Nov 2025 ICMBio confirmed that all 11 surviving wild-released birds tested positive for circovirus (psittacine beak and feather disease). About 20 birds "had initially been freed", which conflicts with Wikipedia's figure of 52 released. About 21 birds at the Bahia breeding facility also tested positive. — [BirdGuides](https://www.birdguides.com/news/lethal-virus-strikes-wild-population-of-spixs-macaw-in-brazil/); [Mongabay Oct 2025](https://news.mongabay.com/2025/10/virus-outbreak-deepens-rift-over-return-of-spixs-macaw-to-brazil/); [Malay Mail](https://malaymail.com/news/life/2025/11/29/all-endangered-wild-spixs-macaws-in-brazil-test-positive-for-deadly-and-incurable-circovirus/199989)
- On 27 May 2026 Brazilian authorities removed 69 macaws from the private Criadouro Ararinha-Azul facility to a centre in Pernambuco, leaving 34 circovirus-positive birds behind. The article describes the species as "considered extinct in nature since 2000", with no wild population currently existing. This contradicts the 11 wild birds reported in Nov 2025, and the fate of those birds is unclear. — [Emol, 27 May 2026](https://www.emol.com/noticias/Tendencias/2026/05/27/1201173/rescatan-guacamayos-azules-brasil.html)

### Inferences
- For the "secret traces at last known places" mode, the strongest candidates with real recordings plus a dated, localised last record are Spix's macaw (Curaçá, 2000), the Atitlán grebe (Atitlán shoreline), the cryptic treehunter (Murici, 12 Oct 2002 onward) and the Alagoas foliage-gleaner (Frei Caneca, to 2011). Rabbs' treefrog has a single captive call (Atlanta, 2014), but its sound origin is El Valle.
- Spix's macaw should probably be flagged in the artwork's data as "EW → reintroduced 2022 → status uncertain 2025–26" rather than simply extant or extinct.

### Gaps
- The Scheele et al. 2019 supplementary species list of the 90 extinctions was not retrieved, so a complete list of Latin American chytrid extinctions is not given here.
- IUCN pages were not fetched directly. Statuses come from secondary sources.

## 2. Birds: range, timing and what is known of their voices

### Takeaway
Real field recordings exist for Spix's macaw (1993, 2000), the Atitlán grebe (LaBastille), the Alagoas foliage-gleaner, the cryptic treehunter and the Pernambuco pygmy-owl. Most Caribbean and Andean losses have no known voice and must be inferred from congeners. Several (Jamaican poorwill, Sinú parakeet, the Bogotá hummingbirds) are voice-unknown by any standard.

### Cited Findings

#### Spix's macaw / ararinha-azul — *Cyanopsitta spixii*
- Range and habitat: riparian caraibeira (*Tabebuia aurea*) gallery woodland in the Rio São Francisco drainage, in the Caatinga of interior NE Brazil. The last three birds were at the Barra Grande–Melancia streams, Curaçá municipality, NE Bahia. The species depended on caraibeira for nesting, feeding and roosting. Its range was a narrow ~50 km corridor along the São Francisco in Bahia and Pernambuco. It weighed ~300 g and measured 56 cm. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spix%27s_macaw)
- Last wild bird: a male (sexed from DNA in moulted feathers) that stayed at Curaçá and disappeared in October 2000. — [search snippet summarising xeno-canto / Wikipedia](https://www.xeno-canto.org/species/Cyanopsitta-spixii)
- **RECORDED**:
  - Frank Lambert, March 1993: two flight calls of the last known wild individual.
  - Juan Mazar Barnett, 7 Jan 2000: the last wild bird perched in gallery forest, giving a "prrr 'song'" followed by softer calls.
  - xeno-canto lists these recordings as restricted (hidden), consistent with sensitive-species policy. — [xeno-canto species page (via search snippet)](https://www.xeno-canto.org/species/Cyanopsitta-spixii)
- Culture: Portuguese name ararinha-azul; the species featured in the film *Rio* (2011). — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spix%27s_macaw); [CBC](https://amp.cbc.ca/lite/story/1.4811416)

#### Glaucous macaw — *Anodorhynchus glaucus*
- Range: middle reaches of the Uruguay, Paraná and Paraguay rivers and adjacent areas of N Argentina (most records from Corrientes), S Paraguay, NE Uruguay and S Brazil. It depended on the yatay palm *Butia yatay*. — [BirdForum / BirdLife summary](https://birdforum.net/threads/glaucous-macaw-anodorhynchus-glaucus.11545); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glaucous_macaw)
- Last records: a direct observation in Uruguay in 1951 and local reports in Paraná in the early 1960s. A 2018 study recommended CR (PE). — [parrots.org](https://parrots.org/encyclopedia/glaucous-macaw/)
- **IMITATION**: the Guaraní name *gua'a hovy* (also rendered *guaa-obi*) refers to its vocalisations. "Call: Not recorded." — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glaucous_macaw); [parrots.org](https://parrots.org/encyclopedia/glaucous-macaw/)

#### Cuban macaw / guacamayo — *Ara tricolor*
- Habitat and behaviour: open savanna with scattered trees in and around the Zapata Swamp, and Isla de la Juventud. It lived in pairs or families and ate royal palm and chinaberry seeds. One nest was recorded in a palm hollow. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cuban_macaw)
- Last records: Gundlach saw birds feeding near Zapata in 1849–50. The last pair known killed in the wild was shot in 1864 (sources give "San Francisco de la Vega" or "near La Vega", Isla de la Juventud). Gundlach reported unconfirmed persistence to 1885. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cuban_macaw); [recentlyextinctspecies](https://recentlyextinctspecies.com/psittaciformes-cockatoos-and-parrots/ara-tricolor)
- **DESCRIBED**: Gundlach reported that it "vocalized loudly like its Central American relatives", with mimicry "inferior to those of other parrots". — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cuban_macaw)

#### Lesser Antillean parrots (hypothetical species)
- Lesser Antillean macaw *Ara guadeloupensis*: described by Du Tertre (1654, 1657) and Labat (1742). Martinique macaw: based only on Bouton's account from the 1630s. Macaws persisted into historic times on Cuba, Isla de Pinos, Jamaica, Guadeloupe, Dominica and Martinique. — [Wikipedia: Martinique macaw](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martinique_macaw); [kiwifoto](https://www.kiwifoto.com/galleries/birds/lesser_antillean_macaw)
- Guadeloupe amazon, **DESCRIBED**: Du Tertre (1664) wrote that "It has a strong voice, talks very distinctly, and learns quickly if taken young." He also described it raising a neck ruff. Du Tertre (1667) described pairs incubating alternately and feeding at houses. — [Wikipedia: Guadeloupe amazon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guadeloupe_amazon)
- The "violet macaw" *Anodorhynchus purpurascens* is argued not to have existed. — [J. Caribbean Ornithology](https://jco.birdscaribbean.org/index.php/jco/article/view/103)

#### Alagoas curassow / mutum-de-alagoas — *Mitu mitu*
- Native to lowland forest in Pernambuco and Alagoas. The last wild specimen was documented around 1984. Captive birds came from 5 rescued in 1979, and about 35% of the captive stock is hybrid. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alagoas_curassow); [Mongabay 2019](https://news.mongabay.com/2019/12/extinct-in-the-wild-a-brazilian-bird-makes-a-tentative-return-to-the-jungle/)
- Reintroduction: three pairs (6 birds, GPS-tagged) were released in September 2019 into a 980 ha Atlantic Forest area in Alagoas, with plans for three more pairs a year until 2024. This was the first Latin American reintroduction of a species declared Extinct in the Wild. — [Mongabay 2019](https://news.mongabay.com/2019/12/extinct-in-the-wild-a-brazilian-bird-makes-a-tentative-return-to-the-jungle/)
- Voice: no description found (see Gaps).

#### Alagoas foliage-gleaner — *Philydor novaesi*
- First collected in 1979 at Murici, Alagoas, and described in 1983. Up to 4 birds were seen at RPPN Frei Caneca (Pernambuco) in 2003. It lived in interior upland forest at 400–550 m, from understorey to subcanopy, and was 18 cm long. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alagoas_foliage-gleaner); [BirdLife RDB PDF](https://datazone.birdlife.org/userfiles/file/Species/AmRDBPDFs/Philydor_novaesi_eng.pdf)
- Sightings, "some accompanied by photographs and sound recordings", continued at Frei Caneca until 2011. Declared extinct in 2019. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alagoas_foliage-gleaner)
- **DESCRIBED + RECORDED**: "a slightly descending series of whistles uü-uü-uü and a thürr alarm call". It was easy to locate because of its loud vocalisations. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alagoas_foliage-gleaner); [BirdLife RDB](https://datazone.birdlife.org/userfiles/file/Species/AmRDBPDFs/Philydor_novaesi_eng.pdf)

#### Cryptic treehunter — *Cichlocolaptes mazarbarnetti*
- In October 2002 a team at Murici recorded a bird they took to be the Alagoas foliage-gleaner, and it proved to be a new species. It was described by Mazar Barnett and Buzzetti in 2014. Last seen in 2007. Declared extinct in 2019. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cryptic_treehunter); [Sci.News](https://www.sci.news/biology/science-new-bird-species-brazil-cryptic-treehunter-02308.html)
- **RECORDED**: xeno-canto holds a recording by Juan Mazar Barnett, 12 Oct 2002, Estação Ecológica de Murici, Alagoas (XC 180942 and XC 163623 appear in search results; the exact mapping to dates is unverified). — [xeno-canto XC180942 (via search snippet)](https://xeno-canto.org/180942)
- **DESCRIBED (analysed)**: the typical song is "a soft rattle followed by a series of burry notes". The description analysed 27 songs and 129 calls. The song is markedly different from *P. novaesi* and close in structure to the pale-browed treehunter *C. leucophrus*, but differs in timbre and in the shape of the short notes. — [SACC proposal 714](https://www.museum.lsu.edu/~Remsen/SACCprop714.htm); [BirdGuides](https://www.birdguides.com/articles/new-brazilian-treehunter)

#### Pernambuco pygmy-owl — *Glaucidium mooreorum*
- Described in December 2002 from two 1980 skins from Reserva Biológica de Saltinho. Vocalisations recorded in 1990 helped establish it as a species. Also found in 2001 in a 100 ha forest at Usina Trapiche (Sirinhaém). Not documented since 2001. It lives in humid forest canopy near edges, below ~150 m (the source gives "490 feet"). — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pernambuco_pygmy_owl); [Search for Lost Birds](https://searchforlostbirds.org/birds/pernambuco-pygmy-owl)
- **RECORDED (1990) + DESCRIBED**: song is "a short phrase of 5–7 notes" with each note rising in pitch. Audio is on xeno-canto. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pernambuco_pygmy_owl); [owlpages](https://owlpages.com/species.php?s=2000)

#### Atitlán grebe / "poc" — *Podilymbus gigas*
- Endemic to Lago de Atitlán, Guatemala, at 1,700 m. It was flightless, 46–50 cm long, and laid clutches of 4–5 white eggs. Population: ~200 (1960), ~80 (1965), ~210 (1973), 32 (1983), last 2 sighted in 1989. Declared extinct by 1990. Causes: bass introduced in 1958 and 1960, the 1976 earthquake lowering the lake, and hybridisation with pied-billed grebes. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atitl%C3%A1n_grebe)
- **RECORDED**: Anne LaBastille contributed recordings to the Macaulay Library (ML asset 943) from the Lake Atitlán shoreline, Sololá. They include a male territorial call and an adult pair in courtship display with duet singing. The territorial call is "a series of pumping sounds much like pied-billed grebe", given with throat swelling and hunching. Source monograph: LaBastille 1974, *Wildlife Monographs* 37, "Ecology and Management of the Atitlan Grebe". — [Macaulay ML943 (via search snippet)](https://macaulaylibrary.org/asset/943); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atitl%C3%A1n_grebe)
- Name: "poc" was the local name. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atitl%C3%A1n_grebe)

#### Colombian grebe — *Podiceps andinus*
- Lago de Tota and nearby wetlands, Colombia. Abundant in 1945. Seen in 1972; last confirmed record was 3 birds in 1977. Searches in 1981 failed. Causes: drainage, siltation, pesticides, reed harvesting, hunting and trout predation on chicks. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colombian_grebe); [Bird Conservation International](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/bird-conservation-international/article/decline-and-probable-extinction-of-the-colombian-grebe-podiceps-andinus/C5E80220B926441AB7716C45252D18B7)
- Voice: no recordings or descriptions found. **INFERRED**.

#### Jamaican poorwill / pauraque — *Siphonorhis americana*
- Known from about 4 specimens, with no definitive record since 1860. Likely causes: mongoose, rats and habitat loss. CR (PE). "Its voice is unknown", which complicates any search. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jamaican_poorwill); [Search for Lost Birds](https://searchforlostbirds.org/birds/1601)

#### Jamaican petrel — *Pterodroma caribbaea*
- Last collected in 1879. Intensive searches in 1996–2000 found nothing. It may survive on Dominica or Guadeloupe (unconfirmed). Closely related to the black-capped petrel *P. hasitata*. Likely causes: mongoose and rats. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jamaican_petrel)

#### Cuban ivory-billed woodpecker / carpintero real — *Campephilus principalis bairdii*
- Formerly found in the Organ Mountains, the Ensenada de Cochinos lowlands and along the Hanabana River. Later restricted to NE Cuban montane pine forests (*Pinus cubensis*, *P. tropicalis*). Dennis relocated a population in the late 1940s. Lamb mapped 6 territories in 1956. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cuban_ivory-billed_woodpecker)
- Last accepted sighting: a female seen flying by Giraldo Alayón and Aimé Pasada on 16 March 1987, Ojito de Agua, E Cuba. Searches in 1991 and 1993 failed. There are unconfirmed reports of calls in the Sierra Maestra in 1998. — [Audubon](https://www.audubon.org/magazine/may-june-2016/can-ivory-billed-woodpecker-be-found-cuba); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cuban_ivory-billed_woodpecker); [Bird Conservation International](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/bird-conservation-international/article/status-of-the-ivorybilled-woodpecker-campephilus-principalis-in-cuba-almost-certainly-extinct/32D41A096E4EBFAA88363F6125430880)

#### San Cristóbal (vermilion) flycatcher — *Pyrocephalus (nanus) dubius*
- In 1929 it was "abundant along the arid western coast to the settlement of Progreso in the highlands". The Steadmans called it "extremely rare" in the 1980s. Last seen in 1987, and a 1998 expedition failed to find it. Causes: invasive plants, insect collapse, smooth-billed ani, and the parasitic fly *Philornis downsi*. — [Wikipedia: Brujo flycatcher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brujo_flycatcher); [Scientific American](https://www.scientificamerican.com/blog/extinction-countdown/first-bird-extinction-galapagos)
- Taxonomy: Carmi et al. 2016 split *P. nanus* and *P. dubius* from the vermilion flycatcher. Later checklists treat dubius as a subspecies of the brujo flycatcher, so its rank as an extinct *species* varies by authority. — [Mongabay 2016](https://news.mongabay.com/2016/08/scientists-have-just-discovered-the-first-endemic-bird-species-to-go-extinct-on-the-galapagos-islands/); [Wikipedia: Brujo flycatcher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brujo_flycatcher)
- Living model: the brujo flycatcher *P. nanus* (NT) survives. Twelve chicks were born on Santa Cruz in 2023 under management. — [Wikipedia: Brujo flycatcher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brujo_flycatcher)

#### Floreana mockingbird — *Mimus trifasciatus*
- Darwin's first Galápagos mockingbird. It was extirpated from Floreana about 50 years after his 1835 visit. About 550 birds survive on Champion and Gardner islets, with numbers tracking rainfall at the start of the breeding season. A reintroduction to Floreana is planned as part of Floreana restoration. — [PMC2865062](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2865062); [Galápagos Conservation Trust](https://galapagosconservation.org.uk/our-work/projects/restoring-floreana/)

#### Bermuda (night heron, hawk, towhee)
- Bermuda night heron *Nyctanassa carcinocatactes*: described by Olson & Wingate (2006) from subfossils. It was a crab specialist related to the yellow-crowned night heron. Reports from 1610 of numerous herons probably refer to it, and it went extinct in the early 17th century. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bermuda_night_heron)
- Bermuda hawk *Bermuteo avivorus*: Diego Ramírez in 1603 described "very handsome sparrow-hawks, so stupid that we even clubbed them". — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bermuda_hawk)
- Bermuda towhee: Strachey (1610, published 1625) wrote "Sparrowes fat and plumpe like a Bunting, bigger then ours". — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bermuda_towhee)
- None of these accounts describes voices.

#### Sinú parakeet — *Pyrrhura subandina*
- Sinú valley, N Colombia. There are 18 specimens from 4 localities, and no confirmed record since 1949. CR (PE), with fewer than 50 birds if any survive. It is on Re:wild's "most wanted lost" list. No voice information. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sin%C3%BA_parakeet)

#### Colombian/Ecuadorian hummingbirds
- Bogotá sunangel *Heliangelus zusii*: known from one specimen bought in Bogotá in 1909, and never observed in nature by biologists. Turquoise-throated puffleg *Eriocnemis godini*: CR (PE), known from a few 19th-century specimens, including two "Bogotá" trade skins. — [Search result summary: Smithsonian repository / BirdLife RDB](https://datazone.birdlife.org/userfiles/file/Species/AmRDBPDFs/Eriocnemis_godini_eng.pdf); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turquoise-throated_puffleg); [Auk 110:1-8](https://sora.unm.edu/sites/default/files/journals/auk/v110n01/p0001-p0008.pdf)

### Inferences
- **Spix's macaw synthesis**: the only wild-voice anchors are a rolling "prrr" song and flight calls. For the rest of the parameters (harsh, fairly high-pitched calls, compared with large Ara), a model from the captive population or from small macaws (*Primolius*) is reasonable. This is INFERRED, and the parameters need checking against the restricted recordings or captive recordings. Wild behaviour to model: pairs flying between caraibeira galleries along creeks at dawn and dusk.
- **Atitlán grebe**: model on pied-billed grebe song, a far-carrying accelerating series of "cow"/"cuck" notes, with the "pumping" timbre. Lower pitch is plausible given the ~2× body size, but this is INFERRED and not measured. Duets and territorial calls from the reed beds (*tul*) during breeding fit a lakeshore at 1,700 m. Unverified approximate coordinates: 14.7°N, 91.2°W.
- **Alagoas foliage-gleaner**: descending "uü-uü-uü" whistle series plus a "thürr" alarm; model on other *Philydor*. **Cryptic treehunter**: soft rattle then burry notes; model on *C. leucophrus*. Both species shared Murici (approx. 9.3°S, 35.9°W, unverified) at 400–550 m, which makes a good "secret trace" pair.
- **Pernambuco pygmy-owl**: 5–7 rising notes. Model spacing and pitch on *Glaucidium minutissimum*/*G. hardyi* group whistles (INFERRED).
- **Voice-unknown birds** (Jamaican poorwill, Colombian grebe, Sinú parakeet, hummingbirds, Bermuda heron, Jamaican petrel): use living congeners strictly as INFERRED models. Candidate models: *Siphonorhis brewsteri* (least pauraque, Hispaniola); *Podiceps occipitalis* (silvery grebe); other *Pyrrhura*; yellow-crowned night heron; black-capped petrel (nocturnal colony calls in the Blue Mountains).
- **Floreana**: the living *M. trifasciatus* song on Champion/Gardner is a direct model for "historical Floreana abundance".

### Gaps
- Exact xeno-canto and Macaulay catalogue numbers, durations and spectral measurements for Spix's macaw, the foliage-gleaner, the treehunter and the pygmy-owl could not be retrieved (bot-protected). Most Spix recordings are restricted.
- No voice description was found for the Alagoas curassow. Captive birds could be recorded, and *Mitu* curassows generally give low booming or humming song, but that is unverified here.
- No Hz or tempo values were retrieved for any bird above.
- It is unknown whether the Cuban ivory-bill was ever sound-recorded (Lamb 1948–56 or Short/Horne 1986–87).
- Caribbean parakeets and amazons on Martinique, Guadeloupe and Puerto Rico's Culebra were not researched individually.
- Sinú parakeet elevation not found. Turquoise-throated puffleg: a 1976 Ecuador record is sometimes cited, but it was not verified here.

## 3. Frogs: the chytrid silence and what the lost frogs sounded like

### Takeaway
Several of the most famous "lost frogs" were quiet or nearly voiceless. The golden toad was described as "voiceless", with only a later-reported low release chirp, and Holdridge's toad is mute. Their soundscape is therefore rain, stream and the other frogs around them, not their own calls. The Rabbs' fringe-limbed treefrog has one real recording: Toughie's call, captured on a phone in 2014. Atelopus harlequin frogs do call (pulsed, tonal and short calls; the jambato's dominant frequency is ~1.7 kHz). Puerto Rico's lost coquís include a "loud and sonorous" stream-dwelling caller.

### Cited Findings

#### Golden toad / sapo dorado — *Incilius (Bufo) periglenes*
- Type locality: Cordillera de Tilarán, 2 miles ENE of Monteverde, Puntarenas, 1,590 m. The holotype was collected 14 May 1964 by James, Scott and Savage, with 142 male and 31 female paratypes taken in April and May 1964. Lower Montane Rainforest along the continental divide at 1,590–1,600 m, with annual rainfall over 4,000 mm and constant fog. — [Savage 1966, Rev. Biol. Trop. 14(2)](https://tropicalstudies.org/rbt/attachments/volumes/vol14-2/02-Savage-Bufo.pdf)
- Abundance: "Within a radius of 5 meters at least 200 toads were visible". Each orange blotch on the black forest floor was a male. Pairs gathered at a water-filled depression 0.3 × 0.2 m and 5 cm deep. Males were active from 3–5 pm, at night in driving rain, and at 8–9 am. — [Savage 1966](https://tropicalstudies.org/rbt/attachments/volumes/vol14-2/02-Savage-Bufo.pdf)
- **DESCRIBED (voice)**: "The species is apparently voiceless and no sound was heard at the type locality that could be attributed to these toads. Males do not emit a release call when grasped… but give a typical release vibration." There is no tympanum, no columella, and no vocal sac or slits. Savage proposed that mate recognition was visual. — [Savage 1966](https://tropicalstudies.org/rbt/attachments/volumes/vol14-2/02-Savage-Bufo.pdf)
- Correction: later work reports that Savage was wrong about the release call. Golden toads gave a release call consisting of "a single low chirp", plus a call given by males in amplexus. This is attributed to Jacobson & Vandenberg 1991, *J. Herpetology* 25(3):321–327 (not read in original). — [ADW (via search snippet)](https://animaldiversity.org/accounts/Incilius_periglenes/)
- Season: explosive breeding in rain pools among tree roots, from late March through April (or March–June in heavy rains). The sex ratio was about 8:1 males to females. There were about 1,500 adults in peak years up to 1987, 10–11 toads in 1988, and a last single male on 15 May 1989. The range was at most 8 km², possibly 0.5 km², at 1,500–1,620 m. Martha Crump described 1987 mating as "statues, dazzling jewels on the forest floor". — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_toad); [ADW](https://animaldiversity.org/accounts/Incilius_periglenes/)
- Sympatric amphibians at the type locality: diurnal *Eleutherodactylus (Craugastor) podiciferus*; nocturnal *Atelopus varius*, *Hyla angustilineata*, *Hyla pseudopuma* and several *Eleutherodactylus*. — [Savage 1966](https://tropicalstudies.org/rbt/attachments/volumes/vol14-2/02-Savage-Bufo.pdf)

#### Rabbs' fringe-limbed treefrog — *Ecnomiohyla rabborum*
- Cloud forest on Pacific-facing slopes above El Valle de Antón (Coclé/Panamá provinces), 900–1,150 m, with a range under 100 km². Discovered in 2005 and described in 2008 by Mendelson et al. It glided up to 9 m, and tadpoles fed on the father's skin. Chytrid hit around 2006, and none was seen in the wild after 2007. The last female died in 2009, one male was euthanised in February 2012, and "Toughie" died 26 Sep 2016 at Atlanta Botanical Garden. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ecnomiohyla_rabborum); [National Geographic](https://www.nationalgeographic.com/animals/article/toughie-rabbs-fringe-limbed-tree-frog-dies-goes-extinct)
- **DESCRIBED (wild)**: males called year-round, peaking at the onset of the rainy season (mid-March to May). The call is a "grrrck" repeated at regular intervals, sometimes preceded by a single "warm-up" call of 3–5 notes. Males were territorial around water-filled tree holes and called from a nearby branch or vine, or sometimes from the tree-hole mouth. — [Wikipedia / AmphibiaWeb summary (via search snippet)](https://amphibiaweb.org/species/7233)
- **RECORDED (captive)**: on 15 Dec 2014 Toughie called for the first known time since his capture in 2005. Mark Mandica recorded it on his phone, the "only existing example" of the species' call. — [Wikipedia: Toughie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toughie_(frog)); [National Geographic](https://www.nationalgeographic.com/animals/article/toughie-rabbs-fringe-limbed-tree-frog-dies-goes-extinct)

#### Panamanian golden frog / rana dorada — *Atelopus zeteki*
- Western-central Panama cordillera. No wild sightings since 2009. CR, possibly extinct in the wild since 2007. More than 50 institutions breed it in captivity. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atelopus_zeteki); [Maryland Zoo](http://www.marylandzoo.org/news-and-updates/2023/11/research-at-maryland-zoo-provides-insight-into-panamanian-golden-frog-vocal-behavior/)
- **RECORDED (captive)**: a 2023 PLOS ONE study ("Critical calls: circadian and seasonal periodicity in vocal activity…", Maryland Zoo) found daily and seasonal cycles "just as they would in the wild", with "patterned, profound bouts" of calling. Males respond to a pulsed call with low-then-high frequency alternation by turning to face the source and answering with pulsed calls. Calls are chirps and trills, and the species lacks eardrums. Data are on Dryad (doi:10.5061/dryad.k98sf7mbx). — [PMC10434951 (via search snippet)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10434951/); [Maryland Zoo](http://www.marylandzoo.org/news-and-updates/2023/11/research-at-maryland-zoo-provides-insight-into-panamanian-golden-frog-vocal-behavior/); [Dryad](https://datadryad.org/dataset/doi:10.5061/dryad.k98sf7mbx)
- Semaphore (hand-waving) is thought to have evolved because of noisy streams. Combined acoustic and visual signals drew more responses than either alone. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atelopus_zeteki); [CZAW](https://czaw.org/?p=6255)
- Culture: a Panamanian national symbol that appears on lottery tickets, with National Golden Frog Day on 14 August rooted in pre-Columbian traditions. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atelopus_zeteki)

#### Jambato — *Atelopus ignescens* (Ecuador; rediscovered)
- **RECORDED**: the first call description comes from the rediscovered Angamarca population. It has 3 call types: tonal (frequency-modulated), short and pulsed. Mean dominant frequency is 1.7 kHz, with up to 11 harmonics. Batallas, Guayasamin & Vega-Yánez 2024, *Herpetozoa* 37. — [Herpetozoa (DOAJ)](https://doaj.org/article/b0b24f8d940449f7915b343b75009e65); [PDF](https://www.zobodat.at/pdf/HER_37_0421-0428.pdf)

#### Variable / Monteverde harlequin frog — *Atelopus varius*
- A diurnal stream-bank toad of fast streams. It was thought extinct in Costa Rica after being last seen in 1991, and 4 populations have been rediscovered since 2004. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atelopus_varius); [Mongabay](https://news.mongabay.com/2013/11/thought-to-be-extinct-halloween-frog-rediscovered-in-costa-rica/)

#### Holdridge's toad — *Incilius holdridgei* (Volcán Barva)
- Declared extinct by IUCN in October 2008 (not seen since 1987) and rediscovered in 2009 or 2010 (sources differ). It lacks vocal slits and is described as mute. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holdridge%27s_toad); [recentlyextinctspecies](https://recentlyextinctspecies.com/anura/incilius-holdridgei)

#### Craugastor stream frogs
- *C. rhyacobatrachus* has not been seen in Costa Rica since 1964 (presumed chytrid). *C. obesus* was last recorded in Costa Rica in 1984. *C. ranoides* has vanished from much of its Costa Rican range. — [Wikipedia: C. rhyacobatrachus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Craugastor_rhyacobatrachus); [iNaturalist CR assessment](https://inaturalist.nz/projects/costa-rica-amphibian-red-list-assessment-forum/assessments/532-craugastor-obesus); [Wikipedia: C. ranoides](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Craugastor_ranoides)

#### Puerto Rico's lost coquís
- Web-footed coquí *E. karlschmidti*: aquatic, in rocky torrents in closed mesic forest from 45 to 630 m, including El Yunque. **DESCRIBED**: "The advertisement call is loud and sonorous"; males called from boulders, banks and waterfalls. Last seen in 1974 or 1976 (sources differ; one source says 1988). Causes: chytrid plus climate change, and possibly invasive predators. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Web-footed_coqu%C3%AD); [AmphibiaWeb (via search snippet)](https://amphibiaweb.org/species/2996)
- Golden coquí *E. jasperi*: found in 1973, last seen in 1976. It is the only ovoviviparous New World frog. — [USFWS](https://fws.gov/species/golden-coqui-eleutherodactylus-jasperi); [10,000 Birds](https://www.10000birds.com/falling-silent-the-eleutherodactylus-frogs-of-puerto-rico.htm)
- Eneida's coquí *E. eneidae*: described by Rivero in 1959, uncommon in the 1980s, last recorded in 1990. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eneida%27s_coqu%C3%AD)
- Macaulay Library holds Puerto Rican frog recordings by George Drewry (e.g. El Verde Field Station). Whether any are of the extinct species is unverified. — [Macaulay ML213327 (via search snippet)](https://macaulaylibrary.org/asset/213327)
- Culture: Puerto Ricans are "used to falling asleep to the melody of the coquí" (CO-QUI). — [topuertorico.org](https://welcome.topuertorico.org/coquies.shtml)

### Inferences
- **Golden toad synthesis**: the honest reconstruction is mostly silence. Emphasise fog drip, rain on leaf litter, and the surrounding Monteverde frogs of 1964–87. Add very sparse, soft, low single chirps (release calls) only where "males" cluster at pools in April–May. Chirp frequency and duration are unmeasured, so this is INFERRED. Density can be very high (200 in a 5 m radius, ~1,500 adults). Approximate unverified location: 10.33°N, 84.78°W.
- **Rabbs' treefrog**: regularly repeated, raspy "grrrck" notes from canopy tree holes at 900–1,150 m, with an occasional 3–5-note warm-up. Seasonal peak mid-March to May, with some calling year-round. Tempo and spectrum should come from Mandica's recording if obtainable (its public availability and exact format are unconfirmed). Approximate location: El Valle de Antón, ~8.6°N, 80.1°W (unverified).
- **Atelopus**: use the jambato (~1.7 kHz dominant, harmonic-rich tonal, short and pulsed calls) and the *A. zeteki* chirp/pulsed-trill repertoire as the family template for lost Atelopus. Place them beside loud streams, with diurnal calling.
- **Web-footed coquí**: loud, sonorous calls against torrent noise from boulders and waterfalls, a distinct timbre from the familiar two-note *E. coqui* chorus.

### Gaps
- There are no quantitative call parameters (Hz, duration, pulse rate) for Rabbs' treefrog, the Atelopus zeteki paper (PMC was blocked), the web-footed coquí or the golden toad chirp.
- Whether Mendelson et al. 2008 made wild recordings of *E. rabborum* is unknown.
- A Macaulay asset (ML 28276) surfaced for a golden toad query, but its content could not be verified. A recording of the golden toad would be notable given Savage's "voiceless" description.
- Calls of *E. jasperi* and *E. eneidae* were not found. Rivero's *Los anfibios y reptiles de Puerto Rico* is the likely source.
- Haitian and Jamaican frog losses, Andean Telmatobius and the many Atelopus species were not individually researched.

## 4. Mammals: monk seal, warrah and island rodents

### Takeaway
Only two lost mammals here have any voice description at all. H. L. Ward (1886–87) heard Caribbean monk seals give "grunts, barks and snarls", and pups give a drawn-out guttural "ah". Bougainville (1760s) gave the warrah a "weak bark". The island rodents and Nesophontes shrews are voiceless in the record and must be fully inferred.

### Cited Findings

#### Caribbean monk seal / West Indian seal, "sea wolf" / lobo marino — *Neomonachus tropicalis*
- Range: Caribbean, Gulf of Mexico and W Atlantic. It hauled out in groups of 20–40, sometimes up to 100, on isolated islets and atolls. Columbus's crew killed 8 at Alta Vela, south of Hispaniola, in August 1494, and Ponce de León's crew killed 14 at the Dry Tortugas in 1513. Plantation-oil hunting ran from the 1680s, with a 1707 record of "hundreds" slaughtered, and commercial hunting ended around 1850. Breeding peaked in early December, and pups were black. Adults were ~2.4 m and 170–270 kg. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caribbean_monk_seal)
- Last record: 1952, a small colony at Serranilla Bank between Jamaica and Nicaragua (C. B. Lewis). NOAA declared it extinct in 2008 after a 5-year review. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caribbean_monk_seal); [Federal Register 2008](https://www.govinfo.gov/link/fr/73/32522)
- **DESCRIBED**: Ward (1887a, b) at the Triangles reefs, Campeche Bank (20.95°N, 92.23°W), in December 1886, where 40 were killed in one day. Adults gave "grunts, barks and snarls". A pup gave a "long drawn out guttural 'ah' with a series of vocal hitches during its enunciation". Another account has them "bark in a hoarse, gurgling, death-rattle tone". — [Monachus Guardian](https://monachus-guardian.org/mguard21/2131scien.htm); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caribbean_monk_seal)

#### Falkland Islands wolf / warrah — *Dusicyon australis*
- The only native land mammal of the Falklands, found on both East and West Falkland. Darwin (1833–34) found it tame and predicted its extinction. The last one was shot at Shallow Bay, West Falkland, in 1876. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Falkland_Islands_wolf); [falklands-southatlantic](https://falklands-southatlantic.com/warrah%20reduction%20and%20extinction.html)
- **DESCRIBED**: Bougainville (1766–69) said it "had a weak bark". Another account describes warrahs "Open mouthed, barking, howling, showing their teeth". It was more active in the evening than in broad day. It lived near the coast in tussac, dug burrows, used shortest-route trails between bays, and fed on penguins, geese, eggs and seal carrion. — [falklands-southatlantic: life and behaviour](https://www.falklands-southatlantic.com/warrah%20life%20and%20behaviour.html); [search-snippet summary](https://falklands-southatlantic.com/warrah.html)

#### Island rodents and insectivores (all INFERRED, no voice data)
- Jamaican rice rat *Oryzomys antillarum*: last record 1877. Causes: mongoose, brown rats and habitat loss. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oryzomys_antillarum)
- Martinique giant rice rat *Megalomys desmarestii*: ~36 cm and ~1 kg, jet black above and cream below, possibly semi-aquatic. Common until the late 19th century and extinct by 1902, from mongooses, with the 8 May 1902 Mont Pelée eruption a possible final blow. Related extinct *Megalomys*: *M. luciae* (St Lucia), *M. audreyae*, *M. georginae*, *M. curazensis*. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martinique_giant_rice_rat)
- Galápagos rice rats: *Nesoryzomys darwini* (Santa Cruz) is known from 4 specimens collected by Frank Wonder on 12–16 Jan 1929, last recorded 1930. *N. indefessus* also died out in the 1930s, from introduced *Rattus*. — [Wikipedia: N. darwini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nesoryzomys_darwini); [Wikipedia: N. indefessus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nesoryzomys_indefessus)
- *Nesophontes* (7 species: Cuba, Hispaniola, Puerto Rico, US Virgin Islands, Caymans): shrew-like, 5–15 cm, related to solenodons. Most died out around European contact, with no radiocarbon dates after the 16th century except the Cayman population (c. 1632–1774, likely around 1700). — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nesophontes)

### Inferences
- **Monk seal**: in-air haul-out soundscape of grunts, barks, snarls and hoarse gurgling barks from groups of 20–100 on sand cays, with pups' drawn-out "ah" with hitches around December. The Hawaiian and Mediterranean monk seals are the obvious living models (low-frequency in-air and underwater calls), but no Hz values were retrieved (see Gaps). Tie the "secret trace" to Serranilla Bank (approx. 15.8°N, 79.8°W, unverified) in 1952 and the Triangles (20.95°N, 92.23°W) in 1886.
- **Warrah**: a weak, fox- or jackal-like bark and occasional howls at dusk along the coast. Model on culpeo (*Lycalopex culpaeus*) and on canid bark/howl structure (INFERRED).
- **Rodents and Nesophontes**: at most ultrasonic or high squeaks and rustle in leaf litter. These should be near-inaudible texture rather than featured voices.

### Gaps
- No quantitative vocal data were gathered for Hawaiian or Mediterranean monk seals: the search budget ran out, and the Wikipedia article has none.
- Taíno or other Indigenous names for the monk seal (and for the Cuban macaw and hutias) were not found.
- Caribbean hutias (e.g. extinct *Geocapromys* on Jamaica/Caymans/Bahamas species), Puerto Rican and Hispaniolan extinct mammals, the Puerto Rican giant hutia and extinct Caribbean bats were not researched (search budget exhausted).

## 5. Insects, and cross-cutting guidance on provenance

### Takeaway
No documented extinct Latin American or Caribbean insect with any known sound was found in this session. For synthesis, the region's catalogue splits cleanly into a small RECORDED set, a DESCRIBED set with qualitative words only, and a large INFERRED set.

### Cited Findings
- Recording archives: xeno-canto holds recordings of Spix's macaw (restricted), the cryptic treehunter and the Pernambuco pygmy-owl. Macaulay holds LaBastille's Atitlán grebe (ML 943) and Drewry's Puerto Rico frogs. Dryad holds the *A. zeteki* captive call dataset. — [xeno-canto](https://www.xeno-canto.org/species/Cyanopsitta-spixii); [Macaulay ML943](https://macaulaylibrary.org/asset/943); [Dryad](https://datadryad.org/dataset/doi:10.5061/dryad.k98sf7mbx)
- Toughie's call was recorded by phone (Mandica, 15 Dec 2014) and is described as the only recording of the species. — [National Geographic](https://www.nationalgeographic.com/animals/article/toughie-rabbs-fringe-limbed-tree-frog-dies-goes-extinct)

### Inferences
- Provenance summary for the artwork's metadata:
  - **RECORDED (wild)**: Spix's macaw (Lambert 1993; Mazar Barnett 2000), Atitlán grebe (LaBastille), Alagoas foliage-gleaner (to 2011), cryptic treehunter (2002–07), Pernambuco pygmy-owl (1990), jambato (2020s, extant).
  - **RECORDED (captive)**: Rabbs' treefrog (2014), Panamanian golden frog (Maryland Zoo), Alagoas curassow and Spix's macaw (captive stocks exist).
  - **DESCRIBED**: golden toad ("voiceless", later a low chirp), web-footed coquí ("loud and sonorous"), Cuban macaw (loud), Guadeloupe amazon (strong voice, talker), Caribbean monk seal (Ward 1887), warrah (weak bark), Holdridge's toad (mute).
  - **IMITATION**: glaucous macaw (Guaraní *gua'a hovy*).
  - **INFERRED/unknown**: Jamaican poorwill, Jamaican petrel, Colombian grebe, Sinú parakeet, Bogotá sunangel, turquoise-throated puffleg, San Cristóbal flycatcher, Bermuda heron and hawk, Lesser Antillean macaws, all rodents and Nesophontes.
- Several lost amphibians were acoustically cryptic (golden toad, Holdridge's toad), and Atelopus signalled partly visually. A "lost species sing again" mode should represent them honestly by absence, or as the ambient chorus of their community, rather than inventing loud advertisement calls.

### Gaps
- Insects: no search was completed (budget exhausted). No candidates were verified.
- Seasonal and diel calling data (dawn chorus timing etc.) are missing for most birds above. Only the Rabbs' treefrog (rainy-season peak mid-March to May), golden toad (late March to June, active day and night) and monk seal (December pupping) have timing from sources.
- Taíno, Maya, Andean and Guarani cultural material beyond the items cited (poc, carpintero real, gua'a hovy, rana dorada/huacas, coquí) was not found.
