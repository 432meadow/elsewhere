# Lost mammals missing from the catalogue (land, bats, marine, freshwater)

Scope note. Sweep of recently extinct / EW / CR(PE) / regionally extirpated mammals since c. 1500, excluding Hawaiʻi and Aotearoa NZ, and excluding everything in `_already_covered.md` (implemented or already catalogued). All bounding boxes `[lon0, lat0, lon1, lat1]` are **my own rough estimates** drawn from the range descriptions cited (marked "bbox est."); none comes from a source. Provenance tiers: RECORDED / KIN / DESCRIBED / INFERRED. Web search worked in this session; a few primary papers were paywalled or refused (noted under Gaps).

## 1. Which recently lost mammal taxa are absent from the covered list and plausibly audible?

### Takeaway
About 25 mammal voices are missing and usable; the strongest are not globally extinct species but **extinct-in-the-wild or regionally silenced animals whose voices are recorded or fully known from kin**: gibbons across China, Sumatran rhino, red wolf, Père David's deer, saiga, dhole, walrus in the Gulf of St Lawrence, dugong in China, Przewalski's horse, Formosan sika, giant otter in Argentina. Most of the truly extinct small mammals on the Wikipedia/IUCN lists (rodents, Nesophontes, hutias, gazelles, sea mink) have **no recorded or described voice at all** and can only be inferred.

### Cited Findings

**Systematic pass through the Wikipedia list (species + subspecies tables), with disposition:**
- The list gives, for marsupials: broad-faced potoroo 1875, eastern hare-wallaby 1889, Lake Mackay hare-wallaby 1932, desert rat-kangaroo 1935, toolache 1939, desert bettong 1950s–60s, Nullarbor dwarf bettong early 1500s, crescent nailtail 1956, thylacine 1936, desert bandicoot 1943, four barred bandicoots (mid-19th–early 20th c.), lesser bilby 1960s, two pig-footed bandicoots 1950s — [Wikipedia: List of recently extinct mammals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_recently_extinct_mammals). *Not covered and new here:* eastern hare-wallaby, Lake Mackay hare-wallaby, desert bettong, desert bandicoot, barred bandicoots (all tier C — no voice description found).
- Rodents on the list not in the covered file: Cuban coney (early 1500s), Puerto Rican hutia (list: early 1900s), imposter/montane/Samaná hutias (early 1500s), Hispaniolan edible rat 1536–46, Little Swan Island hutia 1955, dwarf hutia 1937, San Felipe hutia 1978, Garrido's hutia 1989, Desmarest's pilorie 1902, St Lucia pilorie 1881, five Notomys hopping mice (1843–1901), white-footed rabbit-rat early 1860s, lesser stick-nest rat 1933, blue-gray mouse 1956, Galápagos rice rats 1930/1934, Anthony's and Bunker's woodrats 1926/1931, Gull-Island-type island mice, Candango mouse 1960 — [Wikipedia list](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_recently_extinct_mammals). No sound description located for any of them.
- Bats on the list: Christmas Island pipistrelle 2009 (covered), Sturdee's pipistrelle 1889, small and large Samoan flying foxes 1856 / 1839–41, lesser Mascarene flying fox 1864, Guam flying fox 1968, dusky (Percy Island) flying fox 1870, large Palau flying fox 1874, Lord Howe long-eared bat (catalogued) — [Wikipedia list](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_recently_extinct_mammals).
- Sturdee's pipistrelle is known from a single specimen from Haha-jima, Bonin Islands, last seen 1889 — [Wikipedia: Sturdee's pipistrelle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sturdee%27s_pipistrelle). The Percy Island flying fox is known from a single specimen collected 1859 and described 1878, presumed extinct by the 1890s — [Australian Museum](https://australian.museum/learn/animals/bats/percy-island-flying-fox/) (conflicts with the Wikipedia list's "1870").
- The Puerto Rican flower bat (*Phyllonycteris major*) is known only from subfossil material, "Late Quaternary" extinction with no firm post-1500 date — [Wikipedia: Puerto Rican flower bat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Puerto_Rican_flower_bat). The Okinawa flying fox was moved from Extinct to Data Deficient because its two specimens are taxonomically uncertain and of unknown provenance — [Wikipedia: Okinawa flying fox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Okinawa_flying_fox). Both are therefore weak candidates.
- Eulipotyphla on the list: Marcano's solenodon (1500s), six *Nesophontes* (early 1500s; catalogued), Christmas Island shrew (catalogued) — [Wikipedia list](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_recently_extinct_mammals).
- Primates on the list: Hispaniola monkey (early 16th c.), Jamaican monkey (1707–1725), large sloth lemur *Palaeopropithecus ingens* (1620) — [Wikipedia list](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_recently_extinct_mammals).
- Artiodactyls on the list not covered: red gazelle (late 1800s), Queen of Sheba's gazelle 1951, Saudi gazelle 1970, Lemerle's dwarf hippo (early 1500s), Père David's deer (EW; list gives 1939) — [Wikipedia list](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_recently_extinct_mammals). Subspecies: Caucasian wisent 1927, Carpathian wisent 1852, bubal hartebeest 1925, Portuguese ibex 1892 — same source.
- Carnivores on the list not covered: *Dusicyon avus* (early 1500s), sea mink 1894, giant fossa (before 1658), Japanese otter (catalogued). Subspecies: Texas red wolf 1970, Florida black wolf (list: 1934), Atlas bear (list: 1890), Mexican grizzly 1965, California grizzly 1924, plus eight grey-wolf subspecies (Hokkaido 1889, Japanese 1905, Kenai 1925, Newfoundland 1911, Cascade 1944, S. Rocky Mountain 1935, Mogollon, Texas gray, Banks Island, Sicilian 1924) which fall under the implemented "wolves" — [Wikipedia list](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_recently_extinct_mammals).

**Items the brief asked to check that turn out to be out of scope or non-viable:**
- Caribbean ground sloths: the latest AMS date for Cuban *Megalocnus* is 4190 BP (cal. c. 4700 BP) — i.e. not "surviving to c. 1500" — [Wikipedia: Megalocnus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Megalocnus). Exclude.
- Sardinian pika: radiocarbon evidence puts extinction in antiquity (Sardinia after 810 BC; Corsica between 393 BC and 6th c. AD); Cetti's 1774 "giant rats" of Tavolara are argued by Wilkens to have been brown rats — [Wikipedia: Sardinian pika](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sardinian_pika). The Wikipedia list's "1774" is therefore unreliable. Exclude.
- Tasmanian devil on the mainland: lost c. 3,000 years ago (pre-1500); 26 devils were released into a 400 ha fenced sanctuary at Barrington Tops in 2020 — [Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/amp/news/2020/10/5/tasmanian-devils-back-on-australian-mainland-after-3000-years). Out of date range.
- Atlas bear: the "last killed 1870, Tetouan mountains" claim is questioned by Cabrera and Thévenot for complete lack of physical evidence; only one 19th-c. sighting (1841, non-professional observer); youngest confirmed remains are 8th century — [Wikipedia: Atlas bear](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atlas_bear). Treat as doubtful post-1500 (C at best).
- Sea mink: "No vocal descriptions exist in historical records"; nocturnal, in rock crevices of the Gulf of Maine coast; Abenaki name *mousebeysoo* ("wet thing"); last kills 1880 near Jonesport, Maine and possibly 1894 Campobello Island NB (the latter possibly a large American mink) — [Wikipedia: Sea mink](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sea_mink). Tier C only.
- Guadalupe fur seal (rediscovered breeding in a cave on Isla Guadalupe, 1954), Juan Fernández fur seal (believed extinct by 1900; c. 200 found by Bahamonde in 1965) and northern elephant seal (fewer than 100 on Guadalupe by 1892) are **recovered**, not lost — [Wikipedia: Guadalupe fur seal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guadalupe_fur_seal); [pinnipeds.org: Juan Fernández fur seal](https://pinnipeds.org/seal-information/species-information-pages/sea-lions-and-fur-seals/juan-fernandez-fur-seal); [elephantseal.org](https://elephantseal.org/history-of-the-piedras-blancas-elephant-seal-colony/). They could serve only as "voices that were nearly lost" (elephant-seal males have individually distinctive, fixed-tempo pulsed clap-threat calls — [Science News](https://www.sciencenews.org/blog/science-ticker/elephant-seals-recognize-rivals-tempo-their-calls)).
- Bowhead whales of the Spitsbergen stock (pre-whaling estimates 25,000 to c. 52,500) sing "loudly, 24 hours a day, from November until April"; 184 song types were recorded in Fram Strait 2010–2014 — [Mongabay / Stafford et al. 2018](https://news.mongabay.com/2018/04/audio-bowhead-whales-in-the-arctic-sing-hundreds-of-complex-songs/); [environmentandsociety.org (Allen & Keay)](https://www.environmentandsociety.org/mml/bowhead-whales-eastern-arctic-1611-1911-population-reconstruction-historical-whaling). Probably subsumed by the implemented "whales, as many as before the whaling", but the *song diversity* data are new evidence if a Svalbard-specific voice is wanted.
- Zanzibar leopard (last confirmed live sighting early 1980s; declared extinct mid-1990s after a post-1964 extermination campaign tied to witchcraft beliefs; disputed 2018 camera-trap footage) — [Wikipedia: Zanzibar leopard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zanzibar_leopard); and Formosan clouded leopard (see §2) are viable only as KIN/INFERRED.

### Inferences
- The covered list's "wolves", "lions", "a tiger", "cheetahs", "whales", "elk bugling", "the bison herds" already absorb most extinct large-carnivore/ungulate subspecies; the red wolf is the one canid that is acoustically distinct enough (different howl repertoire) to merit its own voice.
- For Caribbean hutias, *Nesophontes*, rice rats, island woodrats, Australian hopping mice and gazelles there is no acoustic route beyond a kin model; they are best treated collectively (as the implemented "something small in the cay grass" already does) rather than as individual voices.

### Gaps
- IUCN Red List pages themselves were not fetched (Wikipedia list and species pages used as proxy); recentlyextinctspecies.com was not systematically swept.
- Not researched for lack of time: primates lost from Vietnam/Thailand, wild Bactrian camel (extant), Asian elephant retreat within China, Mediterranean monk seal (already catalogued), North Atlantic right whale in European waters (assumed under "whales"), Caucasian moose, Portuguese ibex sound, Kamchatka giant bear (dubious taxon), Barbary leopard, ocelot/jaguarundi extirpations, Samoan flying foxes.

## 2. Candidate dossiers (names, range, timing, last heard, status, provenance, sensitivities)

### Takeaway
Eleven candidates have recorded or kin voices with published numbers and dateable loss areas (rank A); a further group has good verbal descriptions or partly measured kin (rank B); the rest are inference only (rank C). Full ranking in §3.

### Cited Findings

#### 2.1 Gibbons across China (several taxa) — RECORDED/KIN + DESCRIBED
- Turvey et al. (Proc. R. Soc. B 2015) compiled 535 dated gibbon records from 420 local gazetteers (Ming, Qing, Republican); before 1600 gibbons occurred across much of central, southern and eastern China; range contraction began c. 1600 and accelerated after 1850; gone from 57.5% of formerly occupied regions by 1900 and 84.4% by 1950 — [Science News](https://www.sciencenews.org/blog/wild-things/gibbons-have-been-disappearing-china-centuries). A second summary says gibbons were recorded in 20 provinces, and that isolated populations went extinct within c. 40 years, but gives the percentages as "84.4% by 1950, 57.5% by 1980" — [conservationjobs.co.uk](https://www.conservationjobs.co.uk/articles/historical-records-track-gibbon-decline/) (conflict: the 1900 figure from Science News is more plausible).
- Gazetteers do not identify species; gibbons were recorded disproportionately because they were held to have mythical/supernatural properties, to channel *qi*, and to symbolise melancholy in classical poetry — [Science News](https://www.sciencenews.org/blog/wild-things/gibbons-have-been-disappearing-china-centuries); [conservationjobs.co.uk](https://www.conservationjobs.co.uk/articles/historical-records-track-gibbon-decline/).
- **Yunnan white-handed (lar) gibbon** *Hylobates lar yunnanensis*: last stronghold Nangunhe Nature Reserve, SW Yunnan; last observed 1988; "loud, melodious calls were last heard in 1992"; a survey of 4–18 Nov 2007 found no visual or auditory evidence and concluded the lar gibbon is extinct in China (announced 2008) — [Wikipedia: Yunnan lar gibbon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yunnan_lar_gibbon); [ScienceDaily 2008](https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2008/05/080520213819.htm). Subspecies validity debated; IUCN 2008 Data Deficient — same Wikipedia source.
- **Northern white-cheeked gibbon** *Nomascus leucogenys* in China: historically Mengla, Jiangcheng and Lüchun counties between the Lancang (Mekong) and Yuan (Red) rivers; >1,000 in the 1960s; by the 1970s morning calls no longer audible in Mengla town; c. 100 in the 1980s; 36 in nine groups in the late 1980s; interviews in 34 villages (2008) and listening posts (Dec 2011) found none: "extinct, or at least ecologically extinct, in China" — [Fan et al., Oryx](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/oryx/article/extirpation-of-the-northern-whitecheeked-gibbon-nomascus-leucogenys-in-china/45C418B53120683B50C3E4CFCF5F0CF7).
- **Hainan gibbon** *Nomascus hainanus*: >2,000 across the whole of Hainan in the 1950s; 13 individuals by 2003; now confined to Bawangling; sings duets at dawn — [Wikipedia: Hainan black crested gibbon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hainan_black_crested_gibbon).
- Acoustic data (kin, living populations):
  - *H. lar* male song (Khao Yai, 14 males, 48 songs, 3,993 elements): element start F0 median 700 Hz (390–1540), end F0 median 950 Hz (480–1950), max F0 median 950 Hz (530–1480); element duration median 0.27 s (0.01–2.09); call (phrase) duration median 8.4 s; inter-element interval median 0.54 s; solos audible to c. 1 km and may last hours — [Barelli et al. 2013, PLoS ONE](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3867390).
  - *H. lar* female great call climax exceeds 100 dB SPL at 2.7 m and is audible >1 km through dense forest; great call described as "long, extended wail-like hoots" — [Terleph et al., Animal Behavior and Cognition 12(2)](https://www.animalbehaviorandcognition.org/article.php?id=1400). Macaulay Library holds Khao Yai duet recordings timed c. 05:58–11:29 — [Macaulay Library asset 170808](https://macaulaylibrary.org/asset/170808).
  - *N. leucogenys* duet: female gives 15–30 notes of rising pitch, then the male's complex call with rapid frequency changes; cycle <20 s, repeated with increasing intensity for 5–17 min — [Wikipedia: Northern white-cheeked gibbon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_white-cheeked_gibbon). In Laos, first male song started a mean 12 min before sunrise, duets at mean 06:14, bouts mean 16 min, all song over by a mean 13 min after sunrise — ["Singing patterns of white-cheeked gibbons in the Annamite Mountains of Laos"](https://kirim.kmutt.ac.th/converis/portal/detail/Publication/600978564?lang=de_DE).
  - Crested-gibbon male phrase (measured on captive *N. gabriellae*, a congener): minimum c. 779–813 Hz, maximum c. 5,016–5,177 Hz; staccato notes followed by a three-note multi-modulation phrase; duets 10–25 min between 05:00 and 10:00 — [Frontiers in Ecology and Evolution 2023](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/ecology-and-evolution/articles/10.3389/fevo.2023.956922/full) (the page summary printed "kHz" for these values; they are evidently Hz).
- Range boxes (bbox est.): historical China-wide gazetteer range `[100, 21, 122, 35]` (progressive loss 1600–1950); Nangunhe lar gibbon `[98.9, 23.1, 99.4, 23.6]` (last heard 1992); Mengla–Jiangcheng–Lüchun white-cheeked `[101.0, 21.1, 102.7, 23.0]` (Mengla town silent by 1970s; all by c. 2008–2011); Hainan outside Bawangling `[108.6, 18.1, 111.1, 20.2]` (1950s–1980s).
- Habitat/timing: broad-leaved and monsoon forest with tall trees; dawn chorus, year-round; family groups — [Wikipedia: Hainan black crested gibbon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hainan_black_crested_gibbon).

#### 2.2 Sumatran rhinoceros (*Dicerorhinus sumatrensis*), mainland Asia and Malaysia — RECORDED
- Three call types: "eep" (c. 1 s yelp, commonest), "whale" (song-like, varying pitch, 4–7 s), "whistle-blow" (c. 2 s whistle followed immediately by a burst of air; loudest; may carry up to 9.8 km) — [Wikipedia: Sumatran rhinoceros](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sumatran_rhinoceros).
- Measured at Cincinnati Zoo on three adults (Andalas, Emi, Ipuh) at 1–3 m: eeps 70 Hz–4 kHz at 57–92 dB; whales 100 Hz–3.2 kHz at 87 dB; whistle-blows 17 Hz–8 kHz at 100 dB with strong infrasound; animals "produced signals almost continually" — [von Muggenthaler, Reinhart & Lympany, 142nd ASA meeting, Dec 2001](https://acoustics.org/pressroom/httpdocs/142nd/vonmuggenthaler.html).
- Former range: rainforest, swamp and cloud forest in India, Bhutan, Bangladesh, Myanmar, Laos, Thailand, Malaysia, Indonesia and SW China; last wild animals in Peninsular Malaysia (Aug 2015) and Sabah (Apr 2015); extinct in Malaysia when the last captives died in May and Nov 2019; Indian-range reports ended in the 1990s — [Wikipedia: Sumatran rhinoceros](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sumatran_rhinoceros).
- Northern subspecies *D. s. lasiotis*: India, Bhutan, Bangladesh, Myanmar, China; declared extinct in India, Bangladesh and China in the 1920s, again in NE India in 1997, Myanmar in the 1980s; unconfirmed survivors rumoured at Tamanthi (Myanmar) — [Wikipedia: Northern Sumatran rhinoceros](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Sumatran_rhinoceros) (dates conflict internally: 1920s vs 1990s for India).
- Timing: most active at dawn and just after dusk; daily wallowing; solitary, hilly country near water — [Wikipedia: Sumatran rhinoceros](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sumatran_rhinoceros).
- Range boxes (bbox est.): Peninsular Malaysia `[100.0, 1.2, 104.5, 6.7]` (2015 wild / 2019); Sabah `[115.3, 4.1, 119.3, 7.4]` (2015 / 2019); NE India–Bangladesh–N Myanmar `[88, 20, 98, 28]` (1920s–1990s, disputed).
- Status: CR; extant only in Sumatra and Kalimantan.

#### 2.3 Red wolf (*Canis rufus*), SE United States — RECORDED/KIN (reintroduced)
- Historical range from the Ohio valley and central Pennsylvania to the Gulf Coast, west to SE Missouri and central Texas; by the late 1960s only on the Gulf coast of W Louisiana and E Texas; declared extinct in the wild 1980; reintroduced 1987 at Alligator River NWR, Albemarle Peninsula NC; peak 100–120 (2012), c. 23–35 wild in 2023, c. 270 captive — [Wikipedia: Red wolf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_wolf).
- Subspecies: Florida black wolf *C. r. floridanus* extinct 1908; Texas red wolf *C. r. rufus* extinct by 1970; *C. r. gregoryi* surviving — [Wikipedia: Red wolf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_wolf) (conflict: the list page gives Florida black wolf 1934 — [Wikipedia list](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_recently_extinct_mammals)).
- Howl: across 2,000 howls of 13 canid taxa sorted into 21 howl types, the red wolf's most-used type is a "high, looping" howl absent from the timber wolf's low, flat repertoire; red wolf and coyote repertoires overlap heavily (highly modulated, "whining" type-3 howls); smaller canids incl. red wolf favour howls ending in a sharp frequency drop — [Kershenbaum et al. 2016, Behavioural Processes, via Sci.News](https://www.sci.news/biology/canids-have-21-distinct-howling-dialects-03625.html); [Syracuse University news](https://news.syr.edu/?p=92899).
- Close-range calls: squeaks 2.6–9.6 kHz and "wuhs" 100–1600 Hz, in sequences of 1–30 units, 22% with non-linear phenomena (captive wolves) — [Memorial University thesis](https://memorial.scholaris.ca/items/11fc136d-b4ee-4600-b8b7-87788d9de31e). Red wolf squeak band 2.7–9.5 kHz vs coyote 3.0–11.0 and grey wolf 1.8–6.0 kHz — [ASA lay paper](https://acoustics.org/tag/wolves/).
- Field recordings of the last wild animals exist in principle: the 1970s Texas Gulf Coast surveys used a hand-cranked siren to elicit howling, and "the red wolf could be distinguished from the coyote by its patterns of vocalization" — [Russell & Shaw 1971, SEAFWA](https://seafwa.org/journal/1971/distribution-and-relative-density-red-wolf-texas); [Riley & McBride 1972, USFWS Special Scientific Report 162](https://pubs.usgs.gov/publication/ssrw162).
- Cultural: Cherokee *wa'ya* (ᏩᏯ), companion of Kana'ti, father of the Wolf Clan (Aniwaya); killing wolves traditionally avoided — [Wikipedia: Red wolf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_wolf). Consultation with Cherokee and other southeastern nations would be appropriate.
- Range boxes (bbox est.): full historical `[-99, 27, -75, 40]` (lost progressively to 1980); last stand, SE Texas–SW Louisiana coastal prairie/marsh `[-95.5, 29.0, -92.5, 30.4]` (1980); Florida black wolf `[-88, 25, -80, 32]` (1908 or 1934). Exclude the reintroduction area `[-76.9, 35.3, -75.7, 36.1]`.

#### 2.4 Père David's deer / milu (*Elaphurus davidianus*) — RECORDED (captive and reintroduced herds)
- Names: *milu* 麋鹿; *sibuxiang* 四不像 ("four not alike") — [Wikipedia: Père David's deer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/P%C3%A8re_David%27s_deer).
- Once widespread in China's lowland wetlands; the imperial herd at Nanyuan near Beijing was destroyed after the 1895 flood and was gone c. 1900; Wikipedia also states the last wild animals were shot by 1939; all living animals descend from 18 gathered at Woburn Abbey; reintroduced Beijing Milu Park 1985 (20 deer), Dafeng 1986, Shishou/Tian'ezhou 1993 (free-living population c. 700 by 2015 after 1998 flood escape); c. 2,825 wild and 7,380 in reserves in China (2020); still listed EW — [Wikipedia: Père David's deer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/P%C3%A8re_David%27s_deer). (Date conflict: "c. 1900" vs "1939".)
- Rut voice: two stag call types, the common roar and the chasing bark; F0, formants and formant spacing fall with rising dominance rank; intensity differs between harem masters, challengers and bachelors — [Liu et al. 2016, Animal Production Science 56:971](https://connectsci.au/AN/article-lookup/doi/10.1071/AN15048). Bellowing rate differs by rank class (12 stags, Beijing Milu Park); stags also bark — [Li, Jiang & Zeng 2001, Zoological Research](https://www.zoores.ac.cn/article/id/792).
- Verbal description: "a loud resonant bugle beginning harshly and ending on a clear deep note dropping progressively in pitch"; roaring peaks 07:00–09:00 and 16:40–18:30; rut studied 1 June–31 July at Shishou — search-result excerpts from [Chinese Journal of Ecology 2013](https://www.cje.net.cn/EN/Y2013/V32/I5/1277) and a rut-behaviour paper indexed alongside it (exact attribution of the "bugle" sentence not verified — see Gaps).
- Range boxes (bbox est.): historical lower Yangtze–Yellow River wetlands `[111, 28, 122, 40]` (wild loss date uncertain, pre-1900); Nanyuan `[116.3, 39.7, 116.6, 39.9]` (1895–1900).

#### 2.5 Saiga (*Saiga tatarica*) in Europe and China — RECORDED/KIN
- Historic range from the Carpathian foothills and Caucasus to Mongolia and Dzungaria; eliminated from eastern Europe/Ukraine in the 18th century; regionally extinct in China and Dzungaria by the 1960s and in SW Mongolia; Kazakhstan population c. 3.9 million in 2025 — [Wikipedia: Saiga antelope](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saiga_antelope).
- Rutting males roar **through the nose with the mouth closed**, neck and head held horizontal, the nasal vestibulum pulled forward (c. 20% vocal-tract elongation); F0 44.5 ± 5.6 Hz; call duration 418 ± 162 ms; first formant c. 379 ± 47 Hz; audible to 400–500 m (non-rut calls c. 100 m); rut December–January; harems of up to 50 females; females give softer nasal contact calls to calves — [Frey, Volodin & Volodina 2007, J. Anatomy](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2375844) (recorded 2002–2005 at 1–10 m, Volokolamsk breeding station).
- Range boxes (bbox est.): Pontic steppe, Ukraine–Don `[24, 45, 45, 51]` (18th c.); Dzungaria/N Xinjiang `[80, 43, 92, 48]` (1960s).

#### 2.6 Dhole (*Cuon alpinus*) in Central and NE Asia — RECORDED/KIN
- No confirmed reports for >30 years from Russia, Mongolia, Kazakhstan (Altai, Tian Shan), Kyrgyzstan (Tian Shan, Pamir), Afghanistan, Tajikistan or Uzbekistan — "likely extirpated"; gone from >75% of historic range, incl. the Korean Peninsula — [Wikipedia: Dhole](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dhole) and IUCN text quoted via [Cornell Wildlife Health](https://www.wildlife.cornell.edu/our-work/wild-carnivores/dhole-health-and-conservation). A 2022 report claims dhole sign in Kyrgyzstan after three decades — [Down To Earth](https://www.downtoearth.org.in/news/wildlife-biodiversity/researchers-spot-dholes-in-kyrgyzstan-after-3-decades-81411) (flag: possible persistence).
- Voice: biphonic calls with a low component of 0.52–1.44 kHz and a high (whistle-like) component of 5.51–10.77 kHz, occurring together or separately — [Volodin & Volodina, "Biphonation as a prominent feature of the dhole *Cuon alpinus* sounds", Bioacoustics](https://bioacoustics.info/article/biphonation-prominent-feature-dhole-cuon-alpinus-sounds).
- Range boxes (bbox est.): Tian Shan–Altai–Sayan `[68, 38, 100, 53]` (by c. 1990s); Russian Far East–Korea `[124, 34, 140, 52]` (20th c.).

#### 2.7 Atlantic walrus of the Maritimes (*Odobenus rosmarus rosmarus*) — KIN (+ possibly distinct form)
- Once on the coasts of New Brunswick, Nova Scotia, Newfoundland, Sable Island and the Gulf of St Lawrence, in the tens of thousands; heavily hunted in the 17th–18th centuries; Capt. Crofton reported in 1798 that the Magdalen Islands walrus fishery was destroyed; oral tradition places the last Magdalen walrus in 1799 and the last in the Gulf in 1800; listed Extirpated under SARA; Sable Island skulls suggest a distinct "Maritime walrus" — [Parks Canada, Sable Island](https://parks.canada.ca/pn-np/ns/sable/nature/animaux-animals/walrus); [COSEWIC status report ch. 2](https://www.canada.ca/en/environment-climate-change/services/species-risk-public-registry/cosewic-assessments-status-reports/atlantic-walrus/chapter-2.html). (Parks Canada elsewhere says "by the late 1800s" for Sable — minor conflict.)
- Voice: males sing in bouts lasting up to 65 h; knocks and taps at 1–15 per second in four patterns (double knock, sequence, coda, diving vocalisation); the coda is often followed by a "bell" call; calls span 100 Hz–10 kHz, the bell c. 400–1200 Hz, produced from the pharyngeal air sacs — [NAMMCO: Atlantic walrus](https://nammco.no/atlantic-walrus/) (citing Stirling et al. 1983; Sjare et al. 2003).
- Range boxes (bbox est.): Gulf of St Lawrence `[-66, 45.5, -57, 50.5]` (c. 1800); Magdalen Islands `[-62.2, 47.2, -61.3, 47.9]` (1799); Sable Island `[-60.3, 43.85, -59.6, 44.05]` (18th–19th c.).
- Season: knock-and-bell song is a winter–spring breeding display (underwater); in-air roars and grunts at haul-outs in summer.

#### 2.8 Dugong (*Dugong dugon*) in China — KIN, heard through the water
- Lin et al. 2022 (R. Soc. Open Sci. 9(8)): historical records peak c. 1960, fall sharply after 1975; no verified field observation after 2000 and no record after 2008; only 5% of 788 fishers interviewed in four provinces had ever seen one (mean last-sighting 23 years earlier); "functionally extinct in China" — [UCL Discovery (Turvey VoR PDF)](https://discovery.ucl.ac.uk/id/eprint/10152877/15/Turvey_Functional%20extinction%20of%20dugongs%20in%20China_VoR.pdf); [DOAJ record](https://doaj.org/article/a4585dddac2b41979383ef7b6b17d688).
- Former Chinese range: Guangxi, Guangdong, Hainan, Taiwan, Beibu Gulf; legally protected since 1988; tied to regional mermaid legends — [Earth.com](https://earth.com/news/dugongs-declared-functionally-extinct-in-china).
- Voice: bird-like chirps (frequency-modulated, 3–18 kHz, <60 ms; commonest), trills (rising cascades of notes in 3–18 kHz, up to 2.2 s), barks (broadband 500–2,200 Hz, up to 120 ms); mean source level c. 139 dB (underwater); communication range tens to hundreds of metres — [DOSITS: Dugong](https://dosits.org/galleries/audio-gallery/marine-mammals/sirenians/dugong) (after Anderson & Barclay 1995; Ichikawa et al.).
- Range box (bbox est.): N South China Sea coast, Beibu Gulf–Hainan–W Taiwan `[106, 18, 121, 25]` (2000/2008).

#### 2.9 Przewalski's horse / takhi (*Equus ferus przewalskii*) — KIN (reintroduced)
- Mongolian *takhi* (Тахь); last wild sighting a single stallion in 1969 near Takhiin Shar Nuruu, Dzungarian Gobi; EW thereafter; reintroduced Hustai 1992, Takhin Tal, Khomiin Tal, Kalamaili (Xinjiang), Chernobyl zone 1998, Kazakhstan June 2024; Endangered since 2011; harems of one stallion with mares and foals, plus bachelor groups — [Wikipedia: Przewalski's horse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Przewalski%27s_horse).
- Voice: squeals, nickers and whinnies; whinnies are biphonic (two independent fundamentals, "F0" and "G0"; the high one is a laryngeal whistle); Przewalski stallions' separation whinnies are shorter than domestic stallions' and mares' are lower-pitched than domestic mares' — [Scientific Reports 2017, s41598-017-09437-1](https://link.springer.com/10.1038/s41598-017-09437-1) (via search summary); mechanism from [University of Copenhagen news 2026](https://www.ku.dk/news/all-news/how-horses-whinny-whistling-while-singing).
- Range box (bbox est.): Dzungarian Gobi, SW Mongolia–N Xinjiang `[86, 43, 97, 47.5]` (1969). Overlaps conceptually with the implemented "tarpans"; distinct region and culture (Mongolian national significance).

#### 2.10 Formosan sika deer (*Cervus nippon taiouanus*), Taiwan — RECORDED (reintroduced herd)
- Declared extinct in the wild c. 1969; first ten released in Kenting National Park in January 1994 — [Taiwan Today](https://taiwantoday.tw/AMP/society/top-news/18469/); [BioOne, Wildlife Biology 21(4)](https://bioone.org/journals/wildlife-biology/volume-21/issue-4/wlb.00047/Residents-attitudes-toward-reintroduced-sika-deer-in-Kenting-National-Park/10.2981/wlb.00047.full).
- Rut calls: moan and howl; 81–88 dB(A) (moan) and 92–96 dB(A) (howl) at 7 m; both begin mid-October, peak mid- to late November, few after until mid-January; calls individually distinctive (64–75% classification) — [Yen, Shieh, Wang & Wang 2013, Zoological Science 30:1025](https://bioone.org/journals/zoological-science/volume-30/issue-12/zsj.30.1025/Rutting-Vocalizations-of-Formosan-Sika-Deer-Cervus-nippon-taiouanusAcoustic-Structure/10.2108/zsj.30.1025.full). Sika moans have an extremely high fundamental frequency compared with other Eurasian deer — [PMC8602020](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8602020).
- Range box (bbox est.): western plains and foothills of Taiwan `[120.0, 22.0, 121.3, 25.2]` (1969).

#### 2.11 Giant otter (*Pteronura brasiliensis*) in Argentina/Uruguay — KIN (reintroduction under way)
- Last family group seen in Argentina in 1986; considered extinct in Uruguay; a family was released in Iberá on 30 June 2025, "the first reintroduction of a locally extinct mammal in the country" — [Mongabay 2025](https://news.mongabay.com/short-article/2025/07/giant-river-otter-returns-to-argentina-after-almost-four-decades); [Rewilding Argentina project document](https://www.rewildingargentina.org/library/documentos/ibera/nutria_gigante/giant_otter_reintroduction_project.pdf).
- Voice: 22 adult call types incl. whistles, three kinds of scream, hums; alarm snorts, growls, a wavering scream and "hah!" — [Science News on Mumm & Knörnschild 2014](https://www.sciencenews.org/blog/science-ticker/giant-otters-hum-scream-say-hah-and-more).
- Range box (bbox est.): middle Paraná–Uruguay basins, NE Argentina and Uruguay `[-60, -34.5, -53.5, -25.5]` (1986).

#### 2.12 European bison / wisent (*Bison bonasus*) — KIN/DESCRIBED (reintroduced)
- *Żubr* (Polish), *wisent*; lowland form from the Massif Central to the Volga and Caucasus; Transylvania last 1790; Carpathian subspecies gone by 1852; last wild lowland animal killed in Białowieża 1921; last Caucasian bison shot 1927; releases from 1929 and formally from 1951; c. 7,500 by 2019; herds average 8–13; rut August–October; emits a growl-like sound, *chruczenie*, when anxious or in the rut — [Wikipedia: European bison](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_bison). (Conflict: brief gives 1919/1927; Wikipedia 1921/1927.)
- Range boxes (bbox est.): Białowieża `[23.3, 52.5, 24.4, 53.0]` (1921); W Caucasus `[39.5, 43.3, 41.5, 44.3]` (1927); Transylvania/Carpathians `[22, 45.3, 26.5, 47.8]` (1790–1852).

#### 2.13 Miss Waldron's red colobus (*Piliocolobus waldronae*) — DESCRIBED
- High-canopy rainforest of SW Ghana and SE Côte d'Ivoire; groups of 20+; "a social and highly vocal animal, frequently communicating … using loud calls, shrieks and chattering"; last confirmed sighting 1978; surveys 1993–99 negative, declared extinct 2000; hunter-supplied tail (2000), skin and photograph (2001); CR (Possibly Extinct); any survivors would be in the Ehy/Tanoé forest — [Wikipedia: Miss Waldron's red colobus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miss_Waldron%27s_red_colobus). Calls believed to be red colobus were heard in Côte d'Ivoire in 2002 and reportedly in 2008, nothing since — [The Revelator](https://therevelator.org/red-colobus-extinction).
- Range box (bbox est.): `[-4.5, 5.0, -1.0, 7.5]` (1978 confirmed; calls 2002/2008).

#### 2.14 Madagascar: kidoky (giant lemur) and kilopilopitsofy (dwarf hippo) — DESCRIBED (imitated by eyewitnesses)
- Latest radiocarbon dates: *Palaeopropithecus* 510 ± 80 BP; *Megaladapis* 630 ± 50 BP; Wikipedia lists *P. ingens* to c. 1620 — [Wikipedia: Subfossil lemur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subfossil_lemur); [Wikipedia list](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_recently_extinct_mammals).
- Flacourt (1658): the *tretretretre* or *tratratratra*, "an animal as big as a two-year-old calf, with a round head and a human face", monkey-like feet, frizzy hair, short tail, solitary, greatly feared — now usually read as *Palaeopropithecus* — [Wikipedia: Subfossil lemur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subfossil_lemur). No call is described by Flacourt in the sources I could reach; the name may be onomatopoeic (unsourced — see Inferences).
- Burney & Ramilisonina (1998), interviews at Belo-sur-mer: the *kidoky* (seen 1952 by Jean Noelson Pascou; again 1976) resembled a sifaka with a human-like face, dark with white spots on forehead and below the mouth, fled on the ground in bounds ("very baboon-like"); Pascou imitated its call as "a long single 'whoop'", comparable to a short indri call; the animal would approach and keep calling if imitated correctly — [Wikipedia: Kidoky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kidoky). Usually matched to *Archaeolemur*/*Hadropithecus*.
- The *kilopilopitsofy* (reported 1976): cow-sized, dark, "grunted a lot", fled underwater; asked to imitate it, the informant "made noises very similar to that of a hippopotamus"; hippo fossils date to 1,000–400 years ago with one mandible c. 1600 CE; Flacourt heard of the *mangarsahoc* in 1648; highland stories to 1878 (Raybaud 1902); other names *tsy-aomby-aomby*, *omby-rano*, *laloumena* — [Wikipedia: Malagasy hippopotamus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malagasy_hippopotamus).
- Range boxes (bbox est.): Belo-sur-mer, SW coast `[43.9, -20.9, 44.3, -20.5]` (1952/1976 oral reports); Flacourt's Anosy/Fort Dauphin region `[46.3, -25.3, 47.3, -24.3]` (1658).
- Sensitivity: these are living Malagasy oral traditions gathered from named villagers; treat as held knowledge, not folklore to be freely dramatised.

#### 2.15 Sea otter (*Enhydra lutris*) extirpations — KIN
- Historic range from northern Japan across the Aleutians and down the American coast to central Baja California; extirpated from most of it by the early 1900s; relict groups in Haida Gwaii and at San Benito Island (Mexico) "subsequently died out" — [USFWS: Sea otter history and distribution](https://legacy.fws.gov/sites/default/files/documents/Sea%20Otter%20History%20and%20Distribution%2007282021.pdf). Last known Oregon sea otter shot 1906 at Otter Rock — [Oregon History Project](https://www.oregonhistoryproject.org/articles/historical-records/otter-hunting/).
- Voice: ten basic vocal categories (one graded); screams of mothers and pups are individually distinctive (80% / 75% classification); signals suited to short-range communication — [McShane, Estes, Riedman & Staedler 1995, J. Mammalogy 76:414](https://pubs.usgs.gov/publication/1007934).
- Range boxes (bbox est.): Oregon coast `[-124.7, 42.0, -123.8, 46.3]` (1906); Pacific Baja California `[-116.5, 27.0, -114.0, 32.5]` (early 1900s). Elakha is the Chinook-jargon name used by Oregon tribes' restoration effort — [Andy Kerr blog](https://www.andykerr.net/public-lands-blog/bring-back-the-elakha); tribal consultation appropriate.

#### 2.16 Big cats and bears lost regionally — KIN
- **Jaguar in the USA**: last documented female shot 1963 in Arizona's White Mountains / Mogollon Rim; only wandering males since — [High Country News](https://www.hcn.org/blogs/range/the-tail-of-two-kitties-jaguars-and-ocelots-on-the-comeback-trail/). The long-distance call is a deep, repetitive, cough-like "sawing"; a low-grade source gives c. 100–400 Hz, fundamental c. 200 Hz, 1–4 s roars in sequences — [soundcy.com](https://soundcy.com/article/how-does-a-jaguar-sound) (unreliable aggregator; treat numbers as provisional). bbox est. `[-115, 31.3, -103, 36]` (1963).
- **Eastern cougar**: last confirmed specimens Maine 1938 and New Brunswick 1932; USFWS declared extinct 2011, delisted 22 Jan 2018; subspecies validity contested — [Wikipedia: Eastern cougar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_cougar). The oestrous female's "caterwaul" is a long scream sliding from low to a high peak, said to carry >2 miles — [scienceinsights.org](https://scienceinsights.org/what-sounds-do-cougars-make-from-screams-to-purrs/) (popular source). bbox est. `[-90, 34, -64, 48]` (1938).
- **California grizzly**: c. 10,000 bears before Spanish settlement, almost statewide in chaparral, oak woodland and hardwood forest; last killed Aug 1922 (Tulare Co.), last seen 1924 (Sequoia); 2025 genomics finds it closest to Yellowstone grizzlies (subspecies doubtful); "Yosemite" probably derives from an Ahwahneechee word for grizzly — [Wikipedia: California grizzly bear](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/California_grizzly_bear). bbox est. `[-124.4, 32.5, -116, 42]` (1924).
- **Mexican grizzly** (*oso plateado*; Ópata *pissini*): Arizona, New Mexico, Texas, Sonora, Chihuahua, Durango; by the 1930s only Cerro Campana, Santa Clara and Sierra del Nido; c. 30 in 1960; declared extinct 1964; unconfirmed reports 1968; "last confirmed sighting in Sonora 1976" — [Wikipedia: Mexican grizzly bear](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mexican_grizzly_bear) (dates conflict: 1964 / 1965 / 1976). bbox est. `[-112, 24, -103, 34]`.
- Grizzly sounds: huffs (forced exhalations), jaw-popping (a loud wooden click), woofs, low growls and far-carrying bellows over kills — search-result summary of popular sources; recordings at [Acoustic Atlas, Montana State University](https://acousticatlas.org/item.php?id=2366).
- **Formosan clouded leopard** (Rukai *likuljaw*; sacred, hunting taboo; Paiwan nobility alone wore the pelts): last sighting 1983 (Tawu Mountain), last skin 1989 near Taroko; 1,450 camera sites / 128,349 trap-days 1997–2012 with none; declared extinct c. 2013; unconfirmed ranger sightings 2018–19 in Taitung — [Wikipedia: Formosan clouded leopard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Formosan_clouded_leopard). No sound data; essentially a silent presence. bbox est. `[120.6, 22.2, 121.6, 24.6]`.
- Leopard "sawing" roars are individually distinctive (93.1% identification, Nyerere NP) — [Growcott et al. 2024, via British Ecological Society](https://www.britishecologicalsociety.org/leopards-unique-roars-help-ecologists-spot-which-is-which/) — the kin model for the Zanzibar leopard (bbox est. Unguja `[39.15, -6.5, 39.6, -5.7]`, early 1980s–1990s).

#### 2.17 Scimitar-horned oryx, Arabian oryx, bubal hartebeest — status clear, voices undocumented
- Scimitar oryx: extirpated from 13+ range states; a few survived to 1988; EW 2000; reintroduced Ouadi Rimé–Ouadi Achim (Chad) from 2016, c. 400 by 2021; downlisted to Endangered 2023; Arabic *abu harab* — [Wikipedia: Scimitar oryx](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scimitar_oryx). No vocal information in the source.
- Arabian oryx (*maha*, *wudhaihi*): last wild animal reported 1972; reintroduced Oman from 1980, then Saudi Arabia, Israel, UAE, Jordan; first species to go from EW back to Vulnerable (2011); herds of 2–15, up to 100; national animal of Jordan, Oman, UAE, Bahrain, Qatar — [Wikipedia: Arabian oryx](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabian_oryx). No vocal information in the source.
- Bubal hartebeest: Morocco to Egypt north of the Sahara; herds of 100–200 in 16th-c. northern Morocco; gone from the Tunisian Atlas 1870; last Tunisian animal 1902 near Tataouine; last herd of 15 near Outat El Haj 1917; last captive died Paris 1923; last wild animal shot at Missour 1925 — [Wikipedia: Bubal hartebeest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bubal_hartebeest). No vocal information. bbox est. `[-10, 28, 12, 37]`.

#### 2.18 Australian additions — KIN where the species survives on islands/sanctuaries
- Burrowing bettong / boodie (*Bettongia lesueur*): "very vocal, communicating through grunts, hisses, and squeals"; the only burrowing macropodiform; warrens with 4–94 entrances housing 20–40 animals; extinct on the mainland through fox predation — [Wikipedia: Boodie](https://www.wikipedia.com/wiki/Boodie). An acoustic-repertoire thesis exists — [University of Adelaide](https://digital.library.adelaide.edu.au/items/46d6f9e0-90b1-4de8-bb6b-1ba9a38d5e03) (content not retrievable).
- Mala / rufous hare-wallaby: last mainland wild animals vanished from the Tanami in 1991 — [Australian Wildlife Conservancy](https://www.australianwildlife.org/animals/mala). (Culturally central to Anangu/Warlpiri — consultation needed; no voice data found.)
- Eastern quoll: last mainland record Vaucluse, Sydney, 1963; reintroduced to Booderee NP from 2018 — [Australian Geographic](https://www.australiangeographic.com.au/news/2018/03/eastern-quolls-returned-to-the-wild-australian-mainland).
- Lesser stick-nest rat: stick nests up to 3 m long and 1 m high; tame, climbing onto tables for sugar (Krefft 1862); last confirmed 1933 (filmed 18 July); credible 1970 cave report; IUCN CR(PE) 2008, Extinct 2016 — [Wikipedia: Lesser stick-nest rat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lesser_stick-nest_rat). No sound.
- Desert mammals generally: Burbidge, Johnson, Fuller & Southgate (1988) recorded Aboriginal names, former status and biology for the vanished central-desert mammals across 1,645,000 km² by showing museum skins to desert people — [Wildlife Research 15:9–39](https://connectsci.au/wr/article/15/1/9/39265/Aboriginal-knowledge-of-the-mammals-of-the-central). This is the place any call descriptions for Lake Mackay hare-wallaby, desert bandicoot, desert bettong or hopping mice would be (full text not reachable).

#### 2.19 Caribbean land mammals — INFERRED
- Puerto Rican hutia (*Isolobodon portoricensis*): Hispaniola, Gonâve, Puerto Rico, Virgin Islands; a staple food of the Taíno; extinction placed c. 1525 by the species page but "early 1900s" in the list — [Wikipedia: Puerto Rican hutia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Puerto_Rican_hutia); [Wikipedia list](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_recently_extinct_mammals). No behaviour or sound recorded.
- Kin models: Desmarest's hutia has four vocalisation types, one song-like, from >18,000 analysed calls (Děčín Zoo, 2024) — [Charles University thesis](https://dspace.cuni.cz/handle/20.500.11956/209386). Hispaniolan solenodon gives a loud defensive "chirp", aggressive "squeal", soft "squeak", high "clic", and echolocation clicks at 9–31 kHz; nocturnal, zig-zag foraging — [Wikipedia: Hispaniolan solenodon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hispaniolan_solenodon) (after Eisenberg & Gould 1966, [Smithsonian repository](https://repository.si.edu/handle/10088/11117)). This is the model for Marcano's solenodon.

#### 2.20 Extras not in the brief but strong on "characteristic movement sound"
- Dawson's caribou (*Rangifer tarandus dawsoni*), Graham Island, Haida Gwaii: first written record G. M. Dawson 1878; described by Seton 1900; last record four animals in 1908, three shot — [Wikipedia: Dawson's caribou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dawson%27s_caribou). Caribou are largely silent but walk with a castanet-like clicking from tendons rolling over a foot bone (calves do not click) — [Journey North](https://journeynorth.org/tm/caribou/Walking.html). bbox est. `[-133.2, 53.3, -131.6, 54.2]` (1908). Haida territory — consultation appropriate.
- Eurasian beaver: last English reference 1526; Netherlands 1826; Romania 1824; Belgium 1848; Sweden c. 1870; Spain 17th c.; down to c. 1,200 animals in eight relict populations c. 1900; c. 1.5 million by 2020 — [Wikipedia: Eurasian beaver](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurasian_beaver). (Tail-slap not described in that source — see Gaps.) bbox est. Britain `[-6, 50, 2, 58]` (16th c.).

### Inferences
- For "gibbons across central China" the species is unknown (gazetteers do not distinguish taxa); a defensible synthesis is a generic crested-gibbon (*Nomascus*) dawn duet in the south and a lar-type great call in SW Yunnan, with the central/eastern provinces flagged as INFERRED from kin even though the *presence and audibility* are DESCRIBED.
- Red wolf: no published Hz values for the long-distance howl were found; a model sitting between coyote and grey wolf (higher and more frequency-modulated than the implemented wolves, with a terminal pitch drop) is consistent with Kershenbaum et al.'s qualitative result.
- The *tretretretre/tratratratra* name reads as onomatopoeic, but no source I reached states that Flacourt described a call; the only eyewitness-imitated giant-lemur voice is the kidoky "long single whoop".
- Walrus, dugong and sea otter are all "heard through the water or at the shoreline" voices and would sit beside the implemented baiji and sea-cow treatments.
- Indigenous/cultural consultation flags: Cherokee and other southeastern nations (red wolf); Rukai and Paiwan (clouded leopard); Haida (caribou); Mi'kmaq/Wabanaki (walrus, sea mink — Abenaki name sourced, wider relationship not researched); Oregon coastal tribes (sea otter/Elakha); Anangu, Warlpiri and other desert peoples (mala, boodie, stick-nest rat; Burbidge et al. is informant knowledge); Malagasy villagers of Belo-sur-mer (kidoky, kilopilopitsofy); Mongolian herders (takhi).

### Gaps
- Turvey et al. 2015 full text (royalsocietypublishing.org) returned HTTP 403; I could not extract the list of 20 provinces, the northern limit, or per-province last-record dates. The supplementary table exists at [UCL Discovery](https://discovery-pp.ucl.ac.uk/1470080/22/Turvey_et_al._historical_gibbon_tableS1.pdf) (not fetched).
- No measured frequencies found for: red wolf howl; *H. lar* female great call (only SPL); Hainan gibbon song; Père David's roar (Liu et al. 2016 abstract gives no Hz); Formosan sika moan/howl (abstract gives only dB); wisent *chruczenie*; sea otter calls (McShane full text unreachable); boodie repertoire (thesis page empty); jaguar (only an aggregator source).
- The "loud resonant bugle…" description of the Père David's stag roar and the 07:00–09:00 / 16:40–18:30 roaring peaks came from search-result excerpts whose exact source paper I did not open; verify before quoting.
- Sumatran rhino: extirpation years for Thailand, Vietnam, Laos, Cambodia, Bhutan not obtained; northern-subspecies dates are internally inconsistent in the only source.
- Dugong: no source obtained for the Mascarenes or Okinawa populations asked about in the brief.
- Mainland-extinction date for the boodie not found in a source (brief suggests 1960s).
- Beaver tail-slap, bighorn horn-clash (Audubon's bighorn) and prairie-dog town soundscapes were considered as movement/colony sounds but not sourced.
- Sea otter: no dated last record for Hokkaido/Kurils or Baja beyond "early 1900s".
- Burney & Ramilisonina 1998 was read only through Wikipedia's summary, not the original (American Anthropologist 100:957–966).

### Inferences
- If only a handful can be built, the gibbon dawn chorus over southern and central China is the single strongest addition: it has the widest dated range, an explicit historical record of *being heard*, living cultural weight in Chinese poetry, and well-measured kin song.
- Sumatran rhino and saiga are the most acoustically unusual (whale-like song; nasal 44 Hz roar) and both have primary measurements, making them good value per unit of synthesis work.
- Reintroduced taxa (red wolf, milu, takhi, wisent, Arabian and scimitar oryx, sika, giant otter) need the same treatment the catalogue already gives "elk bugling"/"the bison herds": voice the *lost* range and dates, and leave out the reintroduction sites.

### Gaps
- Ranks rest on what could be fetched; several A-rank voices (red wolf, milu, sika, takhi) lack Hz/duration values in the sources reached and would need the primary papers (Kershenbaum 2016; Liu 2016; Yen 2013; the 2017 Scientific Reports whinny paper) before synthesis.
- No archive accession numbers (Macaulay, British Library, Tierstimmenarchiv) were collected for any species except one Macaulay lar-gibbon asset.
