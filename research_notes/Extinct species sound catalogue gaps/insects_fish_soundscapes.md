## (a) Singing insects: extinct, possibly extinct or regionally lost Orthoptera and cicadas

### Takeaway
The IUCN-extinct Orthoptera list is short and acoustically almost blank (only the Central Valley grasshopper is new, rank C); the usable material is in dated regional losses of loud singers: the New Forest cicada (Britain, 1990s, song measured), the mole cricket's churr (Britain, mainland last 1962), the Bermuda cicada (last heard early 1990s, song only by proxy), lost populations of Brood VII and Brood X periodical cicadas, and the prairie mole cricket's lek chorus across four US states. The best A-rank insect candidate is the New Forest cicada; the most culturally sensitive is Brood VII (Onondaga Nation).

Ranking summary: A — New Forest cicada; Brood VII lost populations (Rochester etc.). B — mole cricket (Britain); Bermuda cicada; prairie mole cricket (IL/KY/MS/TN); Brood X on Long Island (not quite extinct); field cricket (Britain, near-loss). C — Central Valley grasshopper; African and Canarian "possibly extinct" Orthoptera.

### Cited Findings

#### A1. New Forest cicada, *Cicadetta montana* — Britain's only cicada (rank A)
- What / status: found only in the New Forest in Britain, hence the English name — [cicadasong.eu article (via search)](https://www.cicadasong.eu/files/article-30.pdf). "*C. montana* has not been seen or heard anywhere in Britain since 1996" — [Wikipedia, Cicadetta montana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Forest_cicada).
- Dates conflict between sources and should be shown as a range (1991–2000): "The last definite sighting of a C. montana adult was at Denny Wood in 1991"; counts fell from 35 in 1970 to 2 in 1993; "evidence of singing males was recorded in this area until the mid-1990's, no records of adults or nymphal turrets have been made since 1994" — [New Forest Cicada Survey Report 2014, HLS New Forest (search snippet; PDF now 404)](https://www.hlsnewforest.org.uk/wp-content/uploads/sites/3/2018/03/New_Forest_Cicada_2014.pdf). "the last confirmed sighting was in 1993"; rediscovered in 1962 after a long gap — [I've never killed a pipit blog](https://iveneverkilledapipit.wordpress.com/2013/02/11/the-extinct-wildlife-of-the-uk-2-new-forest-cicada/). "the last unconfirmed sighting was in 2000" — [University of Southampton ePrints, New Forest Cicada Project (via search)](https://eprints.soton.ac.uk/362628). "no confirmed sightings since the 1990s" — [Species Recovery Trust](https://www.speciesrecoverytrust.org.uk/copy-of-new-forest-cicada); "last confirmed sighting was in the early 1990's" — [Buglife](https://www.buglife.org.uk/bugs/bug-directory/new-forest-cicada/).
- Where: New Forest, Hampshire; last definite adult at Denny Wood (source above). Point approx. 50.86 N, 1.54 W **(est.)**, radius ~10 km for "the New Forest".
- How the sound is known: recorded and measured on the Continent (the species is extant in Europe). "The song contains frequencies from 5 to 20 kHz with a maximum between 12 and 14 kHz"; each phrase is "a many seconds long sequence of short echemes (duration 26 ± 5 ms) repeated about 11 times per second and ending with one long echeme (duration 161 ± 20 ms)" — [cicadasong.eu article-30 (via search)](https://www.cicadasong.eu/files/article-30.pdf). A strong 14 kHz carrier is the feature used by the Southampton phone-app detector — [Southampton New Forest Cicada poster (via search)](https://web-archive.southampton.ac.uk/www.orchid.ac.uk/wp-content/uploads/2013/10/Cicada-poster8-A1_ReducedSize.pdf).
- Description: the call "sounds like static hiss to the unaided human ear and is sustained with relatively short lulls at irregular intervals"; females only click — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Forest_cicada). It "sings at a high frequency typically inaudible to adults"; sings May to July, "at the limits of human hearing" — [Southampton project pages (via search)](https://eprints.soton.ac.uk/362628).
- Reintroduction (so the loss may be temporary): Species Recovery Trust project launched 2024 with stock from Idrija, Slovenia; eleven females from northern France released into enclosures at Paultons Park on 12 June 2025; nymphs stay underground at least four years, so "the earliest signs of success may not appear until 2029" — [Countryfile](https://www.countryfile.com/wildlife/new-forest-cicada-reintroduction-hampshire-england); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Forest_cicada). Quote: "It's like bringing them back from the dead" — Charlotte Carne, Species Recovery Trust, in [Countryfile](https://www.countryfile.com/wildlife/new-forest-cicada-reintroduction-hampshire-england).
- Sensitivities: none cultural; note the live reintroduction so the work does not contradict it.

#### A2. Brood VII periodical cicadas (17-year; "Onondaga brood") — lost populations (rank A for the lost populations; SENSITIVE)
- What / when: the brood survives but is contracting. "Now-extinct populations near Rochester seem well documented" — [UConn Periodical Cicada Information Pages, Brood VII](https://cicadas.uconn.edu/brood_07/). Last reported in Madison County in 1899; in Monroe, Ontario, Tompkins and Yates counties in 1916; in Oswego County in 1933. In June 2001 cicadas were found at only 10 of 37 previously reported sites (2 of 8 counties), with dense populations "restricted to a small region south of Syracuse and centered on the Onondaga Nation" — [Cicada Mania, "The periodical cicada Brood VII revisited" (via search)](https://www.cicadamania.com/cicadas/the-periodical-cicada-brood-vii-revisited/).
- Where: Rochester / Monroe County, NY approx. 43.16 N, 77.61 W **(est.)** (lost 1916); Oswego County approx. 43.4 N, 76.2 W **(est.)** (lost 1933); Madison County approx. 42.9 N, 75.7 W **(est.)** (lost 1899). The living core is on and around the Onondaga Nation.
- Sound: same species complex as the two already-implemented lost broods, so the existing synthesis applies. Collective songs "may reach 100 dB in the immediate vicinity"; decim-type calling song resembles "weeeee-whoa" or "Pharaoh"; cassini/decula songs "intersperse buzzing and ticking sounds" — [Wikipedia, Periodical cicadas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Periodical_cicadas). Emergence years: 2001, 2018 (so 2035 next) — [UConn Brood VII](https://cicadas.uconn.edu/brood_07/).
- Cultural significance: "the cicadas sustained the people in times of need" — the brood is culturally important to the Onondaga Nation — [UConn Brood VII](https://cicadas.uconn.edu/brood_07/). Consultation with the Onondaga Nation would be appropriate before naming the brood "Onondaga" in the work or placing a voice on Nation territory; the lost Rochester/Oswego populations are off-territory and less sensitive.

#### B1. Brood X on Long Island (17-year) — nearly lost, not extinct (rank B)
- "A disjunct population exists on Long Island (Simon and Lloyd 1982)" — [UConn Brood X](https://cicadas.uconn.edu/brood_10/). In 2004 scientists "found no evidence of cicada activity, and it is possible this population is locally extinct" — [Wikipedia, Brood X (via search)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brood_X). But in 2021 Elias Bonaros found small numbers at Bohemia County Park and East Setauket; Cicada Mania lists New York as "not yet extinct" — [Cicada Mania (via search)](https://www.cicadamania.com/cicadas/periodical-cicada-brood-x-10-will-emerge-in-15-states-in-2021/); [amNY, "Small emergence of cicadas thought to be extinct found in New York" (via search)](https://amny.com/new-york/small-emergence-of-cicadas-thought-to-be-extinct-found-in-new-york).
- Where: Suffolk County, Long Island approx. 40.8 N, 73.0 W **(est.)**. Treat as "almost gone", not "lost".
- General cause named by Marlatt for brood disappearances: "the reduction in forests and the introduction and proliferation of insect-eating 'English sparrows'" — [Wikipedia, Periodical cicadas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Periodical_cicadas).

#### B2. European mole cricket, *Gryllotalpa gryllotalpa* — the churr lost from mainland Britain (rank B)
- Status: formerly in 33 vice-counties "mainly across Southern England but also in South Wales, western Scotland and Northern Ireland"; "only four confirmed sightings between 1970 and 2001"; "In 2014, a colony of mole crickets was found persisting in the New Forest" — [Wikipedia, Gryllotalpa gryllotalpa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gryllotalpa_gryllotalpa). One county record: last Cornish mainland record 1962 at Little Gaverigan, Goss Moor (nymphs on lettuces); "it may well be extinct" on the mainland; still on Guernsey — [Cornwall and Isles of Scilly Federation of Biological Recorders, CRDB (via search)](https://cisfbr.org.uk/CRDB/CRDB%20Gryllotalpa%20gryllotalpa.htm). Thought possibly extinct in Britain until the 2014 New Forest find — [Discover Wildlife (via search)](https://www.discoverwildlife.com/wildlife/insects-invertebrates/return-of-the-mole-crickets-following-first-confirmed-sightings-in-decades).
- Where: southern England broadly; Goss Moor approx. 50.40 N, 4.87 W **(est.)**.
- Sound: "a soft, 'churring' song from within a specially constructed chamber in the burrow system" which "acts to amplify the song"; "typically produced on warm mild evenings in early spring and they are similar to the song of the European Nightjar" — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gryllotalpa_gryllotalpa). "Male stridulation is a loud continuous purring" — [Orthoptera Recording Scheme](https://orthoptera.org.uk/node/3371). Males churr "on warm nights from April onwards" — [NatureSpot (via search)](https://www.naturespot.org/node/247675).
- Frequency (sources conflict): Bennet-Clark's abstract gives 1.6 kHz and acoustic power about 2.5 × 10⁻⁵ W for *G. gryllotalpa*, against 3.5 kHz, 8 ms pulses at 14 ms intervals and 1.2 mW mean power for the much louder French *G. vineae* — [Bennet-Clark 1970, J. Exp. Biol. 52:619](https://journals.biologists.com/jeb/article/52/3/619/21554/The-Mechanism-and-Efficiency-of-Sound-Production). A search summary instead attributed "3.2 kHz at 24 °C, an almost pure note" and "3512 ± 107 Hz" to *G. gryllotalpa* — [BioAcoustica record (via search)](https://bio.acousti.ca/node/15181); this may be a species mix-up in the summary. Use 1.6 kHz unless the BioAcoustica record is checked.

#### B3. Bermuda cicada, *Neotibicen bermudianus* — new sound evidence (rank B; already on the "known" list)
- Local name "Bermuda Singer"; sang "afternoons or evenings"; males produced "loud buzzing sounds"; dependent on Bermuda cedar; cedar forests died in the 1940s; survivors eaten by introduced kiskadees; last recorded "early 1990s on Nonsuch Island" — [Government of Bermuda, Dept. of Environment and Natural Resources](https://environment.bm/bermuda-cicada). Cicada Mania dates the blight to the 1950s and says "the cicadas that made the nights so uniquely magical and romantic in sound also largely disappeared. The last one heard buzzing was on Nonsuch Island back in the early 1990s" — [Cicada Mania (via search)](https://cicadamania.com/cicadas/tibicen-bermudiana-an-extinct-cicada).
- Proxy for synthesis: the lyric cicada *Neotibicen lyricen* "is the most closely related species ... behaviorally, morphologically, and genetically" — [Wikipedia, Neotibicen bermudianus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neotibicen_bermudianus). The lyric cicada's song "does not pulsate", a "monotonous zinnnnnnnng" — [University of Illinois Extension, Voice of the Wild ep. 64/67 (via search)](https://extension.illinois.edu/podcasts/voice-wild/episode-64-lyric-cicada-voice-wild).
- Where: Bermuda 32.30 N, 64.75 W **(est.)**; Nonsuch Island approx. 32.348 N, 64.663 W **(est.)**.

#### B4. Prairie mole cricket, *Gryllotalpa major* — lek chorus lost from four states (rank B)
- Largest cricket in North America; "considered extirpated from Illinois, Kentucky, Mississippi, and Tennessee"; in 1984 "thought to be extinct"; tallgrass prairie 99% converted; now only Oklahoma, Kansas, Missouri, Nebraska, Arkansas — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prairie_mole_cricket); [Kansas Dept. of Wildlife and Parks (via search)](https://ksoutdoors.com/Services/Threatened-and-Endangered-Wildlife/All-Threatened-and-Endangered-Species/Prairie-Mole-Cricket); [Fran Stallings (via search)](https://franstallings.com/node/354).
- Sound: "a pattern of long chirps with a frequency of 2 kHz and up to five harmonics" which "can be heard up to 400 m away"; "harmonic chirps, rather than the trills produced by most mole crickets"; males "build an acoustic burrow in prairie soil arranged in a lek-like arena"; substrate vibration 30–300 Hz; females fly 1.5–5 m above the lek and detect males from up to 76 m; late spring — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prairie_mole_cricket).
- Where: no dated last records per state were found; place as a broad former-range voice in the extirpated states (dates unknown — see Gaps).

#### B5. Field cricket, *Gryllus campestris* — near-loss in Britain (rank B; not lost)
- "By the 1980s it was confined to one site in West Sussex with less than 100 individuals and was expected to go extinct"; song audible "from a distance of more than 100m on warm days during May and early June"; now six reintroduced populations in four counties — [RSPB "Crickets on a sticky wicket" (via search)](https://community.rspb.org.uk/getinvolved/b/south-east/posts/crickets-on-a-sticky-wicket); [GOV.UK press release (via search)](https://www.gov.uk/government/news/new-report-highlights-changing-fortunes-of-grasshoppers-crickets-and-allied-species). Useful only as a "lost from most of its English range" voice.

#### C1. Central Valley grasshopper, *Conozoa hyalina* (rank C)
- Endemic to the United States, the "California Central Valley grasshopper"; described 1901; IUCN Extinct — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conozoa_hyalina). Listed with the Rocky Mountain locust and the Antioch dunes shieldback as the three extinct Orthoptera — [Wikipedia, List of recently extinct insects](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_recently_extinct_insects). No sound information found.

#### C2. "Possibly extinct" Orthoptera (rank C; no sound data found)
- The list names, among others, Gran Canaria bush-cricket (*Evergoderes cabrerai*), three-lobed bush-cricket (*Rhacocleis trilobata*), Zulu ambush katydid (*Peringueyella zulu*), Peringuey's seedpod shieldback (*Thoracistus peringueyi*), Maspalomas bow-legged grasshopper (*Dericorys minutus*), and several Tanzanian grasshoppers (Morogoro, Usambara, Kilosa, Zanzibar; one is named "Mpwapwa silent grasshopper") — [Wikipedia, List of recently extinct insects](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_recently_extinct_insects). The list has no cicada entries at all.

### Inferences
- The New Forest cicada is the cleanest insect addition: dated, placed, measured, and its 12–14 kHz carrier makes a piece about what listeners can no longer hear in two senses (the species went, and older ears lose that band). The 11-per-second short-echeme train ending in one ~160 ms echeme is directly synthesizable.
- The Bermuda cicada can be synthesized honestly only as "a lyric-cicada-like unpulsed buzz in cedar, afternoon and evening" and should be labelled as inferred from its closest relative.
- For the band-winged Central Valley grasshopper, any crepitation (wing-snapping in flight) would be inference from the subfamily Oedipodinae; I did not find a source for it, so it stays a C.
- Brood VII's lost outliers can reuse the existing periodical-cicada synthesis with emergence years offset to 1899 / 1916 / 1933 as last-heard dates.

### Gaps
- IUCN Red List pages were not fetched directly (the Wikipedia list was the proxy); last-collection year and locality for *Conozoa hyalina* could not be confirmed (search quota exhausted; the fetch summary's "extinct by 1977" was not corroborated).
- No description of the Bermuda cicada's own song (e.g. in Verrill 1902) was found; no measured frequency for the lyric cicada proxy was retrieved.
- No dated last records for the prairie mole cricket in Illinois, Kentucky, Mississippi or Tennessee.
- Brood I and Brood V "Long Island populations", Brood XXII contraction, *Okanagana* losses, *Cicada orni* range change, tree-cricket range losses, wart-biter in Britain, and Japanese singing-insect losses: not researched (ran out of search quota). I found no reliable sources on these.
- St Helena giant earwig and Lord Howe stick insect are silent and were not pursued.
- The newforestcicada.info site returned an empty page; the 2014 survey PDF now returns 404, so its numbers come from a search snippet only.

## (b) Underwater and waterside: lost or collapsed fish choruses, runs and rookeries

### Takeaway
The strongest new underwater voice is the large yellow croaker of the East China Sea: a chorus people heard from shore and through bamboo tubes and boat planks, with named spawning grounds and a dated collapse (1974 onward), and living witnesses quoted. Close behind are Nassau grouper aggregations (more than 30 of at least 50 gone; sounds published), the totoaba and its Colorado-delta spawning ground, lake sturgeon "thunder" at Keshena Falls (Menominee; sacred; blocked 1892), and silenced salmon rivers with firm dates.

Ranking summary: A — large yellow croaker (Daiquyang / Guanjingyang); Nassau grouper aggregations (Cat Cay, Caye Glory); lake sturgeon at Keshena Falls (SENSITIVE). B — totoaba; Chinese bahaba; weakfish in Delaware Bay; Lake Victoria haplochromines; Atlantic salmon rivers (Thames 1833, Connecticut c.1798–1810, Lake Ontario 1898, Rhine 1950s); upper Columbia salmon above Grand Coulee (1939; SENSITIVE); kelp forests; Bermuda cahow colony (bird). C — Atlantic walrus of the Gulf of St Lawrence; dugong in China; Aral Sea fishery; Danube sturgeon above the Iron Gates.

### Cited Findings

#### A1. Large yellow croaker, *Larimichthys crocea* — the "gu-gu" spawning chorus of the East China Sea (rank A)
- What / status: "once an abundant commercial fish off East and South China"; population "collapsed in the 1970s due to overfishing"; IUCN Critically Endangered, "no recovery in the population" — [Wikipedia, Larimichthys crocea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Larimichthys_crocea). Severely depleted across its range since the 1980s and almost gone from landings — [PeerJ, Diversity of fish sound types in the Pearl River Estuary (search snippet)](https://peerj.com/articles/3924/).
- Heard from the shore: Chen Yuanxiang (born 1956 in a fishing village on Daishan, Zhoushan; former deputy director of the Ningbo marine and fisheries bureau) recalls that beside the Daiquyang, besides waves on the rocks, he could hear in the fourth and fifth lunar months the rising and falling calls of the croakers: "农历四五月间此起彼伏的黄鱼叫声，'咕咕咕咕'" — [The Paper (澎湃新闻), 《追鱼》：东海野生大黄鱼灭门记](https://www.thepaper.cn/newsDetail_forward_4618192). Wang Songzai (born 1939, Dayangshan) remembers hearing the "咕咕咕" of spawning croakers each year from Lixia to the Dragon Boat festival spring tides — [Tencent News / The Paper island anthropology series (search snippet)](https://news.qq.com/rain/a/20251111A03QBJ00).
- Listening as fishing technique: "用竹筒插入水中，能听到大黄鱼'咕咕'的叫声密匝匝逼来" (put a bamboo tube into the water and you hear the croakers' gu-gu pressing in thick and close); two boats, six men, cast the net where the calls are — Wu Yongshi, Nanji Island — [Zhejiang Daily, 10 June 2014](http://zjrb.zjol.com.cn/html/2014-06/10/content_2691386.htm?div=-1). In the wooden-sailboat era fishermen put an ear to the boat planks or pushed a bamboo pole into the water to judge the school's size, density, depth and direction — [Sina article (search snippet)](https://k.sina.cn/article_1887344341_707e96d502000jcct.html). A Ming-dynasty poem by Wang Tingfan (《蛇蟠洋》) has "清夜船头声聒耳，成群石首溯潮来" (in the clear night the noise at the bow is deafening; shoals of croakers come up on the tide); the calls at night are "声如婴儿" (like infants), keeping people awake — [Sanlian Lifeweek, 宁波鱼鲞 (search snippet)](http://ny.zdline.cn/h5/article/detailAuth.do?artId=24442&code=sdon&vcode=cb54234294b6a7154e03e191771edf1b).
- Still (or again) audible in one place: in the spawning season, staying overnight in Sandu'ao you may hear the croakers' "咕咕" from the sea, as fish reach the Guanjingyang spawning ground in May–June — [Guangming Online, June 2026 (search snippet)](https://difang.gmw.cn/fj/2026-06/15/content_38829951.htm). In January 2022 a boat from Shipu, Xiangshan netted over 2,000 kg of wild fish and the crew heard "咕咕咕" as the net came up — [The Paper (search snippet)](https://m.thepaper.cn/newsDetail_forward_16938357). So this is a collapse with a faint return, not an extinction.
- Dates and numbers: sound-driven "knocking" fishing (敲罟, qiao gu) reduced Guanjingyang production in 1963 to under 6% of 1956 — [Aquatic Living Resources 2020 (search snippet; page 403 on fetch)](https://www.alr-journal.org/articles/alr/full_html/2020/01/alr200022/alr200022.html). Qiao gu reached Zhejiang in June 1956, was banned provincially on 22 April 1958 and nationally by the State Council in March 1963 — [The Paper](https://www.thepaper.cn/newsDetail_forward_4618192). "In 1974, 2,000 pairs of fishing boats fished the central fishing ground of large yellow croaker in Zhejiang Province, and the output reached a record of 168,100 tons" (the overwintering ground; the stock never recovered) — [Frontiers in Marine Science 2021](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/marine-science/articles/10.3389/fmars.2021.743836/full). Zhejiang Daily gives 158,200 t in 1957, 168,100 t in 1974 and 190 t in 1993 — [Zhejiang Daily](http://zjrb.zjol.com.cn/html/2014-06/10/content_2691386.htm?div=-1). Sansha Bay was "once the largest spawning and nursery area" — [Frontiers 2021](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/marine-science/articles/10.3389/fmars.2021.743836/full).
- Season and place: fish enter Sandu'ao in batches late April to mid-June and spawn in Guanjingyang on spring tides mid-May to mid-June — [Baidu Baike English (search snippet)](https://baike.baidu.com/en/item/yellow%20croaker/1514422). In the 1960s tens of thousands of fishermen from Jiangsu, Zhejiang and Fujian gathered in Daiqu Ocean, Zhoushan, in the season — [China Daily Zhoushan (via search)](https://zhoushan.chinadaily.com.cn/2021-12/31/c_695575.htm).
- Coordinates: Daiquyang (between Daishan and Qushan islands) approx. 30.3 N, 122.3 E **(est.)**; Guanjingyang / Sansha Bay approx. 26.6 N, 119.8 E **(est.)**.
- Acoustic detail: main frequency band of cultured *L. crocea* sounds 550–800 Hz — [Frontiers in Marine Science 2023, citing Chen et al. 2021 (search snippet)](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/marine-science/articles/10.3389/fmars.2023.1131522/full); feeding and spawning sounds share "the same power spectrum and the peak frequencies at about 800 Hz" — [Journal of Dalian Fisheries University 2007 (search snippet)](https://xuebao.dlou.edu.cn/EN/abstract/abstract4114.shtml); calls "from the afternoon until just before midnight" — [JMSE 2025, small yellow croaker chorus paper (search snippet)](https://doi.org/10.3390/jmse13071380). Conflicting figure: a Korean-language study reported dominant frequency 38–142 Hz and mean pulse duration 280 ± 156 ms for "yellow croaker" — [Korea Science (via search)](https://koreascience.kr/article/JAKO200707341588335.pdf); species and method were not checked.
- Mechanism of qiao gu is itself acoustic: stunning or herding fish by percussion on the hull; one source says the otoliths resonate — [Frontiers 2021](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/marine-science/articles/10.3389/fmars.2021.743836/full); Liu Jiafu is reported as saying it was sound-driven herding, not otolith resonance — [The Paper](https://www.thepaper.cn/newsDetail_forward_4618192).
- Sensitivities: living fishing communities' memory; not sacred. Using named witnesses' words would need permission/attribution.

#### A2. Nassau grouper, *Epinephelus striatus* — lost spawning aggregations (rank A)
- Scale of loss: "More than 30 of at least 50 known aggregations across Nassau grouper's range have disappeared, including the one at Cat Cay"; in January 1971 a biologist documented "30,000 to 100,000" adults at Cat Cay, Bahamas — [NOAA Fisheries, "Saving Nassau grouper" (via search)](https://www.fisheries.noaa.gov/feature-story/saving-nassau-grouper-protecting-spawning-aggregations). Caye Glory, Belize: catches of over 30,000 fish/year in the 1960s from a site estimated at over 100,000 spawners; by 1986 fewer than 20,000 — [NOAA CoRIS / Belize flyer and GCFI documents (via search)](https://www.ncei.noaa.gov/data/oceans/coris/library/NOAA/CRCP/project/1395/SaveBelizeNassauGrouperFishery_2002Flyer.pdf). "an estimated third or more of spawning aggregations have disappeared"; Glover's Reef is "one of only two viable sites remaining ... of nine originally known locations" in Belize; aggregations form "in December and January, always around the time of the full moon, and always in the same locations" — [Wikipedia, Nassau grouper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nassau_grouper). In Puerto Rico "commercially extinct since the early 1980s", one known aggregation left at Bajo de Sico; listed threatened under the US ESA in 2016 — [GCFI proceedings / NOAA (via search)](https://proceedings.gcfi.org/wp-content/uploads/2018/10/GCFI_66-98.pdf).
- Sound (recorded): two sounds at aggregations in Belize (Feb 2011) and Puerto Rico (Feb 2012): "a pulse train sound thought to be associated with alarm or warning behavior, and a tonal sound associated with reproductive behaviors, including courtship displays" — [Schärer et al. 2012, Endangered Species Research 19:29–38 (via search; abstract page 401 on fetch)](https://int-res.com/abstracts/esr/v19/n1/p29-38). Recordings exist at [DOSITS audio gallery (403 on fetch)](https://dosits.org/galleries/audio-gallery/fishes/nassau-grouper).
- Where: Cat Cay, Bahamas approx. 25.55 N, 79.28 W **(est.)**; Caye Glory, Belize approx. 17.1 N, 88.0 W **(est.)**. Season: winter full moons.

#### A3. Lake sturgeon "thunder" at Keshena Falls, Wolf River, Wisconsin (rank A for place/date; SENSITIVE — sacred)
- Loss: "The first dam was built at Shawano in 1892, whereby the sturgeon could no longer reach reservation waters to spawn" at Keshena Falls — [Menominee sturgeon management presentation (via search)](https://www3.uwsp.edu/cnr-ap/UWEXLakes/Documents/programs/convention/2013/DonReiter-MenomineeLakeSturgeonManagement.pdf). The sturgeon is in the Menominee creation story and a clan symbol; annual ceremonies with prayer and tobacco; the fish dance was performed at Keshena Falls and "the sacred drum ... would once again call them home"; ceremonies and a feast resumed from the early 1990s with fish supplied by the state, now each April — [NOAA Voices / Dave Grignon and related sources (via search)](https://voices.nmfs.noaa.gov/dave-grignon); [NBC26 on returning nearly 100 sturgeon (via search)](https://www.nbc26.com/news/local-news/dnr-menominee-nation-return-nearly-100-sturgeon-to-wolf-river-ahead-of-feast-pow-wow).
- Sound (recorded): lake sturgeon "produce low-frequency sounds during spawning bouts; energy from these sounds can break the surface of the water occasionally and propagate harmonics in the terrestrial atmosphere, creating a faint drumming popularly known as 'sturgeon thunder'"; drumming fundamental 5–8 Hz in Wisconsin rivers — [Bocast et al., AFS 2014 abstract (via search)](https://afs.confex.com/afs/2014/webprogram/Paper15356.html). Detroit River drums show "average frequency peaks at 40 and 92 Hz" — [Dryad dataset, lake sturgeon calling in the Detroit River (via search)](https://datadryad.org/dataset/doi:10.5061/dryad.9kd51c5j1). Other sounds: "crisp crackles; sharp knocks or raps ... and squeaky whistles"; ~6 Hz, below human hearing — [UW–Madison Nelson Institute story (via search)](https://nelson.wisc.edu/news/story.php?story=2033).
- Where: Keshena Falls approx. 44.88 N, 88.63 W **(est.)**; season April–May (spawning run).
- Sensitivities: the drum that calls the sturgeon home is ceremonial. This should not be implemented without consulting the Menominee Indian Tribe of Wisconsin; the connection between "sturgeon thunder" and the drum is my reading, not a sourced Menominee statement.

#### B1. Totoaba, *Totoaba macdonaldi* — the largest croaker, Colorado River delta (rank B)
- "endemic to the Gulf of California"; spawns "in the mouth of the Colorado River Delta"; breeding January–May, peak March–April; up to 2 m and 135 kg, the largest drum; catch 2,000 t in 1943, 50 t in 1975 when Mexico banned the fishery; river flow to the delta since Hoover and Glen Canyon dams "only about 4% of the average flow during the period from 1910 to 1920"; poached for swim bladders — [Wikipedia, Totoaba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Totoaba) (Wikipedia lists it as IUCN Vulnerable; the brief's "critically endangered" reflects the older assessment).
- Sound (recorded in captivity only): Kino Bay, 29 April–4 May 2019; five call types: croaks; "knocks" (<0.03 s, <1 kHz, narrowband); longer broadband clicks with energy above 10–15 kHz; two others, one possibly an alarm call; call rate and type depend on age and sex — [Acoustical Society of America lay paper, Goldie Phillips](https://acoustics.org/2pab-sound-production-of-the-critically-endangered-totoaba-applying-underwater-sound-detection-to-fish-species-conservation-goldie-phillips/).
- Reference for what a delta croaker chorus is like (extant, same place): Gulf corvina aggregate in the Colorado delta each spring, more than 2 million fish over about 20 nights; sounds up to 192 dB, "the loudest sound ever recorded for a fish species"; the chorus "sounds like a crowd cheering at a stadium or perhaps a really loud beehive" — [ScienceDaily on Erisman & Rowell 2017](https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2017/06/170613101959.htm); [Phys.org (via search)](https://phys.org/news/2017-12-fish-sex-loud-deafen-dolphins.html). Rowell warned that over-harvest "could result in the functional extinction of the species" — [ScienceDaily](https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2017/06/170613101959.htm).
- Where: upper Gulf / delta mouth approx. 31.7 N, 114.7 W **(est.)**.
- Sensitivities: the delta is Cucapá (Cocopah) homeland and the corvina fishery is theirs; I did not research this and it should be checked before any text names the fishery.

#### B2. Chinese bahaba, *Bahaba taipingensis* (rank B)
- Range from the Yangtze estuary and Zhoushan south through the Min and Pearl River estuaries to Hong Kong and Macau; to 2 m and 100 kg; 50 t/yr in the 1930s, about 10 t/yr by the 1950s–60s; "enters estuaries to spawn and was historically seasonally abundant"; Critically Endangered; Class I protected in China since 2021 — [Wikipedia, Chinese bahaba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_bahaba).
- Sound: a paper exists, "Vocalization Behavior of Chinese Bahaba (*Bahaba taipingensis*) during the Reproduction Season" — [JMSE 2023, 11(4):712 (title via search; 403 on fetch)](https://www.mdpi.com/2077-1312/11/4/712). Parameters not retrieved.
- Where: Pearl River estuary approx. 22.3 N, 113.7 E **(est.)**.

#### B3. Weakfish, *Cynoscion regalis* — Delaware Bay spawning "purr" (rank B; collapsed, not lost)
- Biomass "reaching an all-time low of 342,990 pounds in 2011 (compared to 45.6 million pounds in 1981)"; the number using Delaware Bay as principal spawning area "declined dramatically in 1989" — [ASMFC weakfish page and New Jersey documents (via search)](https://asmfc.org/species/weakfish/).
- Sound: males "purr" during courtship; "Each call includes a series of 6-10 sound pulses, and each pulse expresses a damped, 2-3 cycle acoustic waveform"; with rising temperature, SPL, dominant frequency and repetition rate rise and pulse duration falls — [Connaughton et al., J. Exp. Biol. 205:2183 (via search)](https://cob.silverchair.com/jeb/article/205/15/2183/8985/Weakfish-sonic-muscle-influence-of-size); [ASA press room (via search)](https://acoustics.org/pressroom/httpdocs/135th/luczkovi.htm).
- Where: Delaware Bay approx. 39.1 N, 75.2 W **(est.)**.

#### B4. Lake Victoria haplochromine cichlids (rank B)
- "during the years 1984–1987 Lake Victoria ... experienced what is probably the largest mass extinction of contemporary vertebrates, with about 200 endemic species of haplochromine cichlids disappearing within a decade" — [Wikipedia, Haplochromis / Haplochromine (via search)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haplochromine).
- Sound: males of Lake Victoria cichlids produce species-specific sounds in aggression and courtship; fish size correlates with peak frequency; playback affects female preference — [Verzijden et al. 2010, Behavioral Ecology 21:548–555 (via search)](https://ideas.repec.org/a/oup/beheco/v21y2010i3p548-555.html). Generic cichlid courtship "growls": peak frequencies < 720 Hz, pulse duration 9–12 ms, pulse period 60–70 ms, sound duration 500–700 ms — [search summary of cichlid acoustics literature; primary source not pinned down](https://tfhmagazine.com/articles/freshwater/can-you-hear-me-now-sound-production-in-cichlid-courtship-and-speciation).
- Where: Lake Victoria, centre approx. 1.0 S, 33.0 E **(est.)**. These are quiet, close-range sounds; a "chorus" would be artistic licence.

#### B5. Silenced salmon rivers (rank B for dates/places; the sound itself is inferred)
- Thames: "The last Thames salmon of which there was a record was taken in June, 1833"; virtually absent by 1820 because of weirs and locks, then pollution — [Essex Field Club archive (via search)](https://www.essexfieldclub.org.uk/archivetext.php/s/046/o/0103); [Gresham College lecture (via search)](https://www.gresham.ac.uk/node/11670).
- Rhine: "totally extinguished by the end of the 1950s" (also Elbe, Weser) — [NASCO, Germany implementation plan (via search)](https://nasco.int/wp-content/uploads/2020/02/IP_Germany.pdf).
- Connecticut River: dam completed at Peskeomskut (Turners Falls) in 1798; "by 1800, the Connecticut River strain of Atlantic salmon was effectively dead"; native runs lost by 1810 — [Hitchcock Center, "Salmon story, part 2" (via search)](https://www.hitchcockcenter.org/?p=21818); [EBSCO Research Starters (via search)](https://www.ebsco.com/research-starters/zoology/atlantic-salmon-return-connecticut-river). Turners Falls approx. 42.61 N, 72.55 W **(est.)**. Peskeomskut is also the site of the 1676 massacre of Native people — sensitive; I did not research this and it should be checked.
- Lake Ontario: "the world's greatest freshwater population of Atlantic salmon"; "By 1898, the fish had officially vanished from the lake" — [Nature Conservancy of Canada (via search)](https://natureconservancy.ca/en/blog/archive/atlantic-salmon-lake.html).
- Upper Columbia: Grand Coulee Dam "would forever block anadromous fish runs to the 1,140 linear miles of upstream spawning grounds"; runs stopped in 1939; a 64-pound salmon was photographed on the Spokane River in spring 1937 — [NPS, Lake Roosevelt administrative history (via search)](https://www.nps.gov/parkhistory/online_books/laro/adhi/adhi10a.htm); [Northwest Power and Conservation Council, June Hogs (via search)](https://www.nwcouncil.org/history/JuneHogs/). Tribes and federal agencies are now working on reintroduction — [Washington State Standard, 20 May 2024 (via search)](https://washingtonstatestandard.com/2024/05/20/northwest-tribes-feds-dive-into-work-on-salmon-revival-in-upper-columbia-river/). See Kettle Falls in section (c).
- Klamath (a return, not a loss): Chinook began migrating on 3 October 2024 above the former Iron Gate dam, for the first time in more than 60 years there and about a century further up — [ABC News / AP (via search)](https://abcnews.go.com/US/wireStory/salmon-swim-freely-klamath-river-1st-time-century-114571347). Elwha: dam removal began 17 September 2011 — [Earthjustice (via search)](https://earthjustice.org/article/elwha-dam-removal-brings-hope-to-northwest-salmon).

#### B6. Kelp forests — new numbers for the already-catalogued reef soundscapes (rank B)
- "More than 95 percent of eastern Tasmania's kelp forests are gone" — [Yale Environment 360 (via search)](https://e360.yale.edu/features/as-oceans-warm-the-worlds-giant-kelp-forests-begin-to-disappear). Northern California bull kelp: "greater than 95% loss in kelp canopy from 2014 to 2019" — [California Ocean Protection Council Kelp Action Plan (via search)](https://www.opc.ca.gov/webmaster/ftp/pdf/agenda_items/20210216/Item7_KelpActionPlan_ExhibitA_FINAL.pdf).
- Soundscape: in Channel Islands kelp forests, urchin density, kelp cover and fish diversity were related to soundscape features; snapping-shrimp band 2.5–7.5 kHz; "Structurally complex kelp forests are quieter, likely due to attenuation of external noise" — [Gottesman et al. 2020, Marine Ecology Progress Series 654:35–52 (via search)](https://int-res.com/abstracts/meps/v654/p35-52). Note the search summary contradicted itself about whether shrimp snap rate rises or falls with kelp cover; check the paper before using direction of effect.

#### B7. Bermuda petrel (cahow) — a vanished seabird-colony night chorus (rank B; a bird, probably another researcher's remit)
- The name comes from "its eerie cries"; Spanish sailors in the 1500s "thought the bird's eerie, nighttime courtship calls were the shrieks of devils", hence "Isle of Devils"; perhaps half a million birds; "By the 1620s, the cahow had gone extinct" (as then believed); 17 pairs refound in 1951 on islets in Castle Harbour — [National Wildlife Federation (via search)](https://www.nwf.org/Magazines/National-Wildlife/2013/FebMarch/Conservation/Bermuda-Petrels); [Nonsuch Island cahow history (via search)](https://nonsuchisland.com/cahow-history). Species survives and is recovering; the island-wide colony is what was lost.

#### C1. Atlantic walrus of the Gulf of St Lawrence and Sable Island (rank C for sound)
- "formerly, when undisturbed by the Americans, to the amount of seven or eight thousand" hauled out on the Magdalen Islands' échoueries, slopes "about 80 or 100 yards wide at the water side" — Molineux Shuldham, 1775, Philosophical Transactions — [transcription](https://jimclifford.ca/philosophical-transactions-ocr-1665-1869/articles/jstor-106192/). Shuldham says nothing about their noise (checked).
- Oral tradition: last walrus seen on the Magdalen Islands in 1799; hunting reported "completely destroyed" in 1798 — [micami.ca (via search)](https://micami.ca/once-upon-an-island/locale/en.txt). Sable Island: disappeared "by the late 1800s" according to [Parks Canada](https://parks.canada.ca/pn-np/ns/sable/nature/animaux-animals/walrus) (this date looks late to me; verify).
- Sound: modern haul-out recordings exist — [USGS audio, walruses hauled out, NW Alaska](https://www.usgs.gov/media/audio/walruses-hauled-out-northwest-coast-alaska). Magdalen Islands approx. 47.4 N, 61.8 W **(est.)**; Sable Island approx. 43.93 N, 59.9 W **(est.)**.
- Sensitivity: Mi'kmaq relationship to these waters not researched.

#### C2. Dugong in Chinese waters (rank C for sound)
- "functionally extinct" in China, no sighting since 2008 — [ZSL (via search)](https://zsl.org/news-and-events/news/gentle-giants-sea-now-extinct-china); [Turvey et al. 2022, Royal Society Open Science (UCL copy, via search)](https://discovery.ucl.ac.uk/id/eprint/10152877/). Last seen in Taiwan in 1986 (dead animal, Liuqiu Island); also gone from Mauritius, the Maldives and Lakshadweep — same sources and [CMS dugong documents (via search)](https://www.cms.int/sites/default/files/document/10.2305_IUCN.UK_.2019-3.RLTS_.T157011948A157011982.en_.pdf). No acoustic source was retrieved.

#### C3. Aral Sea fishery; Danube sturgeon (rank C)
- Aral: fishery employed about 40,000 and produced one-sixth of the Soviet catch; by 1987 commercial harvest had ended; 20 native fish species could not survive — [Wikipedia, Aral Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aral_Sea). (The sea itself is in section c.)
- Danube: Iron Gate I (1972) "permanently interrupted" spawning routes of several sturgeon species — [Wikipedia, Iron Gates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iron_Gates). No sturgeon sound source for the Danube species was found.

### Inferences
- The yellow croaker is the natural companion to the existing "cod spawning, through the hull": same listening posture (ear to the planks, or a bamboo tube), a different sea, and a human testimony dense enough to quote. Synthesis guidance from the sources: pulsed "gu-gu" calls with energy peaking roughly 550–800 Hz, dense overlapping chorus, afternoon to just before midnight, spring tides of the 4th–5th lunar months (roughly May–June) at Daiquyang and Guanjingyang. Because the fish is faintly returning, the label should say collapsed, not extinct.
- Nassau grouper tonal courtship sounds and pulse trains at winter full moons give a Caribbean counterpart; place at Cat Cay and Caye Glory with last-heard dates in the 1970s–80s (the exact year each aggregation ended was not found).
- For totoaba, only captive call types are documented; a delta spawning chorus is inferred from its relative the Gulf corvina. Label as inferred.
- Sturgeon thunder at 5–8 Hz is infrasonic; in a browser it would have to be rendered as its audible harmonics, surface drumming and "knocks, crackles, whistles". That is consistent with the source's own wording about harmonics breaking the surface.
- Salmon rivers have no published "run acoustics" in what I found; the sound (leaping, splashing, falls) is inferred, so they rank B only for place/date. They pair naturally with the drowned falls in section (c).

### Gaps
- No English-language scholarly source was found for the yellow-croaker hull-listening practice (Chinese journalism and oral testimony only); Liu & Sadovy de Mitcheson 2008 (Fish and Fisheries) was not retrieved. One fetch summary of The Paper article gave a 1974 removal of "25–30+ million tons", which is implausible and was discarded.
- Measured parameters for Nassau grouper sounds (Hz, durations), for Chinese bahaba calls, for goliath grouper beyond "dominant frequencies around 60 Hz, 50–100 Hz pulses, most frequent 01:00–03:00" ([Mann et al. 2009, ESR 7:229–236, via search](https://int-res.com/abstracts/esr/v7/n3/p229-236)) and frequencies of the corvina chorus were not retrieved; DOSITS pages returned 403. Goliath grouper is not a loss case in what I found (no lost-aggregation dates).
- Year of extirpation for each named Nassau grouper aggregation (Cat Cay, Caye Glory, Bermuda, Mahahual) not found.
- Not researched for lack of quota: Atlantic croaker and black drum declines, haddock, European sturgeon river dates, river herring/alewife/shad runs, eulachon, Yangtze sturgeon and Reeves shad, desert pupfish, lost seabird-colony seas other than the cahow, lost fur-seal and sea-lion rookeries. I found no reliable sources on these.
- No historical description of walrus herd noise in the Gulf of St Lawrence was found.

## (c) Collective and non-animal soundscapes lost since ~1500

### Takeaway
Drowned waterfalls are the richest new category: Guaíra Falls (1982; roar heard 30 km), Celilo Falls (1957; "Wy-am", echo of falling water; elders' accounts of the day the sound died) and Kettle Falls (1940; "Keep Sounding Water") have exact coordinates, exact dates and described sound, and all three need Indigenous consultation. Dead glaciers have dates and coordinates but only inferred sound; Lake Suwa's omiwatari "ice thunder" has a record back to 1443 and a Shinto dimension; the springbok treks have a sound quote and a firm last date (1896).

Ranking summary: A — Guaíra Falls; Celilo Falls (SENSITIVE); Kettle Falls (SENSITIVE); Lake Suwa omiwatari (SENSITIVE); springbok trekbokke; Dnieper Rapids. B — Laufenburg rapids; Nile Second Cataract; Cataract Canyon rapids (returning); Kariba Gorge (SENSITIVE); Three Gorges shoals; Ripon Falls; Mesopotamian marshes (SENSITIVE); Colorado delta; Tulare Lake (SENSITIVE); Whittlesey Mere and the Fens; Kankakee marsh; Everglades rookeries; Japan's singing-sand beaches; George River caribou (SENSITIVE); Kalahari wildebeest; spring dawn chorus; pre-engine quiet on land and in the sea. C — dead glaciers (Okjökull, Pizol, Humboldt, Anderson, Clark, Ayoloco, Sarenne, Chacaltaya); Lake Poopó (SENSITIVE); Aral Sea; Owens Lake; Hula; Arctic sea-ice soundscape.

### Cited Findings

#### Drowned waterfalls and rapids

**A1. Guaíra Falls / Sete Quedas, Paraná River, Brazil–Paraguay (rank A)**
- 24°04′23″S 54°17′01″W; 19 falls in 7 groups; total height about 114 m; river narrowed from 380 m to 60 m; flow estimates 13,000–50,000 m³/s; "The roar of the plunging water could be heard from 30 km (20 mi) away"; drowned in 1982 by the Itaipu reservoir in 14 days; a footbridge collapsed on 17 January 1982 "killing dozens of tourists"; Carlos Drummond de Andrade wrote a farewell poem; before submergence Indigenous people performed a quarup, "an indigenous ritual of remembrance for the dead, in memory of the falls" — [Wikipedia, Guaíra Falls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gua%C3%ADra_Falls).
- Sensitivities: the area is Avá-Guarani territory and the quarup shows the loss was mourned ritually; consultation appropriate (Guarani relationship not researched beyond this).

**A2. Celilo Falls (Wy-am), Columbia River, Oregon–Washington (rank A; SENSITIVE — consult)**
- 45°38′58″N 120°58′41″W; three sections (Horseshoe/Tumwater Falls, the Cul-de-Sac, the main channel); about 20 ft drop at low water, drowned out in spring freshet; river narrowed to 140 ft; average flow about 190,000 cfs, floods to 1,240,000 cfs; "sixth-largest by volume in the world"; "On March 10, 1957, hundreds of observers looked on as a rising Lake Celilo rapidly silenced the falls"; a 2008 sonar survey showed the falls "remain intact below the artificial lake" — [Wikipedia, Celilo Falls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Celilo_Falls).
- Name and sound: "Wy-am" means "echo of falling water" or "sound of water upon the rocks"; the roar was reportedly audible up to 10 miles away; four tribes fished there (Yakama, Nez Perce, Warm Springs, Umatilla); as many as 5,000 people gathered; "Celilo still reverberates in the heart of every Native American who ever fished or lived by it. They can still hear its roar" — Ted Strong (Yakama); "the spirit of Wy-am still lives in the traditions and religions" of the tribes — [Columbia River Inter-Tribal Fish Commission](https://critfc.org/salmon-culture/tribal-salmon-culture/celilo-falls/).
- The day it stopped: "When the falls died, the sound died. You could just hear the wind and the sound of people crying. They wailed" — Thomas Morning Owl; "like a freight train. It was really loud" — Wilfred Yallup as recounted by Dorothy Cyr — [East Oregonian, 9 June 2017, via Save Our Wild Salmon](https://www.wildsalmon.org/news-and-media/news/east-oregonian-progress-silences-celilo-falls-stories-keep-its-memory-alive.html).
- Sensitivities: a sacred and grieved place; the sound itself is what was taken. Do not implement without asking CRITFC and the four treaty tribes (and the Celilo Village / Wy-am community). The falls physically survive under the reservoir.

**A3. Kettle Falls (Shonitkwu), upper Columbia, Washington (rank A; SENSITIVE — consult)**
- 48°38′22″N 118°06′34″W; "a series of rapids and cascades", nearly 50 ft (15 m) drop over quartzite; "the sound of the falls could be heard for miles away"; Salish names Shonitkwu, "roaring or noisy waters", and Schwenetekoo, "Keep Sounding Water"; flooded in 1940 by Lake Roosevelt behind Grand Coulee Dam; the Ceremony of Tears in June 1940 drew 8,000–10,000 people from the Colville, Yakama, Spokane, Nez Perce, Flathead, Blackfeet, Coeur d'Alene, Tulalip and Pend d'Oreilles; a Jesuit in 1841 "found native people catching up to 3,000 fish a day"; partly re-exposed in drawdowns of 1969 and 1974 — [Wikipedia, Kettle Falls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kettle_Falls).
- Sensitivities: consult the Confederated Tribes of the Colville Reservation and the Spokane Tribe.

**A4. Dnieper Rapids, Ukraine (rank A)**
- 48°11′00″N 35°11′20″E, between Dnipro and Zaporizhzhia; nine major rapids plus 30–40 smaller and about 60 islands; 50 m drop over 66 km; the largest, Nenasytetskyi (also "Revuchyi", the roarer, or "Peklo", hell), 2.4 km long: "Its roaring could be heard several kilometers away"; "inundated in 1932" by the Dnieper Hydroelectric Station — [Wikipedia, Dnieper Rapids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dnieper_Rapids).
- Sensitivities: active war zone; Cossack heritage site. Handle with care in wording.

**B1. Laufenburg rapids ("Kleiner Laufen"), High Rhine, Switzerland–Germany (rank B)**
- "To build the power station, the rapids of Laufenburg—known as the smaller Laufen—were blown up"; power station built 1908/09–1914; the blasting and flooding of "the widely known rapids" caused "a heated debate about technical interventions in the landscape" — [Swiss Spectator (via search)](https://www.swiss-spectator.ch/?p=81818); [Canton Aargau heritage inventory, Kraftwerk Laufenburg 1908–1914 (via search)](https://www.ag.ch/denkmalpflege/suche/detail.aspx?id=2542). Approx. 47.56 N, 8.06 E **(est.)**. No sound description retrieved.

**B2. Cataracts of the Nile (rank B)**
- First Cataract 24.0354°N 32.8721°E, altered since the 1902 Aswan Low Dam; Second (Great) Cataract 21.8115°N 31.2159°E, 10 km south of old Wadi Halfa, "now underwater in Lake Nasser"; Fourth Cataract 18.91°N 32.36°E, submerged since 2008 by the Merowe Dam — [Wikipedia, Cataracts of the Nile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cataracts_of_the_Nile). Meinarti island at the foot of the Second Cataract was inundated in 1965 — [BAR Publishing, Meinarti I (via search)](https://barpublishing.com/meinarti-i.html).
- Sensitivities: Nubian displacement (Wadi Halfa; Manasir at the Fourth Cataract). No sound quote retrieved; Amelia Edwards' 1888 chapter on the Second Cataract is online at [Heidelberg University Library](https://digi.ub.uni-heidelberg.de/diglit/edwards1888/0301) and may contain one (not read).

**B3. Cataract Canyon rapids, Colorado River, Utah (rank B; returning)**
- Glen Canyon Dam (1963) "wiped out nearly half of Cataract Canyon's 50 rapids", inundating 25 of 41 miles; since the reservoir began dropping in 2002 the river has cut through the sediment and "eight of the river's rapids have reappeared"; the Returning Rapids Project tracks how many of the 26 lost rapids come back — [NPCA, "Wild River, Ghost Lake" (via search)](https://www.npca.org/articles/11224-wild-river-ghost-lake); [Sierra magazine (via search)](https://sierraclub.org/sierra/2021-4-fall/field-trip/rapid-returns-utah-lake-powell). Approx. 37.9 N, 110.2 W **(est.)**. A lost sound that is audibly coming back.

**B4. Kariba Gorge, Zambezi (rank B; SENSITIVE)**
- Lake Kariba filled 1958–1963; about 57,000 people displaced; Nyami Nyami, the Zambezi river god of the Tonga, lives in the Kariba Gorge with his wife; the dam is believed to have separated them; the January 1958 flood is attributed to his anger — [Wikipedia, Nyami Nyami (via search)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nyami_Nyami). Approx. 16.52 S, 28.76 E **(est.)**. Consult Tonga representatives; no sound description retrieved.

**B5. Three Gorges shoals, Yangtze (rank B)**
- The Kongling, Xietan and Qingtan/Xintan shoals in Xiling Gorge were the most dangerous in the gorges ("Death Passage"); "Sometimes over 100 trackers were needed to literally pull boats up the rapids"; the reservoir began filling in June 2003, 660 km long — [Wikipedia, Xiling Gorge (via search)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xiling_Gorge); [Probe International on Yangtze tow-men (via search)](https://journal.probeinternational.org/2008/09/04/yangtze-river-tow-men/). Approx. 30.9 N, 110.8 E **(est.)**. (The trackers' work songs are human sound and out of scope.)

**B6. Ripon Falls, Victoria Nile, Uganda (rank B)**
- At Jinja, the outlet of Lake Victoria; 5 m high, 275 m wide; submerged on completion of the Owen Falls Dam in 1954 — [Wikipedia, Ripon Falls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ripon_Falls). Approx. 0.42 N, 33.19 E **(est.)**.

**Iron Gates, Danube**: cataract stretch ("Kataraktenstrecke") with the Prigrada and Greben rocks; Iron Gate I dam 1972; Ada Kaleh island submerged — 44°40′16″N 22°31′47″E — [Wikipedia, Iron Gates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iron_Gates).

#### Ice

**A5. Lake Suwa omiwatari ("the gods' crossing"), Nagano, Japan (rank A; SENSITIVE)**
- 36°02′54″N 138°05′03″E; pressure ridges across the frozen lake; in Shinto belief the path of Takeminakata-no-kami crossing to the goddess Yasakatome; records from 1443; the last omiwatari was 2018, "only a few inches high"; the lake "had failed to freeze over in 18 of the past 25 years" — [Wikipedia, Lake Suwa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Suwa). The priest of Yatsurugi Shrine leads the annual watch; reporting in early 2025–26 describes the absence since 2018 as approaching or tying the record gap of 1507–1514 — [search results incl. York University and Irish News coverage](https://www.yorku.ca/yfile/?p=330690).
- Sound: ridges "crack through the surface accompanied by a loud 'ice thunder'" — [Japan National Tourism Organization blog (via search)](https://www.japan.travel/en/sg/jbyj-blog/omiwatari). "crackles, whizzes and pings break in on soothing melodious tones, while distant sounds create images of a thunder board and a caged lion" — [Japan Experience (via search)](https://www.japan-experience.com/plan-your-trip/to-know/understanding-japan/omi-watari). Formation happens with night-time contraction and daytime expansion — same sources.
- Climate record: freeze dates 1443–2014 for Suwa and breakup dates 1693–2013 for the Torne River; before the Industrial Revolution Suwa froze over 99% of the time, "more recently ... only half the time"; freeze date shifted from about 0.19 days/decade to about 4.6 days/decade later — [Sharma et al. 2016, Scientific Reports, via USGS](https://www.usgs.gov/publications/direct-observations-ice-seasonality-reveal-changes-climate-over-past-320-570-years); [Smithsonian (via search)](https://www.smithsonianmag.com/smart-news/japanese-priests-collected-almost-seven-centuries-climate-data-180958929/).
- Sensitivities: a living religious observance of Suwa Taisha / Yatsurugi Shrine; ask before representing the god's crossing; the plain "lake ice" can be done without the ritual framing.
- General lake-ice acoustics: thermal cracks launch flexural waves; high frequencies travel faster in the ice plate, so a listener hears a falling "pew" chirp (acoustic dispersion); booms from thermal expansion and contraction — [Live Science (via search)](https://livescience.com/48309-acoustics-of-ice-skipping-rocks.html); [The Kid Should See This (via search)](https://thekidshouldseethis.com/post/why-does-a-frozen-lake-sound-like-a-star-wars-blaster).

**C1. Arctic sea-ice soundscape (rank C)**
- Under ice, "low-frequency ice noise below 100 Hz is created by wind-driven collisions between ice chunks, while noises between 100 and 600 Hz are caused by ridging, shearing, cracking"; ice-covered Arctic waters can have some of the lowest ambient levels in the ocean; loss of ice makes the open-water season louder with wind and waves; modelled shipping noise rose 5–20 dB across the pan-Arctic from 2013 to 2019 — [NOAA Arctic Report Card 2021, Marine Soundscape (via search)](https://library.oarcloud.noaa.gov/noaa_documents.lib/OAR/GOMO/Arctic_Report_Card/ARC21_Marine_Soundscape.pdf); [DOSITS, Noise field in the Arctic (via search)](https://dosits.org/?p=23394).

#### Dead glaciers (rank C for sound: dates and places are firm, the sound is inferred)

- Okjökull, Iceland: 64°35′53″N 20°52′52″W; 15 km² early 20th century, 0.7 km² in 2015; declared dead in 2014 by glaciologist Oddur Sigurðsson (ice thinner than the 40–50 m needed to flow); plaque "A letter to the future" by Andri Snær Magnason installed 18 August 2019; 415 ppm CO₂ — [Wikipedia, Okjökull](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Okj%C3%B6kull). (Home-ground relevance for an Iceland-based artist.)
- Pizol, Switzerland: funeral 22 September 2019; about 2,700 m; lost 80–90% of volume since 2006, leaving 26,000 m² — [NPR/WGBH (via search)](https://www.wgbh.org/news/international-news/2019-09-22/hundreds-attend-funeral-for-pizol-a-disappearing-glacier); [Wikipedia, Pizol (via search)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pizol). Approx. 46.96 N, 9.39 E **(est.)**.
- Humboldt (La Corona), Venezuela: 8.5501°N 70.9981°W; about 3 km² in 1910, 0.1 km² in 2015, 0.01 km² in 2024, "too small to flow"; "Venezuela is now the first postglacial nation in the Andes" — [NASA Earth Observatory](https://science.nasa.gov/earth/earth-observatory/humboldt-glaciers-demise-152893).
- Anderson Glacier, Olympic Mountains, Washington: 47°42′48″N 123°20′06″W — [Wikipedia, Anderson Glacier (via search)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anderson_Glacier); disappearance dated 2015 by the Global Glacier Casualty List — [University of Zurich news (via search)](https://www.geo.uzh.ch/en/news/2024/global-glacier-casualty-list.html) — but "by 2009 had disappeared" according to [From a Glacier's Perspective (via search)](https://glacierchange.blog/tag/anderson-glacier-loss/). Dates conflict.
- Clark Glacier, South Sister, Oregon: declared dead summer 2020; funeral 18 October 2020 by the Oregon Glaciers Institute — [Columbia Climate School, State of the Planet (via search)](https://news.climate.columbia.edu/2020/10/27/clark-glacier-funeral-oregon/). Approx. 44.09 N, 121.78 W **(est.)**.
- Ayoloco, Iztaccíhuatl, Mexico: declared gone 2018; plaque placed 2021 at 4,626 m by UNAM — [Gaceta UNAM (via search)](https://www.gaceta.unam.mx/coloca-la-unam-placa-en-memoria-del-ayoloco/). Approx. 19.18 N, 98.64 W **(est.)**. The name is Nahuatl ("heart of water" per [Atalayar (via search)](https://www.atalayar.com/en/articulo/society/ayoloco-heart-water-has-stopped-beating-mexico/20210608141322151575.html)).
- Sarenne, Alpe d'Huez, France: declassified 16 October 2023 after 75 years of observation — [Argia (via search)](https://argia.eus/en/albistea/alpe-dhuez-glaziarra). Approx. 45.12 N, 6.13 E **(est.)**.
- Chacaltaya, Bolivia: 16°21′12″S 68°07′53″W; up to 18,000 years old; 0.22 km² in 1940, 0.01 km² in 2007, "completely gone by 2009" — [Wikipedia, Chacaltaya (via search)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chacaltaya).
- Also listed as disappeared: Martial Sur, Argentina (2018); Basòdino, Switzerland (46°24′57″N 8°28′33″E) had a commemoration in 2021 but is not yet gone — [UZH on the Global Glacier Casualty List (via search)](https://www.geo.uzh.ch/en/news/2024/global-glacier-casualty-list.html); [Wikipedia, Basòdino Glacier (via search)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bas%C3%B2dino_Glacier). The list itself is at [glaciercasualtylist.rice.edu](https://glaciercasualtylist.rice.edu) (certificate error on fetch).
- Published glacier acoustics (tidewater glaciers, not these small mountain glaciers): Icy Bay, Alaska has an annual average 120 dB re 1 µPa with a broad peak at 1,000–3,000 Hz from bubbles released by melting ice — louder than nearly all measured natural ocean environments — [Pettit et al. 2015, Geophysical Research Letters 42:2309–2316, via USGS](https://pubs.usgs.gov/publication/70142330). A calving event at Meares Glacier "begins with an infrasound rumble (1–20 Hz), then the ice fractures (20–100 Hz), falls and impacts the water (200–600 Hz)", then >10 kHz spray and turbulence — [Journal of Glaciology / Annals of Glaciology paper (via search)](https://www.cambridge.org/core/product/3B220B63A9155BEEC5F577227A53F4F2/core-reader).

#### Lost lakes, deltas and wetlands

- **Mesopotamian Marshes, Iraq/Iran (rank B; SENSITIVE)**: 31°00′N 47°00′E; "Before 2003, the marshes were drained to 10% of their original size"; 58% re-flooded by late 2006, back to around 30% by 2009; Marsh Arabs (Maʻdān) fell from about 500,000 in the 1950s to about 20,000; Basra reed warbler, marbled teal, smooth-coated otter; UNESCO World Heritage 2016 — [Wikipedia, Mesopotamian Marshes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mesopotamian_Marshes) (the page's area figures are internally inconsistent). Sound description: "Stars reflected in the dark water, the croaking of frogs, canoes coming home in the evening, peace and continuity, the stillness of a world that never knew an engine" — Wilfred Thesiger, *The Marsh Arabs* (1964), quoted in [Halfman Halfbook review (via search)](https://halfmanhalfbook.co.uk/review/the-marsh-arabs-by-wilfred-thesiger/). Sensitivity: the drainage was an act of persecution; consult or at least word carefully.
- **Colorado River delta, Mexico (rank B)**: Aldo Leopold, 1922: "the river was nowhere and everywhere"; "a huge 'chimney' of cranes, wheeling high in the sky"; "Fleets of cormorants drove their black prows in quest of skittering mullets"; the delta is now about ten percent of the nearly two million acres of wetlands he saw; Morelos Dam 1950; Glen Canyon Dam completed 13 September 1963 — [NASA Earth Observatory, "Green Lagoons No More" (via search)](https://earthobservatory.nasa.gov/images/146839/green-lagoons-no-more); [Places Journal, "Nowhere and Everywhere" (via search)](https://placesjournal.org/article/nowhere-and-everywhere-the-landscape-of-the-colorado-delta/). Cucapá homeland (not researched).
- **Tulare Lake (Pa'ashi), California (rank B; SENSITIVE)**: 36.05°N 119.788°W; 690 sq mi (1,780 km²) at full; "By 1899, the lake was dry except for residual wetlands"; Tachi Yokuts name Pa'ashi, "big water"; Tachi, Wowol and Chunut nations; pelicans, cormorants, sandhill cranes, tule elk; returned in 1983, 1997 and 2023 (over 114,000 acres) — [Wikipedia, Tulare Lake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tulare_Lake). Consult the Tachi Yokut Tribe; the 2023 return was received by them as a homecoming (my recollection; verify).
- **Whittlesey Mere and the Fens, England (rank B)**: "once the largest lake in lowland England", about 1,570 acres; drained 1851 with Appold's pump from the Great Exhibition, empty by 1852; "over half the wildlife recorded in the area became extinct here" — [Great Fen (via search)](https://greatfen.org.uk/node/81); [Wikipedia, Whittlesey Mere (via search)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Whittlesey_Mere). Related fen voice: the northern pool frog's last English colony at Thompson Common, Norfolk, "officially extinct by 1995"; its mating calls were part of the evidence that it was native; reintroduced from Sweden 2005–2008 and to Thompson Common in 2015 — [Amphibian and Reptile Conservation Trust (via search)](https://www.arc-trust.org/pool-frog). Bittern boom for reference: 2–6 foghorn-like booms carrying "well over a mile" — [Wild Ambience (via search)](https://wildambience.com/wildlife-sounds/eurasian-bittern/).
- **Grand Kankakee Marsh, Indiana–Illinois (rank B)**: 500,000 acres (some sources say a million); dredging from 1902; by 1917 the river in Indiana was straight ditches for 82 miles; in 1922 the last bend was bypassed, "turning its 250 miles of meandering river into a 90-mile-long ditch"; "Everglades of the North"; "flocks of waterfowl darkening the skies" — [Earth Island Journal (via search)](https://earthisland.org/journal/index.php/articles/entry/northern-indianas-lost-marsh-grand-kankakee); [Wikipedia, Kankakee Outwash Plain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kankakee_Outwash_Plain). Approx. 41.2 N, 87.0 W **(est.)**. Potawatomi homeland (not researched). The claim that drainage removed "one-fifth of the migratory bird population in the United States" appears in popular sources only; treat as unverified.
- **Everglades wading-bird rookeries (rank B)**: wading birds down nearly 90% by the 1980s; white ibis nests down 87% and wood stork 78% since the 1930s; in the 1930s–40s about 90% of nesting was in the southern mangrove estuaries; recent wet years have exceeded 100,000 nests — [Audubon Florida (via search)](https://fl.audubon.org/sites/default/files/audubon_everglades_wadingbirds_jan2013.pdf); [Audubon magazine (via search)](https://www.audubon.org/magazine/flocks-flamingos-returned-rejuvenated-everglades-are-they-back-good). Miccosukee and Seminole homeland (not researched).
- **Hula Lake and swamps, Israel (rank C)**: drained 1951–1958; 119 animal species lost to the region, 37 lost from Israel; peat fires; Agamon HaHula re-flooded 1994; Hula painted frog refound 2011 — [Wikipedia, Hula Valley (via search)](https://www.wikipedia.com/wiki/Lake_Hula). Approx. 33.07 N, 35.60 E **(est.)**.
- **Lake Poopó, Bolivia (rank C; SENSITIVE)**: 18°33′S 67°05′W, 3,686 m; about 1,000 km² (3,500 km² at 1986 maximum); "By December 2015, the lake had completely dried up"; disaster zone declared 20 January 2016; 34 aquatic bird species including three flamingos; recovery "considered unlikely" — [Wikipedia, Lake Poopó](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Poop%C3%B3). The Uru-Murato ("people of the lake") lost their livelihood (named in the brief; not confirmed in the fetched page) — consult.
- **Aral Sea (rank C)**: 45°N 60°E; 68,000 km² in 1960; split 1987; about 10% by 2007; eastern basin dry 2014 (Aralkum Desert); Aralsk and Muynak stranded; North Aral partly restored after the Kokaral dike (2005) — [Wikipedia, Aral Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aral_Sea). The lost sound is a shoreline: waves at Muynak and Aralsk.
- **Owens Lake (Patsiata), California (rank C)**: diversion from 1913, dry by 1926; Joseph Grinnell, 1917: "Great numbers of water birds are in sight along the lake shore--avocets, phalaropes, ducks"; partial re-watering since a 2004 court order — [Wikipedia, Owens Lake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Owens_Lake). Approx. 36.43 N, 117.95 W **(est.)**. Paiute/Shoshone homeland.

#### Migrations that ended

**A6. Springbok treks (trekbokke) of the Karoo and Northern Cape (rank A)**
- Last great trek 1896, Prieska district; S.C. Cronwright-Schreiner and two farmers estimated half a million animals in view on one plain, "but the whole trek covered an area of one hundred and forty miles by fifteen miles"; "When one says they were in their millions, it is the literal truth" — quoted from Cronwright-Schreiner (*The Migratory Springbucks of South Africa*, 1925) in [Farming Portal / Karoo Space](https://www.farmingportal.co.za/index.php/agri-index/84-wild-life/7383-the-springbok-migrations-thundering-hooves-of-death-meat-like-a-fine-p-te). (Another article places his 1896 sighting "near Hopetown" — [Karoo Space](https://karoospace.co.za/springbok-across-the-horizon/).)
- Sound: "We were awakened one morning by a sound as of strong wind before a thunderstorm, followed by the trampling of thousands" of game — John Fraser, Beaufort West, 1849 (a three-day passage); "the rumbling on the horizon became louder and louder" — Gert van der Merwe, via Lawrence Green's *Karoo* — both in [Karoo Space, "Springbok Across the Horizon"](https://karoospace.co.za/springbok-across-the-horizon/). "the slightest sight or sound would send troops of 10,000 to 20,000 scampering off" — T.B. Davie, Prieska — same source.
- Other dated treks: 1888 (Prieska; Dr Gibbons' estimate "at least one hundred million buck"), 1892 (to the Atlantic coast; W.C. Scully: bodies "in one continuous pile along the shore for over thirty miles") — [Farming Portal](https://www.farmingportal.co.za/index.php/agri-index/84-wild-life/7383-the-springbok-migrations-thundering-hooves-of-death-meat-like-a-fine-p-te).
- Where: Prieska approx. 29.67 S, 22.75 E **(est.)**; Beaufort West approx. 32.35 S, 22.58 E **(est.)**.
- Cultural: springbok were central to |Xam life (skins, sinews, "ears as dancing rattles") — Chris Roche's UCT thesis as cited in [Karoo Space](https://karoospace.co.za/springbok-across-the-horizon/); the Bleek and Lloyd |Xam archive has an entry on "Springbok ewes and lamb's cries" — [Digital Bleek and Lloyd (via search)](https://digitalbleeklloyd.uct.ac.za/metadata/stories/Lucy_Lloyd__xam_notebooks/story_705/index.html). The |Xam themselves were destroyed as a people in the same decades; word this with care.

**B7. George River caribou herd, Québec–Labrador (rank B; SENSITIVE)**
- About 800,000 in the early 1990s; 5,500 in 2018, "a decline of over 99% since 1993" — [Government of Newfoundland and Labrador, 21 Sept 2018 (via search)](https://www.gov.nl.ca/releases/2018/flr/0921n03/).
- Sound: caribou "make a unique clicking sound, sort of like castanets, when they walk", from tendons snapping over sesamoid bones; "Like the timekeeping ticking of a thousand metronomes, the clicking of the caribou's ankle bones counts cadence for the marchers" — [Journey North (via search)](https://journeynorth.org/tm/caribou/Walking.html).
- Sensitivities: the caribou is central to Innu, Inuit and Cree identity and the hunting ban is contested — [CBC (via search)](https://www.cbc.ca/1.4833339). Consult; herd range is vast (place broadly, approx. 57 N, 65 W **(est.)**).

**B8. Kalahari wildebeest migration, Botswana (rank B)**
- The Kuke veterinary fence (completed 1958) cut the routes between the Central Kalahari and the Okavango, Lake Xau and the Makgadikgadi; wildebeest in central and southern Botswana "declined by 94 per cent between 1979 and 1994" — [Africa Geographic (via search)](https://africageographic.com/stories/should-namibia-and-botswana-bring-down-their-veterinary-fences/); [Okavango fences report (via search)](https://the-eis.com/elibrary/sites/default/files/downloads/literature/The%20effects%20of%20veterinary%20fences%20on%20wildlife%20populations%20in%20Okavango%20Delta_Botswana.pdf). #### Sand

**B9. Japan's singing-sand beaches (naki-suna) (rank B)**
- "within the last 50 years most of these beaches have lost their singing properties", through construction or sea-water pollution; contaminated sand (soil, dust, oil, detergent) stops singing — [Doshisha / bishogai.com musical sand pages](https://bishogai.com/msand/sand/e_japan.html). Counts conflict: "90 such beaches used to exist in Japan. Today, however, just 20 singing sand beaches remain" — [Crossroad Fukuoka, Anego no Hama (via search)](https://www.crossroadfukuoka.jp/en/spot/10252); versus "around a dozen" reduced to 3 — [tabi-mag (via search)](https://tabi-mag.jp/ky0358/).
- Dated cases: Anego no Hama, Fukuoka, "stopped 'singing' in the early 1960's" and sang again from about 1994 after citizen clean-ups — [Crossroad Fukuoka](https://www.crossroadfukuoka.jp/en/spot/10252). Kotogahama, Shimane, almost stopped singing between 1968 and 1984 after offshore breakwaters; recovered after they were replaced with submerged reefs in 1989–1999 — [Shimane Prefecture (via search)](https://www.pref.shimane.lg.jp/infra/nature/shizen/shimane/minnademamoru/narisuna.html).
- Mechanism: clean quartz grains rubbing — [Crossroad Fukuoka](https://www.crossroadfukuoka.jp/en/spot/10252). No frequency data retrieved.

#### Whole-soundscape change

- **Spring dawn chorus (rank B)**: reconstructing soundscapes at more than 200,000 sites in North America and Europe from survey data and species recordings shows "a pervasive loss of acoustic diversity and intensity of soundscapes across both continents over the past 25 years" — [Morrison et al. 2021, Nature Communications, via University of East Anglia](https://ueaeprints.uea.ac.uk/id/eprint/81972). This is itself a synthesis-from-data method close to what "elsewhere" does.
- **Quiet on land (rank B)**: "anthropogenic noise doubled background sound levels in 63% of U.S. protected area units and caused a 10-fold or greater increase in 21%"; main sources roads, aircraft, development, extraction — [Buxton et al. 2017, Science](https://angelonilab.colostate.edu/Site/Publications_files/Buxton_et_al_2017.pdf); [ScienceDaily (via search)](https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2017/05/170504161513.htm).
- **Quiet in the sea (rank B)**: west of San Nicolas Island, California, ambient noise at 30–50 Hz was 10–12 dB higher in 2003–2004 than in 1964–1966, about 2.5–3 dB per decade, attributed to shipping (vessel numbers about doubled, tonnage quadrupled, 1965–2003) — [McDonald, Hildebrand & Wiggins 2006, JASA 120:711–718](https://www.cetus.ucsd.edu/docs/publications/McDonaldJASA2006.pdf).

### Inferences
- The three Columbia/Paraná falls are the strongest "non-animal" additions and fit the work's premise exactly (a place whose defining sound was removed on a known day). For synthesis: Guaíra — broadband roar from 13,000–50,000 m³/s through a 60 m throat, audible to 30 km, so a distance-attenuated low-frequency rumble model is justified; Celilo — about 6 m drop but huge discharge, "like a freight train", audible about 10 miles, seasonally drowned in the May–June freshet (so quieter at peak flow); Kettle Falls — 15 m of cascades over quartzite, "heard for miles". None has a published spectrum; the spectra are inferred from flow and drop.
- Because Celilo physically survives under Lake Celilo and tribes say they "can still hear its roar", restoring its sound is not neutral: it could be received as honouring or as appropriation. The decision belongs to the tribes. The same holds for Kettle Falls, Keshena Falls, the Onondaga cicadas, and the omiwatari.
- Dead glaciers are, acoustically, the loss of meltwater trickle, firn-settling cracks and occasional icequakes at small cirque glaciers; the published numbers (Pettit; Meares calving) are from large tidewater glaciers and should not be transposed without saying so. An honest rendering for Okjökull or Pizol is quiet: meltwater, wind on ice, rare cracks.
- Omiwatari is unusual in having a 580-year presence/absence record; the piece could literally play the record (ridge sound in years it formed, silence in years it did not), using the dispersion "pew" and thermal booms described for lake ice.
- The springbok trek's sound is well enough described to synthesize: first a wind-before-thunderstorm rush, then a rumble growing over minutes, then trampling at close range; passages lasted up to three days.
- Morrison et al. 2021 gives a quantitative basis for a "thinned dawn chorus" mode anywhere in Europe or North America without naming a single species.
- Singing sand is a rare case where a non-animal sound was lost and regained by cleaning; two dated Japanese beaches make it mappable.

### Gaps
- No published acoustic measurements were found for any drowned waterfall, for small mountain glaciers, for moulins, or for singing sand (frequency); all such detail would be inferred.
- Guarani/Avá-Guarani relationship to Sete Quedas, Cucapá relationship to the Colorado delta, Potawatomi (Kankakee), Miccosukee/Seminole (Everglades), Uru-Murato (Poopó), Nubian (Second and Fourth Cataracts) and Tonga (Kariba) perspectives were not researched beyond the single lines cited; each needs proper sourcing before implementation.
- Sound descriptions were not found for: Laufenburg rapids, Nile cataracts, Kariba Gorge, Three Gorges shoals, Ripon Falls (Speke's 1862 account not retrieved), Iron Gates, Kalahari wildebeest, Kankakee marsh.
- Cronwright-Schreiner's own text was not read; his sound descriptions, if any, are unverified. Whether the 1896 trek was at Prieska or near Hopetown differs between the two articles.
- Not researched for lack of quota: Lake Chad, Lake Urmia and Lop Nur, Glen Canyon proper (beyond Cataract Canyon), Tryweryn, permafrost thaw sounds, saiga and Mongolian gazelle migrations, singing desert dunes that fell silent, North American lake ice-out records (e.g. Mendota), first-aircraft/first-road dates for the "pre-industrial quiet", insect-biomass decline as a soundscape. I found no reliable sources on these.
- Anderson Glacier's end date conflicts (2009 vs 2015); Pizol, Clark, Ayoloco, Sarenne and Martial Sur coordinates are my estimates; the Global Glacier Casualty List site could not be fetched.
- The Sable Island walrus date ("late 1800s") and the Kankakee "one-fifth of migratory birds" claim are from single sources and look doubtful.
